## Primary versus Secondary Sources

A secondary chart can be useful for speed, but the primary source should define:

- series construction;
- release schedule;
- historical revisions;
- exclusions;
- seasonal adjustment;
- methodological breaks.

Never build a rule from a chart whose definition is unknown.

## Timeliness versus Reliability

Fast data may be noisy. Reliable data may lag. Build a layered stack:

- market prices: immediate, endogenous, potentially distorted;
- high-frequency indicators: timely, model-dependent;
- surveys: leading, sentiment-sensitive;
- official monthly data: authoritative, revised;
- national accounts: comprehensive, lagged and revised.

## Triangulation

Use sources with different error structures. For growth:

- spending/income;
- labor;
- production;
- surveys;
- credit;
- market pricing.

Agreement increases confidence. Disagreement should be explained, not averaged blindly.
