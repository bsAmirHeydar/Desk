## Scope

Bitcoin can trade as:

- a high-beta liquidity asset;
- a speculative adoption asset;
- a scarce digital asset;
- an institutional-flow vehicle;
- a leveraged collateral ecosystem;
- a policy/regulatory asset.

Its dominant identity changes by regime.

## Driver Stack

- global and dollar liquidity;
- real yields and policy expectations;
- risk appetite and volatility;
- ETF/fund flows;
- leverage and funding rates;
- stablecoin and crypto-market liquidity;
- regulation and legal access;
- network/adoption developments;
- idiosyncratic supply and custody events.

## Macro Regimes

### Liquidity easing

Lower real yields, weaker dollar, stable credit, and rising risk appetite can support Bitcoin.

### Stress liquidation

Bitcoin can sell off with equities as leverage is reduced. “Digital gold” behavior is not reliable intraday.

### Adoption/flow regime

ETF, corporate, sovereign, or platform flows can dominate macro temporarily.

### Regulatory shock

Legal and market-access changes can overwhelm rates.

## Confirmation

- Nasdaq and high-beta equities;
- real yields;
- dollar;
- credit and volatility;
- crypto funding rates and basis;
- ETF flows;
- stablecoin/liquidity measures;
- options skew.

## Day-Trading Rule

Do not use a structural scarcity thesis to justify an intraday long during deleveraging. The daily permission must reflect the current liquidity, leverage, and catalyst state.

## Swing Rule

For short swings, separate:

- macro liquidity thesis;
- flow thesis;
- regulatory thesis;
- implementation positioning thesis.

Each needs independent invalidation.

## Common Traps

- assuming fixed supply determines short-term price;
- treating every rate cut as bullish;
- ignoring leverage and liquidation;
- calling it a safe haven from one episode;
- using on-chain metrics without methodology;
- ignoring weekend liquidity and gap risk;
- mixing structural adoption with day-trading timing.
