## What Nasdaq Represents

Nasdaq 100 is a concentrated portfolio of large non-financial companies with substantial technology, communication-services, and consumer exposure. It is not a pure technology index and not a direct proxy for GDP.

Its macro sensitivity comes from:

- long-duration expected cash flows;
- high margins and secular growth assumptions;
- global revenues;
- mega-cap concentration;
- capital-expenditure cycles;
- real-yield sensitivity;
- options and systematic flows.

## Fundamental Driver Stack

### 1. Earnings and Revision Cycle

Track:

- revenue growth;
- cloud, advertising, semiconductor, software, and consumer demand;
- gross and operating margins;
- AI/data-center capex;
- monetization;
- buybacks;
- forward guidance;
- revision breadth.

### 2. Real Discount Rate

Rising real yields can compress valuation multiples. The effect is strongest when:

- valuations are extended;
- earnings revisions are not rising;
- the move is policy/real-rate driven;
- credit or volatility also deteriorates.

### 3. Policy and Liquidity

Easier policy expectations, stable funding, and lower volatility can support risk capacity. Cuts caused by crisis are not equivalent to benign disinflation.

### 4. Concentration

Index movement can be dominated by a few firms. Always compare:

- Nasdaq 100 vs equal-weight technology;
- semiconductors vs software;
- mega caps vs small caps;
- market breadth;
- earnings-weighted leadership.

### 5. Dollar

A stronger dollar can reduce translated foreign revenues and tighten global conditions, but the daily relationship is conditional.

### 6. Options and Flow

Mega-cap options, index options, buybacks, systematic exposure, and expiry can shape intraday path.

## Intraday Confirmation

A **rate-relief long** is stronger when:

- real yields remain lower;
- dollar does not reverse sharply higher;
- credit remains stable;
- semiconductors and breadth confirm;
- volatility falls;
- the cash open accepts the move.

A **hawkish short** is stronger when:

- front-end and real yields remain higher;
- dollar strengthens;
- long-duration sectors underperform;
- volatility rises;
- rebound attempts fail after the first repricing.
