## What the Dollar Represents

The US dollar is:

- the unit of account for global trade and finance;
- a major reserve currency;
- a funding currency;
- a relative-rate asset;
- a safe and liquid asset in stress;
- a balance-sheet variable for dollar borrowers.

A dollar index is a basket with fixed weights. It is not identical to “the dollar against everything.” DXY is heavily influenced by the euro.

## Driver Stack

1. US expected policy path relative to peers.
2. US growth relative to peers.
3. Global risk and demand for dollar funding.
4. Treasury yields and real returns.
5. Fiscal and institutional credibility.
6. Commodity and trade invoicing.
7. Reserve and portfolio flows.
8. Positioning and hedging.

## Strong-Dollar Regimes

### US exceptionalism

US growth and earnings outperform; rates remain higher.

### Hawkish divergence

Fed path reprices higher relative to peers.

### Global stress

Funding and safe-haven demand dominate.

### Commodity deflation/global slowdown

Dollar strengthens as global cyclicals and exporters weaken.

## Weak-Dollar Regimes

### Dovish relative repricing

Fed path falls faster than peers.

### Global reflation/risk-on

Capital moves toward non-US and high-beta assets.

### Fiscal/credibility concern

Possible but not automatic; yields, term premium, and risk channels can conflict.

### Reserve/portfolio diversification

Usually structural and slow, not a daily trigger.

## Cross-Asset Effects

A stronger dollar tends to:

- tighten global financial conditions;
- pressure dollar-priced commodities;
- reduce translated foreign earnings;
- challenge EM assets;
- sometimes coexist with higher US yields.

But causality can run both ways.

## Day-Trading Use

Ask:

- Is the move broad or euro-specific?
- Is it rates, growth, or funding led?
- Are front-end differentials confirming?
- Are gold and commodities reacting?
- Is risk-off driving the move?
- Does USD strength persist after the US cash open?

## Common Traps

- using DXY as the entire FX market;
- assuming high deficits automatically weaken USD;
- assuming falling yields weaken USD in stress;
- ignoring relative foreign weakness;
- treating de-dollarization headlines as intraday signals;
- ignoring hedging and funding.
