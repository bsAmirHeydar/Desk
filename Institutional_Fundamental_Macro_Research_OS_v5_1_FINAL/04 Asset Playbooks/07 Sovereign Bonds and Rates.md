## Price and Yield

Bond prices and yields move inversely. But the macro task is to identify why yields change.

## Driver Stack

- expected policy path;
- expected inflation;
- expected real growth;
- neutral-rate estimates;
- term premium;
- fiscal supply;
- safe-haven demand;
- foreign and pension demand;
- quantitative easing/tightening;
- dealer capacity and market liquidity.

## Curve Segments

### Front End

Most sensitive to policy meetings and near-term data.

### Belly

Sensitive to medium-term policy path, growth, and inflation.

### Long End

More exposed to term premium, fiscal supply, long-run inflation and growth, liability demand, and credibility.

## Curve Trades

- steepener;
- flattener;
- butterfly;
- real vs nominal;
- cross-country spread.

Each needs a specific driver.

### Bull steepener

Yields fall, front end falls more. Can reflect easing or recession.

### Bear steepener

Yields rise, long end rises more. Can reflect growth, inflation, fiscal supply, or term premium.

### Bull flattener

Long end falls more. Can reflect disinflation/safe-haven demand.

### Bear flattener

Front end rises more. Often hawkish policy repricing.

## Data Reaction

Inflation and labor data usually hit the front end first. Treasury supply or fiscal credibility can hit the long end. Growth data can affect both, with the sign depending on policy and risk.

## Auction Interpretation

Consider:

- pre-auction concession;
- tail/stop-through;
- bid-to-cover versus history;
- indirect/direct/dealer allocation;
- issue maturity;
- market volatility;
- post-auction follow-through.

One auction does not define the regime.

## Day-Trading Use

For equity and gold trading, rates are often a causal confirmation market. For direct rates trading, define the curve node and catalyst before entry.

## Swing Use

A swing rates thesis needs:

- expected policy-path gap;
- inflation/growth trajectory;
- term-premium view;
- supply calendar;
- cross-country context;
- positioning;
- carry and roll;
- event risk.

## Common Traps

- “Recession = buy every bond maturity.”
- “Inflation down = long end down.”
- ignoring term premium;
- ignoring supply;
- treating curve inversion as precise timing;
- using nominal yields without TIPS;
- assuming safe-haven demand always dominates.
