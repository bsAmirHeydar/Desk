# Hypothesis Evidence Ledger, Dominance and Falsifiability

Every load-bearing hypothesis must show a two-sided ledger. Evidence “for” the hypothesis and evidence “against” it are stored separately; missing evidence is not silently treated as neutral.

Dominance is earned by explanatory control, not by the number of supporting observations. Same-root descendants count once for independence even if they appear in several assets or sciences. A rival with fewer observations can remain more credible if those observations are direct, independent and causally prior.

The strongest challenger must remain visible in the final report. A hypothesis without a clear falsification condition is not eligible to become the sole dominant explanation. If the supposed invalidation trigger cannot be observed within the decision horizon, mark the risk as partly unmonitorable and transfer it to the pre-mortem.
