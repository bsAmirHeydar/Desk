# Competing Causal Hypotheses and Model Tournament

A decision-material run should normally carry multiple causal explanations. The preferred active set is two to six hypotheses. One hypothesis is allowed only when the system records what rival families were searched and why they are not credible at the cutoff.

A hypothesis is not a narrative headline. It is a falsifiable mechanism connecting facts to expectations, policy or cash-flow revisions, transmission channels, expected leaders and the target asset over a named horizon.

For every hypothesis store supporting evidence, contradictory evidence, missing evidence, root IDs, regime dependencies, expected leaders, expected market signatures, confirmation triggers and invalidation triggers.

The tournament is **non-additive**. Do not assign arbitrary points and select the highest score. Use qualitative dominance only when the hypothesis explains the material facts, its expected leaders are not materially broken, rival explanations were tested, and no decision-critical contradiction remains on the load-bearing path.

When two hypotheses explain different parts of the tape or operate on different horizons, retain co-dominance or `CONTESTED`. A forced single winner is worse than an explicit conflict.
