# Glossary

> [!abstract] Research mandate
> Construct a point-in-time, source-controlled, model-aware and falsifiable understanding of **Glossary**. Canonical doctrine is linked; this note contains the topic-specific research object.

## Definition and economic object

**Glossary** is a research object inside source-controlled data, definitions, metadata, transformations, lineage, schemas, and reproducible research interfaces. The analyst must isolate the measurable state, the expectation already embedded in prices, the mechanism connecting them, and the horizon on which that inference can survive.

For **Glossary**, the relevant institutional domain is **data_reference**: source-controlled data, definitions, metadata, transformations, lineage, schemas, and reproducible research interfaces. Classify every input as observation, derived measurement, model estimate, market-implied estimate, forecast, causal claim, scenario assumption, judgment, or decision rule.

## Research questions

1. What exact state or mechanism does **Glossary** represent, in what unit, population, instrument, and convention?
2. Which **Glossary** observations existed at the decision cutoff, which are estimates, and which are revised?
3. What distribution about **Glossary** is embedded in consensus, curves, options, valuation, positioning, or physical basis?
4. Which market or variable must lead if the proposed **Glossary** mechanism is active?
5. What rival model can create the same target move while **Glossary** is unchanged?
6. How do regime, horizon, positioning, liquidity, carry, and implementation alter the payoff?
7. Which predeclared evidence rejects, caps, or expires the **Glossary** decision?

## Causal transmission

1. **Glossary channel 1:** test `publisher → raw immutable observation`.
2. **Glossary channel 2:** test `raw → curated point-in-time feature`.
3. **Glossary channel 3:** test `feature → model and decision object`.
4. **Glossary channel 4:** test `decision → audit and reproducibility record`.

**Glossary asset translation:** Data: no field enters a decision without units, vintage, lineage, and quality status. Predeclare the leader. If the target moves without the leader or with contradictory independent evidence, reduce the **Glossary** posterior or activate a rival explanation.

## Day-trading decision translation

- Identify the new **Glossary** information since the prior close and its source timestamp.
- Reconstruct the priced **Glossary** baseline before reading the target move.
- Name the liquid leader closest to the **Glossary** mechanism and one independent confirmation.
- Compare observed transmission with the **Glossary** event/quiet-day historical distribution.
- Assign a permission and a confidence cap; record the **Glossary** cancellation condition.
- Pass only the permission, leader, invalidation, expiry, and size ceiling to technical execution.

For **Glossary**, fundamentals restrict the allowed trade set; they do not supply the candle trigger or authorize widening a structural stop.

## Two-to-ten-day swing translation

- Define the still-open **Glossary** pricing gap rather than the general narrative.
- Estimate the **Glossary** impulse half-life and its uncertainty by regime.
- Map catalysts capable of confirming, reversing, or exhausting the **Glossary** campaign.
- Compare outright and relative expressions for carry, convexity, gap, liquidity, and factor purity.
- Specify terminal realization, time expiry, and evidence-based invalidation for **Glossary**.

A valid **Glossary** thesis with no residual pricing gap, adverse carry beyond expected payoff, or an imminent dominating catalyst is not a deployable swing.

## Falsification and known failure modes

- **Failure test 1 for Glossary:** using download time as release time.
- **Failure test 2 for Glossary:** overwriting vintages.
- **Failure test 3 for Glossary:** unclear units or seasonal adjustment.
- **Failure test 4 for Glossary:** broken source-to-claim lineage.
- **Failure test 5 for Glossary:** silent vendor methodology changes.

Score **Glossary** separately for state estimation, expectation measurement, causal transmission, expression, timing, sizing, execution, and residual noise. Neither a winning outcome nor a losing outcome alone establishes research quality.

## Preserved subject-specific foundation

This material survived consolidation because it contains subject-specific instruction for **Glossary**, not the former repeated institutional wrapper.

## A

**Acceleration** — Change in the rate of change. Growth can remain positive while decelerating.

**Actual** — Reported value at release time, distinguished from consensus, prior, and revised prior.

**Alpha** — Return unexplained by chosen benchmark/risk factors, after realistic costs and model risk.

**Annualization** — Transforming a subannual change into an annual rate under a repetition assumption.

**Asset expression** — The instrument selected to express a causal view.

## B

**Backwardation** — Commodity futures curve with near contracts above later contracts, often associated with prompt tightness, convenience yield, or risk premium.

**Balance-sheet channel** — Transmission through borrower/lender net worth, collateral, leverage, and lending capacity.

**Base effect** — Change in a year-over-year rate caused by the comparison period entering or leaving the calculation.

**Basis** — Price difference between related instruments, such as futures and spot/CFD.

**Bayesian update** — Revising scenario probability after new evidence.

**Bear steepener** — Long yields rise faster than short yields.

**Bear flattener** — Short yields rise faster than long yields.

**Breadth** — Degree to which index constituents participate in a move.

**Breakeven inflation** — Nominal Treasury yield minus inflation-protected yield; a market compensation measure that includes expectations and risk/liquidity premia.

## C

**Carry** — Expected return from holding an instrument if market conditions remain unchanged, including yield/roll effects.

**Cash-flow channel** — Effect of growth, margins, demand, and costs on expected asset cash flows.

**Catalyst** — Information or flow event capable of changing pricing during the selected horizon.

**Central-bank reaction function** — Conditional relationship between economic/financial conditions and likely policy response.

**CISD-style trigger** — The execution trigger in Amir's system after trend continuation and structural pullback; it remains separate from macro permission.

**Commitments of Traders (COT)** — CFTC reports classifying futures/options positions by trader categories; useful for weekly positioning context.

**Confidence** — Degree of belief in a claim/model, distinct from direction and payoff asymmetry.

**Consensus** — Aggregated forecast estimate; not necessarily identical to the market-implied hurdle.

**Contango** — Commodity futures curve with later contracts above near contracts.

**Convexity** — Nonlinear sensitivity of price or hedging demand to changes in an underlying variable.

**Core inflation** — Inflation measure excluding selected volatile components, commonly food and energy; not a universal measure of persistence.

**Credit spread** — Yield premium over a reference government/risk-free curve; reflects default, liquidity, and risk compensation.

**Cross-asset confirmation** — Evidence from markets other than the traded asset that supports the causal interpretation.

## D

**Data vintage** — The version of a data observation available at a particular historical timestamp.

**Dealer gamma** — Sensitivity of dealer delta exposure to underlying price; can influence hedging flows.

**Diffusion** — Breadth of change across components, sectors, or respondents.

**Directional permission** — Constraint produced by this OS: `LONG_ONLY`, `SHORT_ONLY`, `TWO_WAY_REDUCED`, or `NO_TRADE`.

**Discount rate** — Rate used to value future cash flows; includes risk-free rates and risk premia.

**Dovish/hawkish** — Relative policy descriptors; meaningful only compared with prior guidance and market pricing.

**Duration** — Sensitivity to discount-rate changes. Long-duration equities derive more value from distant expected cash flows.

## E

**Earnings revision** — Change in analysts' or market participants' expected company/index earnings.

**Economic surprise** — Difference between reported data and a pre-release expectation, adjusted for composition/revisions where relevant.

**Event study** — Analysis of market behavior around timestamped information events.

**Expectation gap** — Difference between likely future reality and what current prices discount.

**Expiry** — Time after which a thesis no longer grants permission, even if not causally invalidated.

## F

**Financial conditions** — Composite influence of rates, credit, equity prices, FX, volatility, and lending standards on financing/economic behavior.

**Fiscal impulse** — Change in fiscal support or drag, rather than the deficit level alone.

**Flow** — Buying/selling generated by allocation, hedging, issuance, redemption, rebalancing, or mechanical constraints.

**Forward guidance** — Central-bank communication about likely future policy, usually conditional.

**Funding stress** — Difficulty or increased cost in obtaining short-term financing/collateral.

## G

**Gamma** — Option sensitivity describing how delta changes as the underlying changes.

**GDPNow/nowcast** — Model-based estimate of current-period activity using available data; not an official forecast.

**Growth impulse** — Direction and acceleration of economic activity relative to trend/expectation.

## H

**Hard data** — Direct measured activity such as spending, production, or employment, contrasted with surveys.

**Headline inflation** — Broad inflation including food and energy.

**Horizon mismatch** — Using evidence whose relevance window does not match the trade holding period.

## I

**Implied volatility** — Option-price-derived volatility compensation, not a pure forecast.

**Information shock** — Asset response to what a policy action/communication reveals about the economy, separate from the mechanical policy shock.

**Inflation breadth** — Distribution of price increases across categories.

**Inflation compensation** — Market compensation for expected inflation plus inflation risk and liquidity premia.

**Invalidation** — Evidence that makes a causal thesis false.

## L

**Lagging indicator** — Variable that turns after the broader cycle.

**Leading indicator** — Variable that tends to change before aggregate activity, with imperfect reliability.

**Liquidity** — Ability to transact without large price impact; also used more broadly for availability of funding/cash, requiring precise definition.

**Liquidity premium** — Compensation for holding a less liquid asset.

## M

**Market hurdle** — Outcome required to surprise the marginal price setter; can differ from published consensus.

**Measured move** — Technical objective based on structural symmetry in Amir's execution model.

**Monetary transmission** — Path from policy actions/expectations through markets, credit, demand, inflation, and activity.

**Momentum** — Direction of recent change; in macro, distinguish level from rate of change.

## N

**Natural rate / neutral rate** — Real policy-rate concept consistent with equilibrium under assumptions; uncertain and time-varying.

**Nominal yield** — Yield not adjusted for inflation compensation.

**Nowcast** — Estimate of current conditions before complete official data.

## O

**Output gap** — Difference between actual and potential economic output; model-dependent and revised.

**Overnight risk** — Risk from information arriving when the position cannot be exited at the intended price.

## P

**Path dependency** — Later market response depends on the sequence of prior information and repricing.

**Policy path** — Expected sequence of future policy settings, not only the next decision.

**Positioning** — Existing market exposure that can amplify, delay, or reverse response.

**Pricing gap** — Actionable difference between current price-implied expectations and a supported alternative.

**Primary source** — Original official producer or venue of information.

## Q

**Quantitative easing/tightening** — Central-bank balance-sheet policies affecting duration, reserves, liquidity, and signaling.

## R

**Rate of change** — Change over a selected interval; may turn before the level.

**Reaction function** — See central-bank reaction function.

**Real yield** — Nominal yield adjusted by inflation measure/compensation; exact definition matters.

**Reflexivity** — Feedback in which market prices affect economic behavior and then validate or undermine the original move.

**Regime** — Persistent configuration of macro state, policy, liquidity, risk premium, and market behavior.

**Repricing** — Change in market-implied expectations or compensation.

**Revision bias** — Artificial research advantage from using later revised data instead of contemporaneous vintages.

**Risk premium** — Expected compensation for bearing risk beyond risk-free value.

## S

**Scenario** — Conditional causal path with trigger, probability, transmission, confirmation, invalidation, and horizon.

**Seasonal adjustment** — Statistical removal of recurring seasonal patterns.

**Soft landing** — Inflation reduction without severe growth/credit damage; a scenario, not a directly observed fact.

**State dependence** — Market response changes with economic/financial regime.

**Stop-through/tail** — Treasury auction stop yield better/worse than when-issued yield.

**Structural invalidation** — Technical price level where the execution thesis fails.

**Structural pullback** — Required retracement/location stage in Amir's continuation model.

**Surprise** — Difference between new information and expectation, not simply high or low absolute data.

## T

**Tail risk** — Low-probability, high-impact outcome.

**Term premium** — Compensation for interest-rate/duration uncertainty in long-term yields; model-estimated.

**Terms of trade** — Relative export prices versus import prices; important for commodity-linked currencies.

**Thesis** — Versioned causal claim with evidence, confirmation, invalidation, and expiry.

**Transmission failure** — Expected causal channel does not appear in relevant markets.

## V

**Veto** — Condition that forbids execution without necessarily falsifying the underlying thesis.

**Volatility risk premium** — Difference between implied and expected/realized volatility compensation.

## W

**Whisper expectation** — Informal expectation beyond published consensus; must be treated as uncertain.

**Walk-forward validation** — Sequential out-of-sample testing that simulates model updating through time.

## Y

**Yield curve** — Interest rates across maturities; shape reflects expected policy, growth, inflation, term premium, and supply/demand.

**Yield-curve control** — Policy targeting yields at selected maturities.
