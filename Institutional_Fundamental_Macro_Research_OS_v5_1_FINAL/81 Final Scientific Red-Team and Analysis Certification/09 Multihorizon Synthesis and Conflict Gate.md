# Multihorizon Synthesis and Conflict Gate

## Required horizon stack

- structural: years and institutional constraints;
- cyclical: quarters to years;
- tactical: weeks to months;
- multi-day: roughly two to ten trading days;
- daily/session: the current day or session;
- event: minutes to several sessions.

Microstructure evidence may inform transmission, liquidity and implementation constraints but cannot replace fundamental state analysis.

## Horizon inheritance

Longer horizons provide priors, not commands. Shorter horizons can temporarily oppose the structural view when pricing, positioning, policy timing or liquidity dominate. A certified report must state which horizon controls which conclusion.

## Conflict matrix

| Higher-horizon state | Lower-horizon impulse | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| aligned | aligned | strongest coherence, still subject to valuation |
| aligned | opposed | temporary counterforce or emerging transition |
| uncertain | strong impulse | narrow conclusion with short expiry |
| opposed | opposed | structural thesis may be changing |

## Double-counting control

Avoid counting one shock through multiple correlated indicators. A policy repricing may appear simultaneously in front-end yields, the dollar, equity duration and implied volatility. These are transmission evidence, not four independent causal observations.

## Half-life and expiry

Every conclusion needs an estimated information half-life based on mechanism:

- pure announcement surprise may decay quickly;
- policy-path repricing can persist through multiple releases;
- earnings revisions may persist for quarters;
- physical commodity shortages persist until supply, demand or logistics adjust;
- structural demographic or institutional changes evolve slowly.

## Veto conditions

- one conclusion is stated for all horizons;
- short-term price action is used to rewrite structural state without evidence;
- long-run valuation is used to justify a current-state conclusion;
- expiry and update triggers are absent.
