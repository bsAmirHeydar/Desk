## Day-trading decision translation

- Identify the new **Daily Pre-Market Process** information since the prior close and its source timestamp.
- Reconstruct the priced **Daily Pre-Market Process** baseline before reading the target move.
- Name the liquid leader closest to the **Daily Pre-Market Process** mechanism and one independent confirmation.
- Compare observed transmission with the **Daily Pre-Market Process** event/quiet-day historical distribution.
- Assign a permission and a confidence cap; record the **Daily Pre-Market Process** cancellation condition.
- Pass only the permission, leader, invalidation, expiry, and size ceiling to technical execution.

For **Daily Pre-Market Process**, fundamentals restrict the allowed trade set; they do not supply the candle trigger or authorize widening a structural stop.

## Preserved subject-specific foundation

This material survived consolidation because it contains subject-specific instruction for **Daily Pre-Market Process**, not the former repeated institutional wrapper.

> [!rule]
> The daily process must end with a **directional permission and a set of conditions**, not a compulsory trade.

Target output: a concise decision card that can be read during the session without reopening the entire research stack.

## Stage 1 — Overnight Change Detection

Compare the current morning with the prior close:

- sovereign yields: level, curve, real-rate proxy;
- dollar and key FX crosses;
- equity futures and regional equity leadership;
- credit proxies;
- volatility;
- oil, gold, copper, and other relevant commodities;
- material policy/geopolitical developments;
- earnings or guidance from index-heavy firms;
- changes in the day's catalyst schedule.

Classify each move:

- new fundamental information;
- continuation of prior repricing;
- technical/flow-related move;
- unexplained and requiring caution.

## Stage 2 — Re-anchor to the Weekly Map

Read 02 Weekly Macro Map before interpreting overnight price action.

Ask:

1. Did overnight information change the weekly regime?
2. Did it merely move price within the existing scenario?
3. Has the market already fully repriced the expected effect?
4. Is today's session likely to confirm, reject, or ignore the overnight move?

## Stage 3 — Define the Day's Dominant Question

A high-quality session usually revolves around one or two questions:

- Will rates validate the equity move?
- Is inflation pressure being repriced?
- Is the dollar move relative-rate driven or risk-off driven?
- Is oil responding to physical balance or geopolitical premium?
- Is gold trading real yields, dollar weakness, or tail-risk demand?
- Is the index move broad or concentrated?
- Is the market waiting for a later catalyst?

Write one sentence:

> **Today matters because the market must decide whether ________.**

## Stage 4 — Construct the Intraday Driver Stack

| Layer | Question |
|---|---|
| Structural | What multiweek force remains active? |
| Cyclical | Which growth/inflation/policy regime dominates? |
| Tactical | What is repricing over several sessions? |
| Intraday | What information or flow can dominate today? |

The intraday layer can temporarily overpower the tactical layer, but it should not automatically rewrite it.

## Stage 5 — Expectations and Reaction Function

Before a release or speech, record:

- consensus;
- distribution/range if available;
- relevant whisper or market-implied hurdle;
- most policy-sensitive subcomponents;
- current rate-path pricing;
- likely “good news/bad news” regime;
- what outcome is already priced;
- what would be genuinely surprising.

## Stage 6 — Session Scenarios

Create no more than four:

1. **Continuation:** overnight move confirms.
2. **Reversal:** overnight move fails after liquidity improves.
3. **Compression:** market waits for catalyst.
4. **Shock:** unscheduled information breaks the map.

For each, specify cross-asset confirmation and the technical behavior required.

## Stage 7 — Set Trade Permission

```yaml
permission: LONG_ONLY | SHORT_ONLY | TWO_WAY_REDUCED | NO_TRADE
confidence: 0-100
valid_from: session/open/catalyst
valid_until: time/catalyst/close
dominant_driver:
required_confirmation:
veto:
```

### Permission logic

- `LONG_ONLY`: macro context and cross-asset behavior make shorts structurally inferior.
- `SHORT_ONLY`: macro context and cross-asset behavior make longs structurally inferior.
- `TWO_WAY_REDUCED`: context is mixed; both directions allowed at smaller risk and higher technical threshold.
- `NO_TRADE`: driver ambiguity, imminent catalyst, broken liquidity, or transmission conflict.

## Stage 8 — Define Vetoes

A veto overrides directional preference. Examples:

- major event inside the trade's expected holding window;
- yields and dollar strongly reject the equity thesis;
- price gaps beyond a reasonable reward-to-risk entry zone;
- volatility/liquidity disorder;
- thesis dependent on an unverified headline;
- cross-asset relationships become unstable;
- technical invalidation distance becomes economically unacceptable.

## Stage 9 — Technical Handoff

Fundamental work ends here. Then require:

1. trend/continuation structure;
2. structural pullback;
3. valid CISD-style trigger;
4. structural stop;
5. measured-move objective;
6. acceptable session timing and liquidity.

See 09 Integration with the Trend-Pullback-CISD Strategy.
