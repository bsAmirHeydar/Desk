## Day-trading decision translation

- Identify the new **Weekend Macro Process** information since the prior close and its source timestamp.
- Reconstruct the priced **Weekend Macro Process** baseline before reading the target move.
- Name the liquid leader closest to the **Weekend Macro Process** mechanism and one independent confirmation.
- Compare observed transmission with the **Weekend Macro Process** event/quiet-day historical distribution.
- Assign a permission and a confidence cap; record the **Weekend Macro Process** cancellation condition.
- Pass only the permission, leader, invalidation, expiry, and size ceiling to technical execution.

## Preserved subject-specific foundation

This material survived consolidation because it contains subject-specific instruction for **Weekend Macro Process**, not the former repeated institutional wrapper.

> [!purpose]
> Convert an unstructured week of headlines and data into a small number of **state variables, repricing themes, asset scenarios, and explicit invalidations** for the coming week.

The weekend process is not a forecast contest. Its function is to reduce Monday-morning improvisation. It should produce a conditional map that can survive being wrong.

## Required Outputs

By the end of the process, produce:

1. A one-paragraph **global macro state**.
2. A one-paragraph **US macro state**.
3. The market's currently priced **policy, growth, inflation, and risk-premium baseline**.
4. The three largest **pricing gaps or unresolved tensions**.
5. A base, upside-risk, and downside-risk scenario.
6. An asset-specific permission for NQ, ES, Gold, Oil, USD, and rates.
7. A list of catalysts that can change the map.
8. Explicit thesis invalidations and no-trade conditions.

## Step 1 — Separate New Information from Repeated Narrative

Create two columns:

| New information | Repeated narrative |
|---|---|
| Data revision, policy shift, material guidance change, new supply shock, financial accident | Recycled headline, unchanged talking point, lagging commentary |

A narrative becomes useful only when it changes one of:

- expected cash flows;
- expected inflation;
- expected policy path;
- expected liquidity;
- expected risk premium;
- expected physical supply/demand;
- positioning or forced-flow risk.

## Step 2 — Update the State Vector

Use a compact state vector:

\[
S_t = \{G, \pi, L, MP, FC, CR, FISC, USD, COM, GEO\}
\]

Where:

- \(G\): growth momentum;
- \(\pi\): inflation level, breadth, and impulse;
- \(L\): labor-market tightness and direction;
- \(MP\): monetary-policy stance and reaction function;
- \(FC\): financial conditions and liquidity;
- \(CR\): credit stress;
- \(FISC\): fiscal impulse and sovereign supply;
- \(USD\): dollar regime and global funding pressure;
- \(COM\): commodity and energy impulse;
- \(GEO\): geopolitical/policy shock state.

For each component record:

- **level**: tight/easy, strong/weak, high/low;
- **direction**: improving, deteriorating, stable;
- **acceleration**: accelerating or decelerating;
- **confidence**: low/medium/high;
- **horizon**: intraday, tactical, cyclical, structural.

## Step 3 — Reconstruct What the Market Prices

Do not infer expectations from commentary alone. Examine market prices:

- front-end rates and meeting probabilities;
- two-year and ten-year yields;
- real yields and inflation compensation;
- curve shape and term premium;
- dollar and key crosses;
- equity index level, breadth, leadership, and earnings sensitivity;
- credit spreads;
- implied volatility and skew;
- oil curve and major commodity relationships;
- gold relative to real yields and the dollar.

The question is not “What do economists expect?” It is:

> **What outcome would be needed to surprise the marginal price setter?**

## Step 4 — Identify Tensions

The best tactical opportunities often begin as unresolved tensions:

- equities rising while real yields rise;
- cyclicals weakening while headline indices remain strong;
- oil rallying while growth expectations fade;
- gold rising with the dollar;
- front-end easing priced while inflation impulse remains firm;
- volatility compressed despite an expanding catalyst set;
- policy rhetoric and market pricing diverging;
- strong index performance carried by very narrow breadth.

A tension is not automatically a trade. It is a **conditional instability** that tells you what confirmation matters.

## Step 5 — Build Three Scenarios

Use 02 Scenario Construction.

| Scenario | Probability range | Required evidence | Expected repricing | Asset map |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| Base | 45–65% | Current trend persists | Incremental | Normal risk |
| Alternative | 20–35% | One major variable changes | Material | Reduced or opposite bias |
| Tail | 5–20% | Nonlinear shock / policy error | Discontinuous | Capital protection |

Never use probabilities to create false precision. Ranges force humility.

## Step 6 — Translate to Asset Permissions

For each asset, answer:

1. What is its dominant driver this week?
2. What is the second-order driver?
3. What is already priced?
4. What would validate the long thesis?
5. What would validate the short thesis?
6. What would force `NO_TRADE`?
7. Which cross-asset market must confirm?
8. How long should the thesis remain valid?

Example format:

```yaml
asset: NQ
dominant_driver: real-yield path
secondary_driver: mega-cap earnings expectations
pricing_gap: market prices faster easing than current inflation impulse supports
permission: TWO_WAY_REDUCED
upgrade_to_long_only: real yields fall and breadth expands
upgrade_to_short_only: real yields rise, USD strengthens, and semis underperform
invalidation: cross-asset evidence reverses
expiry: Friday close
```

## Step 7 — Calendar and Path Dependency

Build a catalyst sequence, not merely a calendar:

```mermaid
flowchart LR
    A[Monday positioning] --> B[Tuesday activity data]
    B --> C[Wednesday inflation]
    C --> D[Thursday auction / earnings]
    D --> E[Friday labor / rebalance]
```

Earlier events alter the expectations entering later events. The same Friday number can have a different impact depending on Wednesday's repricing.

## Step 8 — Write the Weekly Decision Card

Keep the executable summary under one page:

> **Regime → priced baseline → tensions → catalysts → scenario map → permissions → invalidations.**

The full research can be long. The decision card must be compact.

## Failure Modes

- Writing a newspaper summary instead of a market map.
- Listing every data point without ranking causal importance.
- Treating consensus forecasts as identical to market pricing.
- Carrying last week's thesis after its transmission channel breaks.
- Assigning one static driver to an asset for the entire week.
- Converting weak conviction into aggressive position sizing.
- Looking for evidence that only confirms an existing chart bias.

## Completion Test

The process is complete only when you can answer:

- Why is the market where it is?
- Which expectation is most vulnerable?
- What evidence would change the dominant direction?
- What is the highest-quality no-trade condition?
- Which asset offers the cleanest expression?
