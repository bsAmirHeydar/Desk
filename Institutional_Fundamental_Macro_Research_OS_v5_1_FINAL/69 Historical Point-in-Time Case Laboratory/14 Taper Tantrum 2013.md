# 14 Taper Tantrum 2013

> [!case] Historical reconstruction
> This case is a training object, not an analogue by assertion. Rebuild it with contemporaneous vintages and the event clock before using it to reason about a new episode.

## Mechanism hypothesis

The central mechanism was **change in expected asset-purchase path, term premium, convexity, global carry, and EM external vulnerability**.

## Pre-event information set

Reconstruct the facts, estimates, forecasts, policy commitments, balance sheets, market prices, option distributions, and positioning that existed before the decisive transition. Do not use later classifications or final-vintage data.

## Event clock

Create a timestamped sequence containing:

1. official releases, legal actions, policy communications, and operational events;
2. market levels immediately before each event;
3. changes in the causal leaders;
4. liquidity, margin, and positioning feedback;
5. policy or private-sector response;
6. state changes that were knowable only later.

## Causal leaders and confirmations

Track **real/nominal yields, term premium, MBS, dollar, EM FX/rates, and capital flows**. For each observation, state whether it measured the mechanism, priced expectations, reflected forced flow, or merely moved with the episode.

## Scenario tree from the historical cutoff

Build at least three branches: stabilization, controlled deterioration, and nonlinear crisis/overshoot. Assign probabilities using only the contemporaneous information set. Identify policy capacity, balance-sheet buffers, substitution, and reflexive feedback.

## Multi-horizon study

| Horizon | Required reconstruction |
|---|---|
| Event/intraday | leader sequence, spreads/depth, gaps, options, and immediate interpretation |
| 1–10 days | margin, funding, policy response, positioning unwind, and incomplete repricing |
| 1–6 months | credit, demand, earnings, inflation, and institutional adaptation |
| Structural | regulation, market structure, policy doctrine, and capital allocation consequences |

## Counterfactual decisions

Compare no trade, directional trade, relative-value expression, option-defined risk, delayed confirmation, and an early-exit rule. Calculate costs and gap risk. A historical winner is not proof that the ex-ante decision was sound.

## Analogue restrictions

Use this case only when the current episode matches the mechanism, balance-sheet structure, policy capacity, market plumbing, and information distribution. Surface similarity is insufficient.
