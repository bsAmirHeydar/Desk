# State Decay and No-Change Reassessment

## Principle

Time passage can change usability without changing economic direction. At every mandatory checkpoint, reassess state decay even when no new headline appears.

## Decay dimensions

- catalyst freshness generally declines unless reinforced;
- information absorption generally rises as independent markets and participants process the news;
- repricing completion may rise, stall or reverse;
- flow exhaustion may rise as hedging or liquidation runs its course;
- narrative saturation may rise with broad recognition;
- remaining fundamental pressure may fall, remain stable or reopen after reinforcing evidence;
- reversal risk may rise if confirmation weakens or the next catalyst approaches;
- move quality can transition from fundamental-led to flow-led;
- persistence may shorten or lengthen.

## Non-mechanical rule

Do not apply a fixed exponential decay merely because time passed. Decay must be evidence-conditioned. A catalyst can remain fresh for days if it changes policy, earnings or physical balance and has not been fully priced. A small headline can expire within minutes.

## Mandatory decay ledger

Every decay or no-change record includes:

- last material catalyst time;
- elapsed time;
- new information since last checkpoint;
- market-implied adjustment;
- confirmation breadth;
- flow evidence or proxy status;
- next catalyst proximity;
- before/after consumption vector;
- before/after remaining pressure;
- reason for persistence or decay.

## Transition codes

Use:

- `FORMING`
- `STRENGTHENING`
- `CONFIRMED`
- `MATURE`
- `PARTIALLY_CONSUMED`
- `LATE_STAGE`
- `EXHAUSTED`
- `REVERSING`
- `INVALIDATED`
- `UNCLEAR`

`FUNDAMENTAL_TO_FLOW_TRANSITION` requires evidence that the original information remains known but incremental movement is increasingly explained by hedging, passive flow, liquidation, thin liquidity or positioning. `FLOW_TO_FUNDAMENTAL_TRANSITION` requires new information or broader independent confirmation.
