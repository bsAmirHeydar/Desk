# Worked Examples

> [!abstract] Research mandate

## Definition and economic object

**Worked Examples** is a research object inside the institutional research process, its roles, information boundaries, controls, and decision handoffs. The analyst must isolate the measurable state, the expectation already embedded in prices, the mechanism connecting them, and the horizon on which that inference can survive.

For **Worked Examples**, the relevant institutional domain is **orientation**: the institutional research process, its roles, information boundaries, controls, and decision handoffs. Classify every input as observation, derived measurement, model estimate, market-implied estimate, forecast, causal claim, scenario assumption, judgment, or decision rule.

## Research questions

1. What exact state or mechanism does **Worked Examples** represent, in what unit, population, instrument, and convention?
2. Which **Worked Examples** observations existed at the decision cutoff, which are estimates, and which are revised?
3. What distribution about **Worked Examples** is embedded in consensus, curves, options, valuation, positioning, or physical basis?
4. Which market or variable must lead if the proposed **Worked Examples** mechanism is active?
5. What rival model can create the same target move while **Worked Examples** is unchanged?
6. How do regime, horizon, positioning, liquidity, carry, and implementation alter the payoff?
7. Which predeclared evidence rejects, caps, or expires the **Worked Examples** decision?

## Day-trading decision translation

- Identify the new **Worked Examples** information since the prior close and its source timestamp.
- Reconstruct the priced **Worked Examples** baseline before reading the target move.
- Name the liquid leader closest to the **Worked Examples** mechanism and one independent confirmation.
- Compare observed transmission with the **Worked Examples** event/quiet-day historical distribution.
- Assign a permission and a confidence cap; record the **Worked Examples** cancellation condition.
- Pass only the permission, leader, invalidation, expiry, and size ceiling to technical execution.

For **Worked Examples**, fundamentals restrict the allowed trade set; they do not supply the candle trigger or authorize widening a structural stop.

## Two-to-ten-day swing translation

- Define the still-open **Worked Examples** pricing gap rather than the general narrative.
- Estimate the **Worked Examples** impulse half-life and its uncertainty by regime.
- Map catalysts capable of confirming, reversing, or exhausting the **Worked Examples** campaign.
- Compare outright and relative expressions for carry, convexity, gap, liquidity, and factor purity.
- Specify terminal realization, time expiry, and evidence-based invalidation for **Worked Examples**.

A valid **Worked Examples** thesis with no residual pricing gap, adverse carry beyond expected payoff, or an imminent dominating catalyst is not a deployable swing.

## Example 1 — Hot Inflation, NQ Falls

### Before
- Market prices progressive easing.
- Real yields are stable.
- NQ valuation is sensitive to duration.
- Positioning is long but not disorderly.

### New information
Inflation is above the relevant expected hurdle, with firm underlying components and unfavorable revisions.

### Repricing chain

```text
Higher persistent inflation estimate
→ less easing / higher policy path
→ front-end and real yields rise
→ USD firms
→ long-duration equity discount rate rises
→ NQ underperforms
```

### Confirmation
- two-year yield rises;
- real-yield proxy rises;
- dollar firms;
- semiconductors/mega-cap breadth weakens;
- volatility rises but market remains liquid.

### Permission
`SHORT_ONLY` after a valid structural pullback and trigger.

### Invalidation
Rates reverse and the dollar weakens because the market interprets the release as backward-looking or offset by growth weakness.

## Example 2 — Weak Labor Data, NQ Rallies

### Before
- Inflation is cooling.
- Credit remains stable.
- Market fears policy is too restrictive.
- Easing is not fully priced.

### New information
Labor data weakens moderately without signs of systemic stress.

### Chain

```text
Lower overheating risk
→ lower expected policy path
→ real yields decline
→ financial conditions ease
→ duration assets revalue upward
```

### Permission
Candidate `LONG_ONLY`.

### Critical distinction
If credit spreads widen sharply and cyclicals collapse, the same weak data may represent a growth break. Then the risk-premium channel can dominate and the permission becomes `NO_TRADE` or `SHORT_ONLY`.

## Example 3 — Gold Rallies with the Dollar

A naive model expects gold and USD always to move inversely.

### Possible explanations
- geopolitical/tail-risk demand supports both safe havens;
- global funding stress strengthens USD while systemic concern supports gold;
- central-bank/physical demand offsets rate pressure;
- real yields fall even as the trade-weighted dollar rises against weaker currencies.

### Required work
Decompose real yields, dollar source, risk regime, and gold-specific demand. Do not reject the move only because one correlation failed.

## Example 4 — Oil Headline Spike that Fades

### Headline
Potential supply disruption.

### First reaction
Oil spikes.

### Validation questions
- Is production actually lost?
- Is transport disrupted?
- Does the futures curve tighten?
- Do physical differentials confirm?
- Can spare capacity offset?
- Is demand weakening?
- Is the statement credible and implementable?

### Result
No physical confirmation; prompt spread does not strengthen; headline premium fades.

### Lesson
Geopolitical relevance depends on the path to barrels, not emotional intensity.

## Example 5 — Dovish Central Bank, Equities Fall

### Surface contradiction
Policy language sounds dovish, but equities decline.

### Decomposition
- market had priced a larger dovish surprise;
- policy easing reflects adverse growth information;
- long-end term premium rises despite front-end easing;
- credit spreads widen;
- positioning was crowded long.

### Lesson
Policy action contains both a **policy shock** and an **information shock**. Asset response depends on which dominates.

---

## Example 6 — Strong GDP, ES Up, NQ Flat

### Interpretation
- stronger cash-flow/growth expectations support broad cyclicals;
- yields rise and offset duration-heavy tech;
- sector composition matters;
- “equities” are not one homogeneous asset.

### Expression
ES long may be cleaner than NQ long. The macro view should choose the asset, not force the preferred ticker.

---

## Example 7 — Correct Weekly Thesis, Wrong Day

The weekly thesis expects lower yields and higher NQ. On one session:

- Treasury auction tails;
- yields rise;
- NQ sells off;
- later in the week, data validates disinflation and the weekly thesis resumes.

### Lesson
A valid swing context does not grant an unconditional intraday long permission. Session-specific supply and timing can dominate.
