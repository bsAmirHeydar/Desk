## Definition and economic object

**Fundamental Bias Engine** is a research object inside the institutional research process, its roles, information boundaries, controls, and decision handoffs. The analyst must isolate the measurable state, the expectation already embedded in prices, the mechanism connecting them, and the horizon on which that inference can survive.

For **Fundamental Bias Engine**, the relevant institutional domain is **orientation**: the institutional research process, its roles, information boundaries, controls, and decision handoffs. Classify every input as observation, derived measurement, model estimate, market-implied estimate, forecast, causal claim, scenario assumption, judgment, or decision rule.

## Preserved subject-specific foundation

The bias engine converts heterogeneous evidence into a **constrained directional permission**. It does not issue automatic entries.

## Fundamental Equation

\[
B_{a,h,t} =
f(S_t,\ E_t,\ \Delta E_t,\ T_{a,t},\ P_t,\ F_t,\ H)
\]

Where:

- \(B\): bias for asset \(a\), horizon \(h\), time \(t\);
- \(S\): estimated macro/financial state;
- \(E\): priced expectations;
- \(\Delta E\): likely expectation change;
- \(T\): asset transmission sensitivity;
- \(P\): positioning and optionality;
- \(F\): flows and market plumbing;
- \(H\): horizon and catalyst path.

## Seven Decision Layers

### 1. State
What is actually happening?

### 2. Direction of change
Is it accelerating, decelerating, broadening, or narrowing?

### 3. Priced baseline
What does the market already discount?

### 4. Vulnerable expectation
Which priced assumption can change most?

### 5. Transmission
How should that change affect the chosen asset?

### 6. Timing
Why should it matter during the intended holding period?

### 7. Expression
Is the chosen asset the cleanest expression, or is another market better?

## Bias Scorecard

Use ordinal scores to enforce structure, not to manufacture precision.

| Component | Range | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| State impulse | -2 to +2 | Direct macro impulse |
| Expectations gap | -3 to +3 | Magnitude of likely repricing |
| Transmission clarity | -2 to +2 | Asset-specific causal clarity |
| Cross-asset confirmation | -2 to +2 | Independent market validation |
| Positioning asymmetry | -1 to +1 | Squeeze/crowding effect |
| Timing/catalyst | -1 to +1 | Relevance to horizon |
| Risk-premium/liquidity | -2 to +2 | Supportive or restrictive backdrop |

The sign must be defined **per asset**. For NQ, a positive real-rate impulse is often negative; for the dollar it may be positive.

## Score-to-Permission Mapping

Example mapping:

- +7 or higher: candidate `LONG_ONLY`.
- +3 to +6: long preference, but permission may remain `TWO_WAY_REDUCED`.
- -2 to +2: `TWO_WAY_REDUCED` or `NO_TRADE`.
- -3 to -6: short preference.
- -7 or lower: candidate `SHORT_ONLY`.

A score cannot override:

- weak transmission clarity;
- major unresolved catalyst;
- broken liquidity;
- thesis invalidation;
- horizon mismatch;
- qualified implementation condition requirements.

## Confidence Is Not Direction

Track separately:

```yaml
direction: bullish | bearish | neutral
confidence: low | medium | high
asymmetry: favorable | balanced | unfavorable
```

A bullish direction with low confidence is not `LONG_ONLY`. A modest directional edge with excellent asymmetry can still justify selective execution.

## Decision Hierarchy

1. **Vetoes**
2. **State and expectation gap**
3. **Transmission**
4. **Cross-asset validation**
5. **Positioning/flow modifier**
6. **fundamental implementation**

Positioning can amplify or delay a move, but it should not replace a causal thesis.

## Bias Decay

Bias confidence decays with:

- elapsed time;
- catalysts passed;
- pricing gap closed;
- transmission failure;
- new contradictory evidence;
- regime instability.

A useful conceptual model:

\[
C_{t+\Delta} = C_t \times e^{-\lambda \Delta} \times Q
\]

where \(Q\) represents evidence quality after new information.
