## Preserved subject-specific foundation

Fundamental confidence should alter **permission, selectivity, and maximum risk**, not remove stop discipline.

## Three Separate Quantities

### Directional conviction
How likely is the dominant repricing direction?

### Model confidence
How reliable is the causal interpretation?

### Trade quality
How strong is the actual qualified implementation condition and reward-to-risk?

These must not be collapsed into one feeling.

## Permission Matrix

| Macro clarity | Cross-asset confirmation | implementation quality | Permission |
|---|---|---|---|
| High | High | High | Full allowed risk within system |
| High | Mixed | High | Reduced risk or delayed entry |
| Mixed | High | High | Reduced, selective |
| Mixed | Mixed | High | `TWO_WAY_REDUCED` |
| Low | Any | Any | `NO_TRADE` |
| High | High | Low | No entry |

## Risk Multiplier

A conceptual multiplier:

\[
R_{\text{trade}} = R_{\text{base}} \times M_{\text{macro}} \times M_{\text{setup}} \times M_{\text{liquidity}}
\]

Each multiplier is capped at 1.0. Macro confidence never permits risk above the strategy's hard maximum.

Example:

| Condition | Multiplier |
|---|---:|
| High macro clarity | 1.00 |
| Medium | 0.60–0.80 |
| Low | 0.00–0.40 |
| Major catalyst imminent | 0.00–0.50 |
| Poor liquidity | 0.00 |
| Exceptional qualified implementation condition but mixed macro | Still capped by macro permission |

## Confidence Calibration

Record confidence before outcome, then evaluate:

- frequency of correct directional permissions;
- average return conditional on permission;
- false veto rate;
- loss avoided by `NO_TRADE`;
- performance by confidence bucket;
- calibration: did “high confidence” outperform “medium”?

If high-confidence calls do not outperform, the score is emotional rather than informational.

## Asymmetry

A trade can be attractive even with moderate probability when:

- invalidation is tight and meaningful;
- repricing potential is large;
- positioning can amplify;
- the catalyst path is favorable;
- downside is structurally capped.

But “large possible move” is not a substitute for evidence.

## Hard Rules

- Never widen a implementation stop because macro confidence is high.
- Never add after invalidation without a new setup.
- Never use leverage to compensate for low expected frequency.
- Never equate a dramatic narrative with high probability.
- Reduce risk into binary events unless the event strategy is separately validated.
- Risk is based on portfolio exposure, not only per-ticket stop distance.
