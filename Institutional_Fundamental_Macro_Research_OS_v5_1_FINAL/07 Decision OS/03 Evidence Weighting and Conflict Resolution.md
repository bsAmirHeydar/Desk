Macro evidence is noisy, revised, correlated, and released at different frequencies. A disciplined hierarchy prevents the loudest headline from dominating the model.

## Evidence Dimensions

Score each observation on:

- **reliability** — measurement quality and revision risk;
- **timeliness** — how current it is;
- **causal proximity** — direct driver or distant proxy;
- **independence** — unique signal or duplicate;
- **regime relevance** — does the relationship hold now?;
- **market relevance** — is it likely to change pricing?;
- **horizon fit** — does it matter intraday or over weeks?;
- **surprise** — was it already expected?

## Evidence Hierarchy

Typical ordering:

1. Direct market pricing and official policy action.
2. High-quality official data and revisions.
3. Market-based confirmation across rates, FX, credit, volatility, and commodities.
4. High-frequency/nowcast indicators.
5. Surveys and qualitative reports.
6. Corporate guidance and micro evidence.
7. Positioning and flow estimates.
8. Commentary, media interpretation, and social narrative.

This is context-dependent. Market prices can be distorted; official data can lag; surveys can lead. The hierarchy is a starting prior.

## Independence Test

Ask whether two observations share the same upstream source.

Examples:

- two-year yield and Fed funds futures may be related expressions of one policy-path repricing;
- NQ and semiconductor ETF strength may not be independent;
- several inflation nowcasts may use overlapping inputs;
- multiple news articles may repeat one anonymous source.

Do not count repetition as corroboration.

## Conflict Taxonomy

### Time-horizon conflict
Structural bullish, tactical bearish, intraday neutral.

**Resolution:** label each horizon separately.

### State-versus-price conflict
Data improves, asset weakens.

**Resolution:** inspect expectations, valuation, positioning, and alternative transmission.

### Cross-asset conflict
Equities rally while credit and breadth deteriorate.

**Resolution:** reduce confidence; determine which market historically leads in the current regime.

### Data conflict
Hard data resilient, surveys weak.

**Resolution:** examine timing, sector composition, revisions, and whether surveys lead.

### Policy conflict
Dovish language, hawkish rate pricing.

**Resolution:** focus on reaction function, not adjectives.

### Flow conflict
Fundamental impulse is clear but expiry/rebalance dominates.

**Resolution:** shorten horizon or wait for flow window to pass.

## Conflict Resolution Protocol

1. Restate the exact claim being evaluated.
2. Remove evidence outside the claim's horizon.
3. Remove duplicated evidence.
4. Rank direct versus indirect observations.
5. Ask what each scenario predicts.
6. Identify the observation with greatest discriminating power.
7. Lower confidence when conflict cannot be resolved.
8. Use `NO_TRADE` when the unresolved conflict affects the core transmission channel.

## Disconfirming Evidence Rule

For every active thesis, actively search for:

- one macro contradiction;
- one market-pricing contradiction;
- one asset-specific contradiction;
- one timing/flow contradiction.

A thesis that cannot describe its strongest opposing evidence is not mature.
