# Reflexivity, Narrative and Self-Fulfilling versus Self-Defeating Forecasts

> [!abstract] Canonical thesis
> Public beliefs and forecasts can change behavior, policy and financing, causing outcomes that validate or defeat the original forecast. Institutional analysis must model the communication and feedback channel explicitly.

## Analytical intuition

A warning about a bank can accelerate withdrawals and make the warning true. A credible inflation forecast can change policy or wage behavior and make the forecast false. Forecasts are sometimes interventions.

## Institutional definitions and distinctions

| Term or distinction | Precise meaning |
|---|---|
| Self-fulfilling forecast | Belief changes actions in a direction that produces the expected outcome. |
| Self-defeating forecast | Belief triggers stabilizing action that prevents the predicted outcome. |
| Common knowledge | Agents know a fact and know others know it, affecting coordination. |
| Narrative mechanism | Transmission of a simplified causal story through institutions and social networks. |

## Mechanism map

1. Identify who observes the forecast.
2. Specify behavior and policy response.
3. Map coordination and balance-sheet effects.
4. Distinguish informative prediction from performative intervention.
5. Identify stabilizers and thresholds.
6. Evaluate outcomes against the forecast conditional on response, not naively.

## Formal structure and notation

### Performative system

$$
Y_{t+1}=f(State_t,Forecast_t,Response(Forecast_t))
$$

The forecast enters the data-generating process through response, creating evaluation challenges.

## Competing interpretations and adversarial synthesis

| View | What it explains | Where it can fail |
|---|---|---|
| Forecast-as-measurement | Forecast observes but does not materially alter the system. | Often false for public policy and fragile finance. |
| Performative prediction | Prediction changes the target process. | Can be overused to excuse poor forecasts. |
| Strategic communication | Institutions design forecasts or guidance to shape behavior. | Separating belief from policy instrument is difficult. |

## Historical and institutional cases

### Bank-run warnings

Solvency concerns interact with coordination and liquidity.

### Central-bank forward guidance

Communication affects rates and demand, potentially changing the forecast that motivated guidance.

## Failure modes and red-team tests

- Calling all forecast errors self-defeating.
- Ignoring who had access and capacity to respond.
- Treating narrative spread as a mechanism without evidence.
- Failing to distinguish confidence effects from fundamental cash flows.
- Evaluating a conditional forecast as unconditional.
