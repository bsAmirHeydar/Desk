# Lucas Critique, Goodhart Law and Model Endogeneity

> [!abstract] Canonical thesis
> Relationships estimated under one policy and incentive regime can change when authorities or agents respond to them. A statistic used as a target, rule or public signal can lose the behavior that made it useful.

## Beginner intuition

If a government rewards a measure, people optimize the measure. If a central bank changes its rule, households and firms change expectations. Historical coefficients are not constants of nature.

## Institutional definitions and distinctions

| Term or distinction | Precise meaning |
|---|---|
| Lucas critique | Policy evaluation using behavioral relationships that change when policy rules change. |
| Goodhart law | A measure can cease to be useful when it becomes a target. |
| Policy invariance | Whether a parameter remains stable under a defined policy change. |
| Strategic response | Behavioral adaptation to incentives, publication or regulation. |

## Mechanism map

1. Identify parameters assumed stable.
2. Ask how agents learn and respond to the rule.
3. Separate deep constraints from reduced-form correlations.
4. Study pre- and post-policy behavior.
5. Use institutional evidence about gaming and substitution.
6. Maintain model-break conditions and re-estimation rules.

## Formal structure and notation

### Policy-dependent behavior

$$
\theta=\theta(Policy\ Rule,Information,Institutions)
$$

Reduced-form parameters can be functions of the policy regime rather than fixed objects.

## Competing interpretations and adversarial synthesis

| View | What it explains | Where it can fail |
|---|---|---|
| Deep-parameter structural models | Aim for policy-invariant preferences and technology. | Deep parameters and expectation formation are difficult to identify. |
| Reduced-form empirical models | Often forecast well within regime. | Can fail after intervention or public adoption. |
| Adaptive institutional models | Represent changing rules and behavior. | Complex and potentially weakly identified. |

## Historical and institutional cases

### Monetary targeting

Stable money-demand relationships changed with financial innovation and policy adaptation.

### Regulatory ratios

Banks can reclassify, securitize or shift activity when risk metrics become binding targets.

## Failure modes and red-team tests

- Assuming historical beta survives a policy change.
- Using a public indicator without studying strategic adaptation.
- Calling every forecast failure Goodhart’s law.
- Ignoring slow institutional adaptation.
- Seeking invariant parameters without validating their meaning.
