# Market Microstructure and Intermediary-Constraint Traditions

> [!abstract] Canonical thesis
> Prices are formed through trading institutions, inventories, funding, information asymmetry and intermediary risk capacity.

## Beginner intuition

Focus on liquidity, dealer balance sheets, collateral, segmentation, limits to arbitrage and market design.

### Treasury market stress

Dealer balance-sheet constraints can impair a benchmark market despite unchanged long-run repayment fundamentals.

### Dollar funding

Cross-currency basis and swap markets reveal intermediary constraints and hedging demand.

- **Structural/secular:** identify the institutions, property relations, technology, capital structure or balance-sheet architecture that make **Market Microstructure and Intermediary-Constraint Traditions** informative over long horizons.
- **Cyclical:** derive the tradition’s explanation of expansion, contraction, inflation, employment and financial fragility; compare its turning-point logic with serious rivals.
- **Tactical/multi-week:** determine which policy, expectation, funding or distributional change would make the framework’s mechanism newly relevant.
- **Event/current:** ask whether the event is genuine evidence for the mechanism or only a short observation compatible with many models.

## Failure modes and red-team tests

- Short-run mechanisms may not explain long-run value and can be data-intensive.
- Using the school as identity rather than a conditional model.
- Citing famous conclusions without reconstructing assumptions.
- Ignoring evidence that falls outside the preferred ontology.
- Combining incompatible closure rules without explaining the synthesis.
