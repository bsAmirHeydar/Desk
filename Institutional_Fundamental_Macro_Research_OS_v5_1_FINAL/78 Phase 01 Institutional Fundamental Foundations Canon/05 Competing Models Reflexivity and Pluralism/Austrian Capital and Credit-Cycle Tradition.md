# Austrian Capital and Credit-Cycle Tradition

> [!abstract] Canonical thesis
> Interest rates coordinate intertemporal production, and credit expansion can distort capital structure and generate malinvestment.

## Beginner intuition

Focus on heterogeneous capital, entrepreneurial discovery, monetary distortion and liquidation.

## Historical and institutional cases

### Housing and credit booms

Cheap funding and optimistic expectations can lengthen projects and weaken underwriting.

### Liquidation debate

Rapid liquidation may restore relative prices but can amplify debt deflation and coordination failure.

- **Cyclical:** derive the tradition’s explanation of expansion, contraction, inflation, employment and financial fragility; compare its turning-point logic with serious rivals.
- **Tactical/multi-week:** determine which policy, expectation, funding or distributional change would make the framework’s mechanism newly relevant.

## Failure modes and red-team tests

- Operational measurement of natural rates and malinvestment is difficult; policy and balance-sheet stabilizers are often underdeveloped.
- Using the school as identity rather than a conditional model.
- Citing famous conclusions without reconstructing assumptions.
- Ignoring evidence that falls outside the preferred ontology.
- Combining incompatible closure rules without explaining the synthesis.
