# Classical and Neoclassical Equilibrium Traditions

> [!abstract] Canonical thesis
> Prices and quantities coordinate decentralized choices through scarcity, substitution and intertemporal allocation.

## Beginner intuition

Focus on optimization, market clearing, capital accumulation, comparative statics and long-run constraints.

## Historical and institutional cases

### Solow growth accounting

Separates capital deepening, labor and residual productivity but does not identify the residual’s mechanism.

### Real-business-cycle interpretation

Technology and preference shocks generate cycles through intertemporal substitution, but nominal and financial frictions may matter greatly.

## Multihorizon interpretation

- **Structural/secular:** identify the institutions, property relations, technology, capital structure or balance-sheet architecture that make **Classical and Neoclassical Equilibrium Traditions** informative over long horizons.
- **Cyclical:** derive the tradition’s explanation of expansion, contraction, inflation, employment and financial fragility; compare its turning-point logic with serious rivals.
- **Tactical/multi-week:** determine which policy, expectation, funding or distributional change would make the framework’s mechanism newly relevant.
- **Event/current:** ask whether the event is genuine evidence for the mechanism or only a short observation compatible with many models.

## Failure modes and red-team tests

- Representative agents, complete markets and rapid adjustment can conceal institutions, distribution and persistent disequilibrium.
- Using the school as identity rather than a conditional model.
- Citing famous conclusions without reconstructing assumptions.
- Ignoring evidence that falls outside the preferred ontology.
- Combining incompatible closure rules without explaining the synthesis.
