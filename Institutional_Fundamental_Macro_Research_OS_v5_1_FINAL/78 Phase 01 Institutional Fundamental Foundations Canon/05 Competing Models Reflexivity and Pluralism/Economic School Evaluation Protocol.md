# Economic School Evaluation Protocol

> [!abstract] Purpose
> Evaluate each economic tradition in its strongest coherent form without repeating a generic template inside every school monograph.

## Evaluation sequence

1. **Ontology:** What entities, constraints and institutions are primary?
2. **Closure:** What equilibrates or determines the path—prices, quantities, finance, policy, power, expectations or network dynamics?
3. **Money and finance:** Are money, banks, credit and balance sheets neutral, endogenous, destabilizing or institutionally specific?
4. **Expectations:** Are agents rational, adaptive, convention-driven, narrative-based or strategically coordinated?
5. **Adjustment:** Which variables move first and which are sticky?
6. **Distribution and power:** Are income distribution, ownership, bargaining and state capacity central or secondary?
7. **Formal signature:** Which identities, equations or simulations express the model?
8. **Empirical signature:** What observable evidence would be more likely if the tradition’s mechanism dominates?
9. **Historical domain:** In which institutions, regimes and horizons has the framework been most informative?
10. **Failure conditions:** What evidence, regime or institutional fact makes the framework weak or incomplete?
11. **Rival comparison:** Which serious alternative explains the same facts differently?
12. **Synthesis rule:** Which mechanisms can be combined without mixing incompatible definitions or closure rules?

## Accreditation output

For every school, produce:

- a 300-word steelman;
- a causal and accounting map;
- a list of endogenous and exogenous variables;
- at least three discriminating predictions;
- two historical successes and two failures;
- a comparison with three rival traditions;
- a statement of domain, horizon and institutional limits;
- a contradiction register for any attempted synthesis.
