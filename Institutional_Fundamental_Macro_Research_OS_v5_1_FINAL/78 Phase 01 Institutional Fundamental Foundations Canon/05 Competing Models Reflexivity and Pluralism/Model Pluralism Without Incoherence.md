# Model Pluralism Without Incoherence

> [!abstract] Canonical thesis
> Pluralism is disciplined comparison of models with explicit domains, assumptions and discriminating evidence. It is not a license to select whichever framework supports a desired conclusion.

## Beginner intuition

Different models can illuminate different mechanisms. But they cannot all be right in every sense. Good pluralism asks when each model applies, what it predicts and where the models genuinely conflict.

## Institutional definitions and distinctions

| Term or distinction | Precise meaning |
|---|---|
| Model domain | Institutional, historical and horizon conditions under which a model is intended to operate. |
| Commensurability | Whether two models use compatible definitions and can be compared on the same object. |
| Ensemble | A rule for combining predictions or weights across models. |
| Contradiction registry | A record of claims that cannot be jointly true under the same definitions and conditions. |

## Mechanism map

1. Normalize definitions and forecast object.
2. List assumptions and domain for each model.
3. Derive common and discriminating predictions.
4. Score historical performance without hindsight.
5. Weight or select models by evidence and regime.
6. Preserve unresolved contradictions and unknowns.

## Formal structure and notation

### Model mixture

$$
p(y\mid D)=\sum_m w_m p(y\mid M_m,D)
$$

Weights represent evidence and domain relevance, not political compromise. Unknown models remain outside the mixture.

## Competing interpretations and adversarial synthesis

| View | What it explains | Where it can fail |
|---|---|---|
| Eclectic synthesis | Combines useful mechanisms pragmatically. | Can become an untestable list. |
| Paradigm competition | Chooses coherent internally consistent systems. | Can overcommit to one ontology. |
| Bayesian model averaging | Weights predictive models formally. | Marginal likelihood and model-set choices can be fragile. |

## Historical and institutional cases

### Post-pandemic inflation

Demand, supply, fiscal, labor, housing, monetary and expectation mechanisms all mattered, but their relative importance changed through time.

### Financial crisis analysis

Efficient-market, behavioral, credit-cycle and network models offered different predictions; synthesis required explicit balance-sheet and funding channels.

## Multihorizon interpretation

- **Structural/secular:** identify persistent institutions, stocks or constraints governing **Model Pluralism Without Incoherence**; begin with: Normalize definitions and forecast object.
- **Cyclical:** determine how the mechanism changes across expansion, slowdown, recession, recovery, inflation and disinflation; interrogate: List assumptions and domain for each model.
- **Tactical/multi-week:** identify which expectation, valuation or policy assumption is vulnerable; test: Derive common and discriminating predictions.
- **Event/current:** define the new information, contemporaneous expectation and first observable mechanism; verify: Score historical performance without hindsight.
- Use the full inheritance and conflict standard in [[78 Phase 01 Institutional Fundamental Foundations Canon/06 Multihorizon Synthesis and Institutional Conclusions/Horizon Inheritance, Conflict Resolution and Double-Counting Control]].

## Failure modes and red-team tests

- Averaging contradictory definitions.
- Choosing models after outcomes.
- Giving equal weight to unequal evidence.
- Treating model diversity as a substitute for conclusion.
- Ignoring that all candidate models may share the same omission.
