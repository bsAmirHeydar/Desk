# Falsifiability, Severe Testing and the Unknown Register

> [!abstract] Canonical thesis
> A research claim is institutional only when the evidence that would weaken or reject it is stated in advance. An unknown register preserves unresolved questions and prevents uncertainty from being silently converted into narrative certainty.

## Beginner intuition

A theory that explains every possible outcome explains nothing. Before an event, write what you expect to observe if the theory is right, what would count against it, and what remains unknown. Afterward, do not move the goalposts.

## Institutional definitions and distinctions

| Term or distinction | Precise meaning |
|---|---|
| Falsifier | An observation or pattern that is materially less likely under the claim than under a rival. |
| Severe test | A test with a high probability of detecting an error if the claim is false in the relevant way. |
| Auxiliary assumption | A supporting assumption needed to connect a theory to an observable prediction. |
| Unknown register | A maintained list of unresolved variables, inaccessible evidence and unmodeled mechanisms. |
| Goalpost shift | Redefining success after observing the result. |

## Mechanism map

1. Translate the claim into observable implications.
2. List auxiliary assumptions and data limitations.
3. Specify discriminating evidence before observation.
4. Compare the claim with serious rivals, not a null caricature.
5. Record failed predictions and explanation changes.
6. Update, narrow or retire claims rather than immunizing them.

## Formal structure and notation

### Likelihood comparison

$$
BF_{12}=\frac{P(D\mid M_1)}{P(D\mid M_2)}
$$

Evidence discriminates when data D are meaningfully more likely under one model than another; prior plausibility still matters for posterior belief.

### Pre-specified score

$$
Score=\sum_k w_k\,L(Outcome_k,Forecast_k)
$$

A scoring rule fixed before outcomes limits selective emphasis and narrative rescue.

## Competing interpretations and adversarial synthesis

| View | What it explains | Where it can fail |
|---|---|---|
| Popperian falsification | Demands risky predictions and rejection. | Economic tests depend on auxiliary assumptions and noisy data. |
| Bayesian updating | Allows graded belief revision across imperfect evidence. | Flexible priors and model sets can shield favored views. |
| Lakatosian research programs | Evaluates whether theory adjustments are progressive or degenerating. | Judgment about progress can be retrospective. |

## Historical and institutional cases

### Phillips curve

Different specifications survived by adding expectations, slack measures and supply shocks; the key question is whether modifications improved new predictions.

### Fiscal multipliers

Effects vary by slack, monetary regime, openness and financing. A universal claim is easy to falsify but an infinitely conditional claim becomes empty.

## Multihorizon interpretation

- **Structural/secular:** identify persistent institutions, stocks or constraints governing **Falsifiability, Severe Testing and the Unknown Register**; begin with: Translate the claim into observable implications.
- **Cyclical:** determine how the mechanism changes across expansion, slowdown, recession, recovery, inflation and disinflation; interrogate: List auxiliary assumptions and data limitations.
- **Tactical/multi-week:** identify which expectation, valuation or policy assumption is vulnerable; test: Specify discriminating evidence before observation.
- **Event/current:** define the new information, contemporaneous expectation and first observable mechanism; verify: Compare the claim with serious rivals, not a null caricature.

## Failure modes and red-team tests

- Writing invalidation only after the outcome.
- Rejecting a framework because one noisy release differs.
- Adding conditions until no observation can count against the thesis.
- Ignoring failed forecasts while celebrating qualitative stories.
- Deleting unknowns once a committee reaches consensus.
