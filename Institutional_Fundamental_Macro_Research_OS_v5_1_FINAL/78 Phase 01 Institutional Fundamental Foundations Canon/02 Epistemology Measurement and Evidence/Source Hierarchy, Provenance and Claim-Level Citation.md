# Source Hierarchy, Provenance and Claim-Level Citation

> [!abstract] Canonical thesis
> A source list is not evidence architecture. Institutional provenance requires claim-level linkage to the original publication, exact table or passage, release and vintage time, transformation, retrieval method and analyst inference.

## Analytical intuition

Linking to a central bank homepage does not prove a sentence. A reader should be able to trace the statement to a particular release, series, table, definition and date, and then see what the analyst added.

## Institutional definitions and distinctions

| Term or distinction | Precise meaning |
|---|---|
| Primary source | The institution or dataset that originated the information. |
| Secondary source | Analysis or synthesis based on primary evidence. |
| Locator | Page, table, series ID, paragraph, timestamp or query that identifies the supporting material. |
| Lineage | The chain from original source through transformations to the final claim. |
| Evidence packet | A preserved bundle of source snapshot, metadata, transformations and interpretation. |

## Mechanism map

1. Rank sources by authority, proximity and methodological transparency.
2. Capture exact locators and release metadata.
3. Store transformations and units.
4. Separate quotation, paraphrase, calculation and inference.
5. Record conflicts among sources and resolution rules.
6. Make every material claim independently auditable.

## Formal structure and notation

### Lineage chain

$$
Claim\leftarrow Inference\leftarrow Transformation\leftarrow Observation\leftarrow Source
$$

Each arrow must be documented. A missing link turns an assertion into unauditable narrative.

### Evidence weight

$$
w_i=f(Authority,Directness,Timeliness,Method,Independence,Uncertainty)
$$

Weight is multidimensional. A recent source can be less authoritative; multiple sources can be non-independent.

## Competing interpretations and adversarial synthesis

| View | What it explains | Where it can fail |
|---|---|---|
| Authority-first hierarchy | Prioritizes official and primary sources. | Authorities can revise, frame or omit; institutional incentives matter. |
| Triangulation-first hierarchy | Requires independent sources and methods. | Independence may be overstated and conflicts hard to aggregate. |
| Open-source replication | Prioritizes transparent, reproducible evidence. | Some high-value data are licensed or confidential. |

## Historical and institutional cases

### Central-bank statement

The exact wording, publication time and paragraph matter because summaries may remove conditionality.

### Commodity balance

Official production, customs, satellite estimates and company reports can conflict due to definitions and timing; lineage reveals whether disagreement is real.

## Multihorizon interpretation

- **Structural/secular:** identify persistent institutions, stocks or constraints governing **Source Hierarchy, Provenance and Claim-Level Citation**; begin with: Rank sources by authority, proximity and methodological transparency.
- **Cyclical:** determine how the mechanism changes across expansion, slowdown, recession, recovery, inflation and disinflation; interrogate: Capture exact locators and release metadata.
- **Tactical/multi-week:** identify which expectation, valuation or policy assumption is vulnerable; test: Store transformations and units.
- **Event/current:** define the new information, contemporaneous expectation and first observable mechanism; verify: Separate quotation, paraphrase, calculation and inference.
- Use the full inheritance and conflict standard in [[78 Phase 01 Institutional Fundamental Foundations Canon/06 Multihorizon Synthesis and Institutional Conclusions/Horizon Inheritance, Conflict Resolution and Double-Counting Control]].

## Failure modes and red-team tests

- Citing a homepage.
- Citing a secondary article for a primary statistic without need.
- Losing the historical version of a revised publication.
- Treating several outlets repeating the same wire story as independent confirmation.
- Failing to distinguish absence of evidence from evidence of absence.
