# Historical Point-in-Time Case Reconstruction Standard

> [!abstract] Canonical thesis
> A historical case is institutional evidence only when it reconstructs the contemporaneous information set, priced expectations, institutional constraints, rival explanations and multi-horizon outcomes before conducting ex-post attribution.

## Analytical intuition

A case study should not say “everyone should have known.” It should show what was published, believed, priced and knowable on each date—and what remained uncertain.

## Institutional definitions and distinctions

| Term or distinction | Precise meaning |
|---|---|
| Cutoff | Exact historical date, time and timezone beyond which information is inadmissible. |
| Contemporaneous expectation | Survey, curve, forecast or documented belief available by cutoff. |
| Replay | Ex-ante reconstruction without future knowledge. |
| Attribution | Ex-post decomposition of outcome after the replay is frozen. |
| Counterfactual audit | Comparison with alternative conclusions or scenarios using the same historical information set. |

## Mechanism map

1. Define episode and cutoffs.
2. Collect first-release data, documents and market-implied expectations.
3. Build a timestamped chronology.
4. Freeze ex-ante scenarios and conclusion.
5. Reveal outcomes only in a separate section.
6. Attribute errors to state, expectation, model, timing, measurement or unknowable shock.

## Formal structure and notation

### Replay separation

$$
Replay_T=g(\mathcal I_T),\qquad Audit_{T+h}=g(\mathcal I_T,Y_{T:T+h})
$$

The audit may use outcomes; the replay may not.

### Surprise vector

$$
Surprise=Release-ContemporaneousExpectedDistribution
$$

The distribution and component structure matter more than a single consensus median.

## Competing interpretations and adversarial synthesis

| View | What it explains | Where it can fail |
|---|---|---|
| Narrative history | Rich institutional context. | Vulnerable to hindsight and selection. |
| Event database | Comparable structured records. | Can omit qualitative mechanisms. |
| Hybrid case laboratory | Combines chronology, data and documents. | Resource intensive and archive dependent. |

## Historical and institutional cases

### 2008 crisis reconstruction

Solvency, liquidity, collateral, policy facilities and evolving information must be separated.

### 2021–2022 inflation

First releases, reopening narratives, fiscal effects and later revisions must not be collapsed into one hindsight account.

## Multihorizon interpretation

- **Structural/secular:** identify persistent institutions, stocks or constraints governing **Historical Point-in-Time Case Reconstruction Standard**; begin with: Define episode and cutoffs.
- **Cyclical:** determine how the mechanism changes across expansion, slowdown, recession, recovery, inflation and disinflation; interrogate: Collect first-release data, documents and market-implied expectations.
- **Tactical/multi-week:** identify which expectation, valuation or policy assumption is vulnerable; test: Build a timestamped chronology.
- **Event/current:** define the new information, contemporaneous expectation and first observable mechanism; verify: Freeze ex-ante scenarios and conclusion.
- Use the full inheritance and conflict standard in [[78 Phase 01 Institutional Fundamental Foundations Canon/06 Multihorizon Synthesis and Institutional Conclusions/Horizon Inheritance, Conflict Resolution and Double-Counting Control]].

## Failure modes and red-team tests

- Using revised series.
- Choosing only famous successful calls.
- Outcome language in ex-ante sections.
- No priced-expectation reconstruction.
- Attributing all error to bad luck or all success to skill.
