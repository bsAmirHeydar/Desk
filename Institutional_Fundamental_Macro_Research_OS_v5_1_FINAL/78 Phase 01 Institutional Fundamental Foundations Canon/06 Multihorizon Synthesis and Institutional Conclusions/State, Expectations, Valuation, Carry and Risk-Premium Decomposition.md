# State, Expectations, Valuation, Carry and Risk-Premium Decomposition

> [!abstract] Canonical thesis
> A complete fundamental conclusion separates the underlying state from beliefs about that state, the valuation paid, contractual carry and compensation for bearing uncertainty. A favorable state can be an unfavorable proposition when expectations and valuation are excessive.

## Beginner intuition

A strong company can be a poor investment at an extreme price; a weak economy can coincide with rising assets if expectations were worse. Fundamental analysis therefore studies both reality and what is already believed and paid for.

## Institutional definitions and distinctions

| Term or distinction | Precise meaning |
|---|---|
| State | Distribution of economic, financial, institutional or physical conditions. |
| Expectation | Belief distribution about future states and payoffs. |
| Valuation | Price relative to a defined payoff, cash-flow, scarcity or replacement-value benchmark. |
| Carry | Expected contractual or mechanical accrual absent changes in valuation and default. |
| Risk premium | Compensation required for bearing uncertainty, illiquidity, covariance, tail risk or institutional constraints. |

## Mechanism map

1. Estimate current state and uncertainty.
2. Reconstruct the priced or consensus distribution.
3. Identify the gap and which part is genuinely unpriced.
4. Decompose expected return into carry, cash-flow change, valuation change and risk premium.
5. Assess asymmetry and alternative explanations.
6. State what observable evidence would close or widen the gap.

## Formal structure and notation

### Return decomposition

$$
R_{t,t+h}\approx Carry+CashFlowNews+ValuationChange+RiskPremiumChange+Residual
$$

The labels and exact identity differ by asset, but the decomposition prevents “good fundamentals” from becoming a complete conclusion.

### Present value

$$
P_t=\mathbb E_t\left[\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}M_{t,t+j}CF_{t+j}\right]
$$

Price depends on expected cash flows and the stochastic discount factor; state news can operate through either or both.

## Competing interpretations and adversarial synthesis

| View | What it explains | Where it can fail |
|---|---|---|
| Cash-flow-dominant view | Prices primarily reflect revisions to expected payoffs. | Discount-rate variation can dominate over many horizons. |
| Discount-rate view | Risk premia and required returns vary strongly. | Cash-flow and state measurement remain essential. |
| Behavioral expectations view | Belief errors create persistent misvaluation. | Mispricing is difficult to identify independently of model error. |

## Historical and institutional cases

### Late-cycle equity strength

Earnings can remain strong while discount rates and tail risks rise.

### Sovereign bond rally in weak growth

Deteriorating growth may improve duration payoffs while worsening credit or fiscal components.

- **Cyclical:** determine how the mechanism changes across expansion, slowdown, recession, recovery, inflation and disinflation; interrogate: Reconstruct the priced or consensus distribution.
- **Tactical/multi-week:** identify which expectation, valuation or policy assumption is vulnerable; test: Identify the gap and which part is genuinely unpriced.

## Failure modes and red-team tests

- Calling a state favorable without measuring expectations.
- Using one valuation metric across regimes and accounting standards.
- Ignoring carry and financing.
- Treating risk premium as an unexplained residual without institutional analysis.
- Assuming the priced distribution is observable without uncertainty.
