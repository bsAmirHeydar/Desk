# Scenario Trees, Tail Asymmetry and Conditional Fundamental Conclusions

> [!abstract] Canonical thesis
> Institutional fundamental analysis produces conditional distributions and scenario-contingent conclusions, not a single unqualified narrative. Tail mechanisms deserve explicit probability, severity, detectability and response analysis.

## Analytical intuition

Instead of saying “growth will be fine,” write a base case, upside case and downside case, then explain what would make each one more likely and how severe it would be.

## Institutional definitions and distinctions

| Term or distinction | Precise meaning |
|---|---|
| Scenario | Internally coherent path of states, policies and feedbacks. |
| Trigger | Observable development that changes scenario probability or initiates a mechanism. |
| Tail asymmetry | Unequal probability-weighted severity between adverse and favorable outcomes. |
| Early-warning indicator | Observation expected to change before the terminal outcome under a specified mechanism. |
| Conditional conclusion | Statement valid only if named assumptions and scenario conditions hold. |

## Mechanism map

1. Define the decision horizon and variables.
2. Construct mutually intelligible, not necessarily exhaustive scenarios.
3. Assign ranges rather than false point probabilities.
4. Map triggers, feedback loops and policy responses.
5. Estimate severity, reversibility and cross-asset transmission.
6. Update probabilities and conclusions when discriminating evidence arrives.

## Formal structure and notation

### Scenario expected value

$$
\mathbb E[V]=\sum_{s=1}^{S}p_sV_s
$$

Expected value is not sufficient when tails, liquidity, mandate constraints and model ambiguity matter.

### Expected shortfall

$$
ES_\alpha=\mathbb E[L\mid L\ge VaR_\alpha]
$$

Tail severity complements probability and supports explicit downside comparison.

## Competing interpretations and adversarial synthesis

| View | What it explains | Where it can fail |
|---|---|---|
| Probability-weighted scenarios | Enforce consistency and comparability. | Probabilities can create false precision. |
| Narrative stress tests | Explore mechanisms beyond historical data. | Can become unconstrained storytelling. |
| Robust decision making | Seeks conclusions resilient across models. | May sacrifice upside or become overly conservative. |

## Historical and institutional cases

### Sovereign funding deterioration

Base-case refinancing can coexist with a low-probability nonlinear confidence and collateral spiral.

### Energy embargo

Physical availability, substitution, inventory and policy response define several paths rather than one price target.

## Failure modes and red-team tests

- Scenarios that differ only in adjectives.
- Probabilities that do not sum or update coherently.
- Ignoring endogenous policy response.
- Using historical volatility to bound structural tails.
- Confusing a stress test with a forecast.
