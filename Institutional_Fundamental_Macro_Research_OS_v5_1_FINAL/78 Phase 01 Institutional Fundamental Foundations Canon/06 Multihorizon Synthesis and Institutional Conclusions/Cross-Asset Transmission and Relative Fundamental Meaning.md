# Cross-Asset Transmission and Relative Fundamental Meaning

> [!abstract] Canonical thesis
> Fundamental information has no universal sign. Its implication depends on the asset payoff, financing structure, currency denomination, ownership base, policy response and the state already priced.

## Beginner intuition

Higher inflation can hurt a long-duration bond, help some commodity producers, strengthen or weaken a currency depending on policy credibility, and affect equities through both revenues and discount rates. The same fact means different things to different claims.

## Institutional definitions and distinctions

| Term or distinction | Precise meaning |
|---|---|
| Transmission channel | Mechanism linking state or information to an asset payoff or required return. |
| Relative fundamental | Difference between two states, policies, valuations or payoffs relevant to a spread or currency pair. |
| Numeraire | Unit in which value is expressed; changing it can alter apparent returns and exposures. |
| Ownership channel | Effect of investor base, regulation, hedging demand and balance-sheet capacity. |
| Cross-asset confirmation | Evidence from another market that supports a specified mechanism, not mere simultaneous direction. |

## Mechanism map

1. Define each instrument’s contractual and economic payoff.
2. Map state news to cash flow, discount rate, default, scarcity, currency and liquidity channels.
3. Identify first-order and second-order effects.
4. Distinguish absolute from relative implications.
5. Condition on policy reaction and ownership structure.
6. Search for discriminating cross-asset evidence and contradictions.

## Formal structure and notation

### Generic payoff sensitivity

$$
\Delta V_i\approx\sum_k \beta_{i,k}(Regime,Horizon)\Delta Driver_k
$$

Sensitivities are conditional objects; a fixed beta is a simplification requiring validation.

### Relative-value gap

$$
Gap_{A,B}=(Research_A-Priced_A)-(Research_B-Priced_B)
$$

Relative conclusions compare research-pricing gaps after aligning units, horizon and risk.

## Competing interpretations and adversarial synthesis

| View | What it explains | Where it can fail |
|---|---|---|
| Stable-factor mapping | Assets have persistent macro betas. | Betas change with composition, valuation, policy and ownership. |
| Narrative mapping | Market stories determine which channel dominates. | Narratives need mechanism and evidence. |
| Structural cash-flow mapping | Contractual payoffs anchor long-run effects. | Short-run intermediation and risk-premium changes can dominate. |

## Historical and institutional cases

### Inflation shock across assets

Rates, FX, equities, credit, gold and commodities react through different and sometimes opposing channels.

### Dollar funding stress

A stronger dollar can signal relative growth, policy divergence or global balance-sheet constraint; the asset implications differ.

## Multihorizon interpretation

- **Structural/secular:** identify persistent institutions, stocks or constraints governing **Cross-Asset Transmission and Relative Fundamental Meaning**; begin with: Define each instrument’s contractual and economic payoff.
- **Cyclical:** determine how the mechanism changes across expansion, slowdown, recession, recovery, inflation and disinflation; interrogate: Map state news to cash flow, discount rate, default, scarcity, currency and liquidity channels.
- **Tactical/multi-week:** identify which expectation, valuation or policy assumption is vulnerable; test: Identify first-order and second-order effects.
- **Event/current:** define the new information, contemporaneous expectation and first observable mechanism; verify: Distinguish absolute from relative implications.
- Use the full inheritance and conflict standard in [[78 Phase 01 Institutional Fundamental Foundations Canon/06 Multihorizon Synthesis and Institutional Conclusions/Horizon Inheritance, Conflict Resolution and Double-Counting Control]].

## Failure modes and red-team tests

- Assigning a permanent sign to a macro variable.
- Calling correlated direction confirmation without specifying mechanism.
- Ignoring currency and hedge conventions.
- Comparing assets on different horizons.
- Forgetting that index composition changes the fundamental exposure.
