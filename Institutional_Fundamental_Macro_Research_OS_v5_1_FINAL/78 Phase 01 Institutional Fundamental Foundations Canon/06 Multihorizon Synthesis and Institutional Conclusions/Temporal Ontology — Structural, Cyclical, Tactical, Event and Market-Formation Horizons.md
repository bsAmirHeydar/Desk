# Temporal Ontology — Structural, Cyclical, Tactical, Event and Market-Formation Horizons

> [!abstract] Canonical thesis
> A fundamental claim is incomplete until its horizon, update frequency, expected persistence and invalidation clock are defined. Time horizon is part of the research object, not a formatting choice.

## Beginner intuition

The same economy can be structurally productive, cyclically slowing, tactically supported by policy and temporarily shocked by a data release. These statements do not contradict one another because they answer different time questions.

## Institutional definitions and distinctions

| Term or distinction | Precise meaning |
|---|---|
| Structural horizon | Persistent institutions, technologies, demographics, resource constraints and balance-sheet architecture measured in years or decades. |
| Cyclical horizon | Expansion, slowdown, recession, recovery, inflation and credit phases measured in months or years. |
| Tactical horizon | Expectation, policy, financing or earnings revisions measured in weeks or months. |
| Event horizon | The information and response window around a defined release, decision or shock. |
| Market-formation horizon | The short interval in which liquidity, intermediation and order imbalance translate information into observable prices without becoming a non-fundamental pattern system. |

## Mechanism map

1. Define the economic process and its natural adjustment speed.
2. Identify when new evidence becomes available.
3. Estimate whether the mechanism changes levels, growth rates, expectations or risk premia.
4. Separate a persistent state from a temporary impulse.
5. Specify which higher-horizon priors constrain lower-horizon interpretation.
6. Record an explicit expiry clock for every conclusion.

## Formal structure and notation

### Horizon-indexed state

$$
S_t^{(h)}=\mathbb E[State_{t:t+h}\mid \mathcal I_t]
$$

The state is indexed by horizon h and current information set; one state label cannot validly stand for every horizon.

### Observation decomposition

$$
Y_t=Structural_t+Cycle_t+Tactical_t+Event_t+Noise_t
$$

The decomposition is conceptual unless the components are identified and measured; it prevents one narrative from absorbing all variation.

## Competing interpretations and adversarial synthesis

| View | What it explains | Where it can fail |
|---|---|---|
| Nested horizons | Lower horizons inherit constraints from higher horizons. | Can become deterministic and underweight turning points. |
| Independent horizon models | Each horizon is estimated separately. | Can create inconsistent states and double counting. |
| Unified latent-state system | A common state process generates observations at multiple frequencies. | Identification and communication are demanding. |

## Historical and institutional cases

### Productivity boom with monetary tightening

A favorable structural technology thesis can coexist with cyclical inflation pressure and tactical valuation compression.

### Recession scare with strong balance sheets

Weak monthly activity can coexist with resilient long-run solvency, changing the implication across credit and equity horizons.

## Multihorizon interpretation

- **Structural/secular:** identify persistent institutions, stocks or constraints governing **Temporal Ontology — Structural, Cyclical, Tactical, Event and Market-Formation Horizons**; begin with: Define the economic process and its natural adjustment speed.
- **Cyclical:** determine how the mechanism changes across expansion, slowdown, recession, recovery, inflation and disinflation; interrogate: Identify when new evidence becomes available.
- **Tactical/multi-week:** identify which expectation, valuation or policy assumption is vulnerable; test: Estimate whether the mechanism changes levels, growth rates, expectations or risk premia.
- **Event/current:** define the new information, contemporaneous expectation and first observable mechanism; verify: Separate a persistent state from a temporary impulse.
- Use the full inheritance and conflict standard in [[78 Phase 01 Institutional Fundamental Foundations Canon/06 Multihorizon Synthesis and Institutional Conclusions/Horizon Inheritance, Conflict Resolution and Double-Counting Control]].

## Failure modes and red-team tests

- Using “long term” to avoid specifying an expiry date.
- Treating an event surprise as a structural regime change without mechanism evidence.
- Forcing a structural thesis to explain every daily move.
- Ignoring that information arrives at different frequencies.
- Combining horizon scores whose variables overlap and are counted twice.
