# Fundamental Conclusion Taxonomy, Confidence, Invalidation and Expiry

> [!abstract] Canonical thesis
> Institutional research must end in a standardized conclusion that distinguishes state, valuation, scenario asymmetry, evidence quality and actionability. Confidence is calibrated support for a defined claim, not rhetorical force.

## Analytical intuition

“Bullish” hides too much. A better conclusion says the state is improving, expectations are low, the horizon is six months, evidence is moderate, the main risk is policy tightening and the view expires after the next two releases.

## Institutional definitions and distinctions

| Term or distinction | Precise meaning |
|---|---|
| State conclusion | Assessment of the underlying economic or financial condition. |
| Expectation-gap conclusion | Difference between research distribution and priced or consensus beliefs. |
| Valuation conclusion | Assessment relative to a defined payoff and required return. |
| Confidence | Calibrated degree of support for a precise proposition. |
| Invalidation | Evidence that materially contradicts the mechanism or assumptions. |
| Expiry | Date or information event after which the conclusion requires full re-underwriting. |

## Mechanism map

1. Separate state, expectation, valuation and risk conclusions.
2. Specify horizon and target proposition.
3. Grade evidence quality, model agreement and ambiguity.
4. List leading invalidators and updates.
5. Assign calendar and event expiry.
6. Use “insufficient evidence” when the proposition is underidentified.

## Formal structure and notation

### Confidence decomposition

$$
Confidence=f(EvidenceQuality,Identification,ModelAgreement,Stability,Unknowns)
$$

This is a governance decomposition, not a universal numerical formula.

### Log-odds update

$$
\log\frac{p(H\mid D)}{1-p(H\mid D)}=\log\frac{p(H)}{1-p(H)}+\log BF_{10}
$$

Bayesian updating clarifies how evidence changes a specified proposition when likelihoods are defensible.

## Competing interpretations and adversarial synthesis

| View | What it explains | Where it can fail |
|---|---|---|
| Numeric confidence | Supports calibration and aggregation. | Can imply precision unsupported by evidence. |
| Ordinal confidence | Easier to govern under ambiguity. | Harder to score and compare. |
| No-confidence narrative | Preserves nuance. | Encourages unfalsifiable rhetoric. |

## Historical and institutional cases

### Central-bank path conclusion

Confidence may be high about near-term policy but lower about terminal rates and transmission.

### Commodity balance conclusion

Physical evidence can be strong while demand elasticity and policy response remain uncertain.

## Multihorizon interpretation

- **Structural/secular:** identify persistent institutions, stocks or constraints governing **Fundamental Conclusion Taxonomy, Confidence, Invalidation and Expiry**; begin with: Separate state, expectation, valuation and risk conclusions.
- **Cyclical:** determine how the mechanism changes across expansion, slowdown, recession, recovery, inflation and disinflation; interrogate: Specify horizon and target proposition.
- **Tactical/multi-week:** identify which expectation, valuation or policy assumption is vulnerable; test: Grade evidence quality, model agreement and ambiguity.
- **Event/current:** define the new information, contemporaneous expectation and first observable mechanism; verify: List leading invalidators and updates.
- Use the full inheritance and conflict standard in [[78 Phase 01 Institutional Fundamental Foundations Canon/06 Multihorizon Synthesis and Institutional Conclusions/Horizon Inheritance, Conflict Resolution and Double-Counting Control]].

## Failure modes and red-team tests

- One confidence score for several propositions.
- Invalidation based only on adverse outcome.
- Expiry dates extended without re-underwriting.
- Using confidence as position size or certainty synonym.
- Failing to admit insufficient evidence.
