# Regime Conditioning, Transition Risk and Nonlinear Fundamental Relationships

> [!abstract] Canonical thesis
> Fundamental relationships are conditional on regime, constraints and policy response. Nonlinearity, thresholds and transition uncertainty must be treated as first-class research objects rather than post-hoc excuses.

## Beginner intuition

A wage increase during weak demand may support consumption; the same increase during an inflation credibility crisis may tighten policy. Context changes the effect.

## Institutional definitions and distinctions

| Term or distinction | Precise meaning |
|---|---|
| Regime | A recurring configuration of state, policy, behavior and constraints with distinct relationships. |
| Transition risk | Uncertainty about movement from one regime to another. |
| Threshold effect | A response that changes materially after a variable crosses a boundary. |
| State dependence | Variation of a causal or predictive relationship with underlying conditions. |
| Path dependence | Outcome dependence on sequence and history, not only current levels. |

## Mechanism map

1. Define regimes using economic mechanisms, not labels alone.
2. Identify observable and latent state indicators.
3. Estimate or narratively bracket transition probabilities.
4. Test whether sensitivities differ across regimes.
5. Model policy and balance-sheet feedback near thresholds.
6. Preserve multiple-regime scenarios when classification is uncertain.

## Formal structure and notation

### Regime-conditioned response

$$
\frac{\partial Y}{\partial X}=\beta_{S_t},\qquad S_t\in\{1,\ldots,K\}
$$

The effect is indexed by regime; the regime may itself be uncertain.

### Transition matrix

$$
P_{ij}=Pr(S_{t+1}=j\mid S_t=i,\mathcal I_t)
$$

Transition probabilities summarize state movement but do not explain the mechanism by themselves.

## Competing interpretations and adversarial synthesis

| View | What it explains | Where it can fail |
|---|---|---|
| Discrete regimes | Clear states with distinct parameters. | Reality may change continuously and classification can be unstable. |
| Continuous nonlinearities | Relationships vary smoothly with state variables. | Interpretation can be less intuitive and data demanding. |
| Institutional narratives | Regimes are defined by policy and balance-sheet architecture. | Narrative boundaries require disciplined evidence. |

## Historical and institutional cases

### Phillips-curve instability

Inflation-slack relationships change with expectations, supply shocks and credibility.

### Financial accelerator

Balance-sheet constraints are mild in normal conditions and powerful near funding stress.

## Failure modes and red-team tests

- Defining regimes after outcomes.
- Using too many states for the available evidence.
- Treating transition probability as certainty.
- Ignoring policy response that changes the regime.
- Assuming nonlinearity whenever a linear model fails.
