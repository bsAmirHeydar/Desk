# Information Half-Life, Shock Decay, Persistence and Thesis Expiry

> [!abstract] Canonical thesis
> Every information shock and research conclusion has an empirical or judgmental persistence profile. Expiry is determined by mechanism, subsequent evidence and state transition, not by attachment to a narrative.

## Analytical intuition

Some news matters for minutes, some changes forecasts for quarters, and some permanently alters a balance sheet. A disciplined analyst asks how fast the effect should decay and what new information would replace it.

## Institutional definitions and distinctions

| Term or distinction | Precise meaning |
|---|---|
| Information half-life | Time required for the incremental explanatory or forecast contribution of information to fall by half. |
| Persistence | Degree to which a state or shock carries into future periods. |
| Absorption | Process by which expectations, contracts and behavior incorporate information. |
| Thesis expiry | Point at which the original information set is too stale to support the conclusion. |
| Permanent component | Change expected to affect the long-run level or distribution rather than temporarily displace it. |

## Mechanism map

1. Classify the shock as measurement news, policy news, cash-flow news, balance-sheet change or physical constraint.
2. Map contractual and behavioral lags.
3. Identify offsetting mechanisms and mean reversion.
4. Estimate or bracket decay using historical analogues.
5. Update after each material release or institutional action.
6. Expire the thesis when mechanism conditions fail or the information advantage disappears.

## Formal structure and notation

### Exponential decay benchmark

$$
Impact(h)=Impact_0e^{-\lambda h},\qquad HalfLife=\frac{\ln 2}{\lambda}
$$

This is a benchmark, not a law; many shocks have delayed, nonlinear or state-dependent responses.

### Persistent process

$$
x_t=\rho x_{t-1}+\varepsilon_t,\qquad HalfLife=\frac{\ln(0.5)}{\ln|\rho|}
$$

Estimated persistence is sensitive to sample, breaks, aggregation and measurement error.

## Competing interpretations and adversarial synthesis

| View | What it explains | Where it can fail |
|---|---|---|
| Efficient absorption | Public information is incorporated rapidly. | Institutional frictions, disagreement and slow capital can delay adjustment. |
| Underreaction | Beliefs and positions adjust gradually. | Apparent persistence may reflect overlapping news. |
| Reflexive amplification | Initial response changes fundamentals and extends the effect. | Feedback may reverse once constraints or policy responses activate. |

## Historical and institutional cases

### Oil supply outage

Impact depends on duration, spare capacity, inventories and policy response; the same headline can have very different half-lives.

### Earnings guidance revision

A durable change in expected margins can persist across quarters, while an isolated headline surprise may decay quickly.

## Failure modes and red-team tests

- Assigning a fixed half-life across regimes.
- Estimating decay with revised data or overlapping events.
- Calling a thesis stale only after losses appear.
- Ignoring delayed transmission.
- Confusing persistent prices with persistent fundamental information.
