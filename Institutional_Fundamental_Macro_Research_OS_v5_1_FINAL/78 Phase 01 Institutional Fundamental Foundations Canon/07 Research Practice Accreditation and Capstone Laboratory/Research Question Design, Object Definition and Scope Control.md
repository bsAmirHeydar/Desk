# Research Question Design, Object Definition and Scope Control

> [!abstract] Canonical thesis
> A research project is only as rigorous as its question. Institutional work begins with a precisely scoped object, horizon, population, estimand, information set and decision relevance.

## Beginner intuition

“What happens to markets?” is too broad. “How does an unexpected policy-path revision affect real yields and long-duration equity valuation over one day and one month, conditional on inflation regime?” is researchable.

## Institutional definitions and distinctions

| Term or distinction | Precise meaning |
|---|---|
| Research object | Economic, financial, institutional or physical process being studied. |
| Unit of analysis | Country, firm, household, contract, sector, event or time interval. |
| Estimand | Precise quantity or distribution the research seeks to learn. |
| Scope condition | Boundary defining where the conclusion is intended to apply. |
| Exclusion | Explicitly out-of-scope mechanisms, assets, periods or decisions. |

## Mechanism map

1. Translate a broad concern into a falsifiable question.
2. Define outcome, exposure, unit, horizon and comparison.
3. State the information set and measurement conventions.
4. List assumptions and excluded mechanisms.
5. Connect the question to a decision or knowledge gap.
6. Predefine what would count as an inconclusive result.

## Formal structure and notation

### Research tuple

$$
Q=(Object,Unit,Horizon,InformationSet,Estimand,Scope,Loss)
$$

The tuple prevents silent changes in the question as evidence accumulates.

## Competing interpretations and adversarial synthesis

| View | What it explains | Where it can fail |
|---|---|---|
| Narrow identification | Prioritizes credible answers to limited questions. | Can miss system interaction. |
| Systems inquiry | Studies broad feedback and institutions. | Can become weakly falsifiable. |
| Sequential design | Begins narrow and integrates results hierarchically. | Requires disciplined scope governance. |

## Historical and institutional cases

### Fiscal multiplier question

Effects differ by slack, financing, monetary response, openness and horizon; “the multiplier” is not one universal object.

### Gold driver question

Real yields, dollar, reserve demand and risk premia require separate estimands and joint synthesis.

## Multihorizon interpretation

- **Structural/secular:** identify persistent institutions, stocks or constraints governing **Research Question Design, Object Definition and Scope Control**; begin with: Translate a broad concern into a falsifiable question.
- **Cyclical:** determine how the mechanism changes across expansion, slowdown, recession, recovery, inflation and disinflation; interrogate: Define outcome, exposure, unit, horizon and comparison.
- **Tactical/multi-week:** identify which expectation, valuation or policy assumption is vulnerable; test: State the information set and measurement conventions.
- **Event/current:** define the new information, contemporaneous expectation and first observable mechanism; verify: List assumptions and excluded mechanisms.
- Use the full inheritance and conflict standard in [[78 Phase 01 Institutional Fundamental Foundations Canon/06 Multihorizon Synthesis and Institutional Conclusions/Horizon Inheritance, Conflict Resolution and Double-Counting Control]].

## Failure modes and red-team tests

- Research question changes after seeing results.
- Undefined benchmark or comparison.
- Mixing explanation, forecast and valuation in one claim.
- No exclusion boundary.
- No acceptable inconclusive outcome.

2. Define scope for a cross-country inflation comparison.
3. Design an estimand for a policy information shock.
