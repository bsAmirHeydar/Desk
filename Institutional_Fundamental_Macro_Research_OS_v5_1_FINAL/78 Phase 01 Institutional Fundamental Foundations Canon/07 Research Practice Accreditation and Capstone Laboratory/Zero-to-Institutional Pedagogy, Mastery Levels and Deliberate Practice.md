# Zero-to-Institutional Pedagogy, Mastery Levels and Deliberate Practice

> [!abstract] Canonical thesis
> A canon is complete only if a beginner can enter without hidden prerequisites and progress to formal, historical and synthesis mastery. Learning design must separate recognition, explanation, application, critique and original synthesis.

## Beginner intuition

Reading creates familiarity, not necessarily skill. Mastery requires explaining concepts, solving structured problems, reconstructing cases and defending a conclusion against rivals.

## Institutional definitions and distinctions

| Term or distinction | Precise meaning |
|---|---|
| Prerequisite graph | Map of concepts required before a topic. |
| Mastery level | Observable capability from recall to original synthesis. |
| Deliberate practice | Targeted work on weak subskills with feedback. |
| Transfer task | Application to an unfamiliar domain or case. |
| Accreditation evidence | Work product demonstrating capability, not attendance. |

## Mechanism map

1. Define prerequisite sequence.
2. Teach intuition before formalism without replacing it.
3. Use retrieval, exercises and cases.
4. Provide feedback and error taxonomy.
5. Require transfer across countries and assets.
6. Re-accredit after time and new evidence.

## Formal structure and notation

### Learning progression

$$
Mastery=Knowledge+Retrieval+Application+Critique+Transfer
$$

No single component is sufficient for institutional competence.

## Competing interpretations and adversarial synthesis

| View | What it explains | Where it can fail |
|---|---|---|
| Lecture-first | Efficient content delivery. | Passive familiarity and illusion of competence. |
| Problem-first | Builds active reasoning. | Can overwhelm without scaffolding. |
| Spiral curriculum | Revisits concepts at increasing depth. | Requires careful duplication control. |

## Historical and institutional cases

### Yield-curve learning

Moves from definitions to decomposition, historical regimes and cross-country application.

### Inflation learning

Requires measurement, competing theories, cases and current synthesis.

## Failure modes and red-team tests

- Hidden math or accounting prerequisites.
- Multiple-choice recall as final accreditation.
- Examples only from one country or regime.
- No feedback on reasoning errors.
- No update after models or institutions change.
