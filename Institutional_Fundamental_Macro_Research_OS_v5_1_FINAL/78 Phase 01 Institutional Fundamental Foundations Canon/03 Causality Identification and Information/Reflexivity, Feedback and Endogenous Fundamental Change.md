# Reflexivity, Feedback and Endogenous Fundamental Change

> [!abstract] Canonical thesis
> Financial prices can alter the fundamentals they appear to reflect by changing collateral, financing, confidence, investment, policy and distribution. Reflexivity is therefore a causal feedback mechanism, not a license for circular storytelling.

## Analytical intuition

Rising house prices can improve collateral, expand credit and support construction, which can push prices higher. Falling prices can reverse the loop. The price is both an outcome and an input.

## Institutional definitions and distinctions

| Term or distinction | Precise meaning |
|---|---|
| Reflexive loop | A feedback where beliefs and prices alter behavior and state, which then validates or reverses the beliefs. |
| Financial accelerator | Balance-sheet changes amplify real activity and credit conditions. |
| Self-fulfilling equilibrium | Expectations coordinate behavior so the anticipated outcome occurs among multiple feasible equilibria. |
| Stabilizing feedback | A response that counteracts deviation. |
| Destabilizing feedback | A response that reinforces deviation. |

## Mechanism map

1. Identify how price or belief enters budgets, collateral, regulation or policy.
2. Specify the feedback sign and lag.
3. Separate transient valuation effects from persistent state change.
4. Identify nonlinear thresholds and margin constraints.
5. Model policy and institutional stabilizers.
6. Define observations that would break the loop.

## Formal structure and notation

### Feedback system

$$
S_{t+1}=f(S_t,P_t,u_t),\qquad P_t=g(\mathbb E_t[S_{t+1}],constraints_t)
$$

State affects price while price affects future state. Stability depends on derivatives, lags and constraints.

### Local stability

$$
\left|\frac{\partial f}{\partial P}\frac{\partial g}{\partial S}\right|<1
$$

A simplified feedback product below one suggests local damping; above one can imply amplification, though real systems are nonlinear and multidimensional.

## Competing interpretations and adversarial synthesis

| View | What it explains | Where it can fail |
|---|---|---|
| Fundamental-anchor view | Prices ultimately converge to exogenous cash-flow fundamentals. | Underweights financing and policy feedback. |
| Reflexivity view | Beliefs and prices can create fundamentals. | Can become circular if no mechanism or stopping condition is specified. |
| Multiple-equilibrium view | Institutions and coordination determine realized equilibrium. | Hard to identify empirically and vulnerable to after-the-fact labeling. |

## Historical and institutional cases

### Housing-credit boom

Collateral values, lending standards, construction and household borrowing reinforced one another until funding and credit quality broke the loop.

### Sovereign spread spiral

Higher spreads worsen debt dynamics and bank balance sheets, which can justify higher spreads unless policy backstops change the equilibrium.

## Failure modes and red-team tests

- Calling any correlation reflexivity.
- Ignoring stabilizing policy responses.
- Assuming a positive loop has no financing or valuation limit.
- Using price movement as both evidence and conclusion.
- Failing to define the state variable changed by price.
