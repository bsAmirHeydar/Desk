# Information Shocks, News, Surprises and Expectation Revision

> [!abstract] Canonical thesis
> An information event is causal only relative to a prior information set. The relevant innovation is not the headline value but the vector of news across components, revisions, guidance and institutional interpretation compared with the priced distribution.

## Beginner intuition

A data release can be above consensus but still have little effect if the details are weak or the surprise was already anticipated. A central-bank decision can leave the rate unchanged but change expectations through guidance.

## Institutional definitions and distinctions

| Term or distinction | Precise meaning |
|---|---|
| News | Information not contained in the prior information set. |
| Surprise | Observed value minus a defined expectation, with distribution and timestamp. |
| Revision news | Information conveyed by changes to prior observations. |
| Information shock | A change in beliefs due to newly revealed information, separate from an action shock. |
| Priced baseline | The distribution of outcomes and reactions embedded before the event. |

## Mechanism map

1. Reconstruct the pre-event information set.
2. Represent consensus as a distribution, not only a median.
3. Decompose headline, components and revisions.
4. Separate action, guidance and information effects.
5. Observe cross-market response in instruments tied to the mechanism.
6. Estimate whether belief revision persists or reverses as details are processed.

## Formal structure and notation

### Standardized surprise

$$
S_t=\frac{Actual_t-Expected_t}{\sigma(Forecast\ Error)}
$$

Standardization supports comparison but depends on stable error distributions and a credible expectation measure.

### Bayesian news update

$$
Posterior\ Odds=Prior\ Odds\times Likelihood\ Ratio
$$

The same observation causes different belief changes when prior beliefs and model likelihoods differ.

## Competing interpretations and adversarial synthesis

| View | What it explains | Where it can fail |
|---|---|---|
| Consensus benchmark | Widely available and operational. | Median forecasts hide dispersion, stale estimates and strategic rounding. |
| Market-implied benchmark | Reflects tradable expectations. | Contains premia, hedging and liquidity effects. |
| Model-based benchmark | Offers coherent conditional forecasts. | May lag regime changes and omit private information. |

## Historical and institutional cases

### Payroll release

Headline jobs, household employment, unemployment, wages, hours and revisions can send different signals; policy interpretation depends on the vector.

### Policy press conference

The decision, statement, projections and answers arrive sequentially, producing multiple information shocks rather than one timestamp.

## Failure modes and red-team tests

- Using final consensus after the release.
- Ignoring forecast dispersion and update timestamps.
- Treating all components as independent.
- Assuming the first price move is the final belief update.
- Confusing large movement with large fundamental news.
