# External Validity, Transportability and Regime Dependence

> [!abstract] Canonical thesis
> An effect identified in one population, institution and regime does not automatically transport elsewhere. Institutional research must state the mechanisms and moderators required for generalization.

## Beginner intuition

A rate increase can affect a country with floating-rate mortgages very differently from a country with long fixed-rate loans. The same estimated effect cannot be copied without examining the transmission structure.

## Institutional definitions and distinctions

| Term or distinction | Precise meaning |
|---|---|
| Internal validity | Credibility of the causal estimate in the studied setting. |
| External validity | Relevance of the estimate to another population, time or institution. |
| Transportability | Formal conditions for using source-setting evidence in a target setting. |
| Moderator | A variable that changes effect magnitude or sign. |
| General-equilibrium effect | Indirect effects through prices, policy, networks and aggregate constraints. |

## Mechanism map

1. List institutional and state conditions in the source setting.
2. Identify causal mechanisms and moderators.
3. Compare their distribution in the target setting.
4. Adjust or bound effects rather than copying estimates.
5. Account for equilibrium spillovers and policy response.
6. Validate against target-setting historical evidence.

## Formal structure and notation

### Conditional effect

$$
\tau(x,z)=\mathbb E[Y(1)-Y(0)\mid X=x,Z=z]
$$

Effects can vary with observed characteristics x and regime z; average estimates conceal this heterogeneity.

### Transport weighting

$$
\mathbb E_T[Y]=\mathbb E_S\!\left[\frac{p_T(X)}{p_S(X)}Y\right]
$$

Reweighting addresses observed distribution differences under overlap and conditional transportability, not unobserved institutional change.

## Competing interpretations and adversarial synthesis

| View | What it explains | Where it can fail |
|---|---|---|
| Universal-parameter models | Seek stable deep parameters. | Deep parameters may change with policy and institutions. |
| Local empirical estimates | Prioritize credible setting-specific effects. | Offer limited guidance for new regimes. |
| Mechanism-based transport | Generalizes through causal channels and moderators. | Requires strong knowledge of the mechanism. |

## Historical and institutional cases

### Monetary transmission across mortgage systems

Refinancing structure, debt maturity and bank competition change household cash-flow response.

### Capital-flow controls

Effects depend on enforcement, substitution channels, reserve adequacy and global risk regime.

## Failure modes and red-team tests

- Copying U.S. elasticities into another country.
- Assuming an old crisis estimate applies after regulation changes.
- Ignoring general-equilibrium feedback.
- Using observed reweighting to claim full transport.
- Selecting analogues by outcome similarity rather than mechanism.
