# Mediation, Transmission Chains and Mechanism Evidence

> [!abstract] Canonical thesis
> A causal conclusion is more useful when the transmission chain is identified: which intermediate balance sheets, prices, behaviors and constraints carry the effect, with what lag and under which regime.

## Analytical intuition

Knowing that a policy affected output is not enough for institutional interpretation. Did it work through mortgage rates, bank lending, exchange rates, confidence, wealth or fiscal interaction? Different channels imply different persistence and cross-asset effects.

## Institutional definitions and distinctions

| Term or distinction | Precise meaning |
|---|---|
| Mediator | An intermediate variable on the causal pathway. |
| Total effect | All pathways from intervention to outcome. |
| Direct effect | Effect not operating through a specified mediator, under a defined intervention concept. |
| Transmission lag | Time distribution between initial change and intermediate or final effects. |
| Mechanism evidence | Evidence that discriminates among pathways, not merely confirms the final association. |

## Mechanism map

1. List candidate channels before examining outcomes.
2. Define observable implications for each channel.
3. Measure timing and sequence.
4. Avoid conditioning away the mechanism when estimating total effects.
5. Use cross-sectional heterogeneity tied to channel exposure.
6. Test whether channel strength changes across regimes and balance-sheet states.

## Formal structure and notation

### Path decomposition skeleton

$$
Total\ Effect=Direct\ Effect+\sum_k Mediated\ Effect_k
$$

This additive representation is conceptual; nonlinear interactions and treatment-induced confounding can prevent simple decomposition.

### Distributed lag

$$
Y_t=\alpha+\sum_{h=0}^{H}\beta_h X_{t-h}+u_t
$$

Lag coefficients describe timing only under identification; they are not automatically structural transmission parameters.

## Competing interpretations and adversarial synthesis

| View | What it explains | Where it can fail |
|---|---|---|
| Reduced-form sufficiency | A credible total effect is enough for policy evaluation. | Offers limited portability when institutions or regimes change. |
| Mechanism-first analysis | Improves interpretation and external validity. | Mechanism tests can add assumptions and post-treatment bias. |
| Structural transmission models | Represent channels jointly. | Can fit narratives with weak identification. |

## Historical and institutional cases

### Monetary tightening

Transmission may run through mortgages, bank credit, exchange rates, asset valuation and expectations, with different lags by country.

### Oil supply shock

Consumer income, production costs, inflation expectations, monetary response and exporter income form competing and interacting channels.

## Multihorizon interpretation

- **Structural/secular:** identify persistent institutions, stocks or constraints governing **Mediation, Transmission Chains and Mechanism Evidence**; begin with: List candidate channels before examining outcomes.
- **Cyclical:** determine how the mechanism changes across expansion, slowdown, recession, recovery, inflation and disinflation; interrogate: Define observable implications for each channel.
- **Tactical/multi-week:** identify which expectation, valuation or policy assumption is vulnerable; test: Measure timing and sequence.
- **Event/current:** define the new information, contemporaneous expectation and first observable mechanism; verify: Avoid conditioning away the mechanism when estimating total effects.

## Failure modes and red-team tests

- Using intermediate correlation as proof of mediation.
- Ignoring channel interactions.
- Assuming the same lag in every regime.
- Conditioning on a mediator and calling the coefficient total effect.
- Describing a mechanism without a discriminating observation.
