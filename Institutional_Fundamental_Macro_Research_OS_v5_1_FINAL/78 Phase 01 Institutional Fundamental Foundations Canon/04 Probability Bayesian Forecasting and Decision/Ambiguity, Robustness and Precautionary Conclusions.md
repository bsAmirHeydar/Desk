# Ambiguity, Robustness and Precautionary Conclusions

> [!abstract] Canonical thesis
> When probabilities or models are weakly identified, institutional discipline requires ranges, robustness, precaution and explicit insufficient-evidence conclusions rather than fabricated precision.

## Beginner intuition

Sometimes there is not enough evidence to say 60 versus 40. You may only know that several models are plausible and the downside is large. The honest answer can be a range, a robust conclusion or no conclusion.

## Institutional definitions and distinctions

| Term or distinction | Precise meaning |
|---|---|
| Ambiguity set | Collection of distributions or models considered plausible. |
| Robust conclusion | A conclusion that survives meaningful changes in assumptions or models. |
| Precautionary principle | Greater weight on avoiding severe irreversible harm under deep uncertainty. |
| Minimax regret | Choose the action minimizing worst-case regret relative to the best action in each state. |
| Insufficient evidence | A valid output when available evidence cannot discriminate enough for the stated question. |

## Mechanism map

1. Identify which uncertainty is reducible and which is structural.
2. Construct plausible model and parameter sets.
3. Test conclusion sensitivity across the set.
4. Evaluate tail and irreversible outcomes.
5. Use ranges or partial identification when point probabilities are unjustified.
6. State conditions for resolving insufficient evidence.

## Formal structure and notation

### Minimax regret

$$
a^*=\arg\min_a\max_{P\in\mathcal P}[V^*(P)-V(a,P)]
$$

The rule limits worst-case opportunity loss across plausible models instead of optimizing one estimated model.

### Partial-identification interval

$$
\theta\in[\underline\theta(A),\overline\theta(A)]
$$

Under assumption set A, evidence may identify a range rather than a point. Wider honest bounds can be more informative than false precision.

## Competing interpretations and adversarial synthesis

| View | What it explains | Where it can fail |
|---|---|---|
| Single best model | Supports clear probabilities and decisions. | Understates model uncertainty. |
| Robust control | Protects against misspecification. | Can be conservative and sensitive to ambiguity-set design. |
| Adaptive learning | Takes reversible actions while collecting evidence. | Delay can be costly and experiments may alter the system. |

## Historical and institutional cases

### Emerging pathogen policy

Unknown transmission, severity and behavior justify scenario ranges and reversible measures rather than narrow historical estimates.

### Sovereign convertibility risk

Legal and political discontinuities make precise historical probabilities fragile; contract and scenario analysis dominate.

## Failure modes and red-team tests

- Calling ambiguity “50/50.”
- Hiding model disagreement inside an average.
- Using worst-case reasoning without defining plausibility.
- Declaring insufficient evidence to avoid accountable judgment.
- Ignoring the option value of waiting or staged action.
