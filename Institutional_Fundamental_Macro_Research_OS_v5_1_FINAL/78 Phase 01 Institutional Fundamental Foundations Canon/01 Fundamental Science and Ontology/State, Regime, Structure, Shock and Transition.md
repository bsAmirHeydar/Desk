# State, Regime, Structure, Shock and Transition

> [!abstract] Canonical thesis
> State, regime, structure and shock are not interchangeable. Structure describes persistent constraints; state describes current latent conditions; regime describes a configuration of relationships and policy behavior; shock describes new information or disturbance; transition describes movement between states or regimes.

## Analytical intuition

A house has a structure, a current temperature, a heating regime and occasional shocks such as an open window. The economy is similar. Demographics and institutions are structural. Growth and inflation are states. A policy framework can define a regime. A war or data surprise can be a shock.

## Institutional definitions and distinctions

| Term or distinction | Precise meaning |
|---|---|
| Structure | Slow-moving architecture of institutions, technology, demographics, production, legal rules and balance sheets. |
| State | The current but partly unobserved condition summarized by variables such as demand momentum, inflation pressure and financial stress. |
| Regime | A period in which behavioral relationships, policy responses and risk transmission are sufficiently stable to be modeled together. |
| Shock | An innovation relative to the prior information set; it may be fundamental, informational, policy-created or financial. |
| Transition | A probabilistic change in state or regime, often recognized only with delay. |

## Mechanism map

1. Separate slow priors from fast observations.
2. Define state variables and observable proxies.
3. Specify regime-dependent relationships and policy reaction functions.
4. Classify shocks by source, persistence, sign, distribution and transmission.
5. Estimate whether new evidence changes the state within the current regime or changes the regime itself.
6. Document transition uncertainty and alternative labels.

## Formal structure and notation

### Latent-state transition

$$
S_t=A_{z_t}S_{t-1}+B_{z_t}u_t+\eta_t
$$

State dynamics can depend on regime z. A shock u has effects conditioned on the current configuration rather than a universal coefficient.

### Regime transition probability

$$
\Pr(z_t=j\mid z_{t-1}=i,\mathcal{I}_t)=p_{ij,t}
$$

Transition probabilities may vary with information, vulnerabilities and policy choices; they are not immutable frequencies.

## Competing interpretations and adversarial synthesis

| View | What it explains | Where it can fail |
|---|---|---|
| Discrete-regime models | Make structural breaks and nonlinear behavior explicit. | May impose artificial boundaries on continuous change. |
| Continuous-state models | Represent gradual evolution and uncertainty smoothly. | Can average across genuinely different policy or crisis regimes. |
| Narrative regime labels | Communicate complex configurations efficiently. | Risk becoming unfalsifiable labels applied after outcomes are known. |

## Historical and institutional cases

### Great Moderation

Lower macro volatility was interpreted as a durable regime, but leverage and financial-sector vulnerabilities accumulated outside the standard state vector.

### Pandemic transition

The economy moved through shutdown, transfer-supported demand, reopening bottlenecks and tightening. A single “recession/recovery” label could not represent changing mechanisms.

## Multihorizon interpretation

- **Structural/secular:** identify persistent institutions, stocks or constraints governing **State, Regime, Structure, Shock and Transition**; begin with: Separate slow priors from fast observations.
- **Cyclical:** determine how the mechanism changes across expansion, slowdown, recession, recovery, inflation and disinflation; interrogate: Define state variables and observable proxies.
- **Tactical/multi-week:** identify which expectation, valuation or policy assumption is vulnerable; test: Specify regime-dependent relationships and policy reaction functions.
- **Event/current:** define the new information, contemporaneous expectation and first observable mechanism; verify: Classify shocks by source, persistence, sign, distribution and transmission.

## Failure modes and red-team tests

- Declaring a new regime after a single surprising observation.
- Using regime labels without observable membership criteria.
- Assuming structural trends determine the sign of every short-horizon movement.
- Ignoring policy endogeneity: policy responds to the same state being measured.
- Backfilling regime boundaries with future information.
