# Boundary Cases — What Is and Is Not Fundamental Knowledge

> [!abstract] Canonical thesis
> A fundamental-only canon must define its boundary by purpose and mechanism. Price, positioning and liquidity may be legitimate evidence about expectations, constraints and valuation, but they cannot enter as pattern-based signals or unexplained directional rules.

## Beginner intuition

The same dataset can be used in two different ways. An option surface can reveal the market’s priced distribution and demand for protection; that is fundamental market-implied information. Using a visual shape as a standalone entry rule belongs outside this canon.

## Institutional definitions and distinctions

| Term or distinction | Precise meaning |
|---|---|
| Fundamental use of price | Using prices to infer expectations, valuation, risk premia, liquidity, collateral or realized response to information. |
| Non-fundamental use | Using price geometry, indicator thresholds or pattern labels without an economic, contractual or institutional mechanism. |
| Positioning evidence | Data about inventory, leverage, concentration or mandates used to understand forced behavior and payoff asymmetry. |
| Microstructure evidence | Information about auctions, dealer balance sheets, funding and transaction mechanisms. |

## Mechanism map

1. State the economic question for every price-derived variable.
2. Define the model translating price into expectation or constraint.
3. Separate observed market data from inferred beliefs.
4. Require an institutional mechanism for positioning and flow variables.
5. Exclude pattern-based rules and unexplained thresholds.
6. Document when price-derived evidence is contaminated by illiquidity or market design.

## Formal structure and notation

### Market-implied object

$$
Observed\ Price=Model(Expected\ Payoff,Premia,Constraints,Market\ Design)+Error
$$

The inversion from price to beliefs is model-dependent and potentially underidentified.

### Flow-impact distinction

$$
\Delta P_t=Fundamental\ Revaluation_t+Temporary\ Impact_t+Noise_t
$$

Short-run price change can mix information and impact. Fundamental research must not assume the decomposition is directly observed.

## Competing interpretations and adversarial synthesis

| View | What it explains | Where it can fail |
|---|---|---|
| Pure fundamental boundary | Excludes most market data to avoid circularity. | Loses information about expectations and institutional constraints. |
| Market-implied fundamental boundary | Includes derivatives, curves and flows when tied to a valuation or institutional model. | Can quietly reintroduce unsupported signal rules. |
| Behavioral-market boundary | Treats expectation errors and crowding as part of fundamentals. | Needs disciplined definitions to avoid labeling any move “sentiment.” |

## Historical and institutional cases

### Oil futures curve

Curve shape can encode inventories, convenience yield, financing and constraints; reading it requires physical and contractual context, not visual classification alone.

### Credit spread

Spread is evidence about expected loss, risk premium, liquidity and optionality. It is not a direct default probability without decomposition.

## Multihorizon interpretation

- **Structural/secular:** identify persistent institutions, stocks or constraints governing **Boundary Cases — What Is and Is Not Fundamental Knowledge**; begin with: State the economic question for every price-derived variable.
- **Cyclical:** determine how the mechanism changes across expansion, slowdown, recession, recovery, inflation and disinflation; interrogate: Define the model translating price into expectation or constraint.
- **Tactical/multi-week:** identify which expectation, valuation or policy assumption is vulnerable; test: Separate observed market data from inferred beliefs.
- **Event/current:** define the new information, contemporaneous expectation and first observable mechanism; verify: Require an institutional mechanism for positioning and flow variables.

## Failure modes and red-team tests

- Banning all price information and losing the priced baseline.
- Allowing any price-based rule by attaching vague economic language.
- Treating positioning estimates as observed facts.
- Confusing temporary market impact with new fundamental value.
- Using a boundary definition that changes whenever a favored method is challenged.
