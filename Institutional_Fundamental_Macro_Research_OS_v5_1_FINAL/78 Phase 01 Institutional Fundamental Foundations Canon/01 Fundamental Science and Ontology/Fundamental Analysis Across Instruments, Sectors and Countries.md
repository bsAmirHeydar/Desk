# Fundamental Analysis Across Instruments, Sectors and Countries

> [!abstract] Canonical thesis
> The same macro state maps differently into sovereign bonds, currencies, equities, credit and commodities because their payoff functions, legal claims, supply structures and investor bases differ. A universal directional rule is therefore inferior to instrument-specific transmission analysis.

## Analytical intuition

Lower growth can support government bonds through lower expected policy rates, hurt cyclical equities through weaker profits, strengthen or weaken a currency depending on relative policy and funding, and affect oil through demand expectations. One fact can create different outcomes.

## Institutional definitions and distinctions

| Term or distinction | Precise meaning |
|---|---|
| Instrument payoff map | A representation of contractual cash flows, optionality, default, currency and liquidity services. |
| Sector exposure | Sensitivity of revenues, costs, financing and balance sheets to macro and industry variables. |
| Country transmission | How institutions, currency regime, external balance, banking system and fiscal capacity alter a common shock. |
| Relative-value object | A comparison where common drivers are hedged or differenced to isolate a specific wedge. |

## Mechanism map

1. Start with the legal and economic payoff of the instrument.
2. Map direct cash-flow and balance-sheet exposures.
3. Map discount-rate, default, currency and liquidity channels.
4. Identify the marginal investor and relevant mandate constraints.
5. Condition the mapping on country institutions and regime.
6. Compare absolute and relative expressions without assuming equivalence.

## Formal structure and notation

### Equity return identity

$$
R\approx \Delta\ln(Earnings)+\Delta\ln(Multiple)+Dividend\ Yield
$$

Equity outcomes can be separated into cash-flow growth, valuation change and distributions, with interaction and composition effects.

### FX relative-price skeleton

$$
\Delta s_{t+1}=f(\Delta r^{rel},\ \Delta g^{rel},\ BOP,\ risk,\ funding,\ valuation)
$$

A currency is inherently relative; interest rates alone cannot represent external balances, risk premia and funding demand.

## Competing interpretations and adversarial synthesis

| View | What it explains | Where it can fail |
|---|---|---|
| Top-down allocation | Uses common macro states to compare broad assets. | Can overlook instrument-specific legal and microeconomic details. |
| Bottom-up security analysis | Builds from companies, contracts and physical assets. | Can miss common discount-rate and regime shifts. |
| Integrated cross-asset method | Combines top-down states with instrument payoff maps. | Requires more data and disciplined separation of shared versus idiosyncratic drivers. |

## Historical and institutional cases

### Global energy shock

Importers and exporters experience opposite terms-of-trade, fiscal and currency effects; energy producers and consumers within each equity market also diverge.

### Bank tightening cycle

Higher rates can raise bank asset yields yet damage deposit franchises, securities portfolios and credit quality. Country regulation and funding structure determine the result.

## Multihorizon interpretation

- **Structural/secular:** identify persistent institutions, stocks or constraints governing **Fundamental Analysis Across Instruments, Sectors and Countries**; begin with: Start with the legal and economic payoff of the instrument.
- **Cyclical:** determine how the mechanism changes across expansion, slowdown, recession, recovery, inflation and disinflation; interrogate: Map direct cash-flow and balance-sheet exposures.
- **Tactical/multi-week:** identify which expectation, valuation or policy assumption is vulnerable; test: Map discount-rate, default, currency and liquidity channels.
- **Event/current:** define the new information, contemporaneous expectation and first observable mechanism; verify: Identify the marginal investor and relevant mandate constraints.

## Failure modes and red-team tests

- Applying a U.S.-centric model to every country.
- Ignoring contract currency and legal seniority.
- Treating sector labels as homogeneous.
- Assuming a relative trade is neutral without factor decomposition.
- Ignoring the investor base and institutional demand for an instrument.

## Adversarial analytical checks and accreditation questions

1. Map a growth shock into sovereign bonds, bank equity, corporate credit, the currency and oil for an importer.
2. Compare the same shock in a reserve-currency issuer and a foreign-currency borrower.
3. Design a relative-value question that isolates inflation compensation from nominal duration.
