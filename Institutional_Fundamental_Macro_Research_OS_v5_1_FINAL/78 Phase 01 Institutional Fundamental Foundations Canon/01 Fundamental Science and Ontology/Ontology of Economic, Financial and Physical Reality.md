# Ontology of Economic, Financial and Physical Reality

> [!abstract] Canonical thesis
> A serious canon needs an explicit ontology: entities, stocks, flows, contracts, prices, expectations, institutions, constraints, events and states must be represented as different kinds of objects with different persistence and measurement properties.

## Analytical intuition

An ontology is a disciplined inventory of what exists in the system and how the pieces relate. A household deposit is an asset to the household and a liability to a bank. A wage is a flow. Government debt is a stock and a contract. Inflation is a rate of change in a constructed price index. Mixing these categories creates false explanations.

## Institutional definitions and distinctions

| Term or distinction | Precise meaning |
|---|---|
| Entity | A decision-making or legal unit: household, firm, bank, fund, government, central bank, foreign sector or multilateral institution. |
| Stock | A quantity measured at a point in time, such as debt, capital, inventories, reserves or net worth. |
| Flow | A quantity measured over an interval, such as income, spending, issuance, production or credit creation. |
| Contract | A rule-governed set of contingent claims, payment priorities, collateral rights and maturities. |
| Institution | A durable system of rules, enforcement, norms and authority that shapes feasible behavior. |
| Constraint | A budget, collateral, legal, political, technological, physical or risk limit. |

## Mechanism map

1. Map entities and sector boundaries.
2. Record every material stock on both sides of the relevant balance sheets.
3. Reconcile flows with changes in stocks, valuation effects and write-offs.
4. Specify contractual seniority, currency, maturity, collateral and optionality.
5. Distinguish measured variables from latent states and institutional judgments.
6. Track which constraints bind in normal conditions and under stress.

## Formal structure and notation

### Stock-flow reconciliation

$$
S_t-S_{t-1}=F_t+VG_t+OC_t
$$

The change in a stock equals net transactions or flows F, valuation gains and losses VG, and other changes OC such as write-offs, reclassifications or statistical discrepancies.

### Sectoral balance identity

$$
(S-I)+(T-G)+(M-X)=0
$$

Private net saving, government balance and foreign balance are jointly constrained by accounting. The identity does not by itself identify behavioral causality.

## Competing interpretations and adversarial synthesis

| View | What it explains | Where it can fail |
|---|---|---|
| Representative-agent simplification | Offers tractable aggregate models. | Erases distribution, institutional heterogeneity and balance-sheet chains. |
| Stock-flow-consistent ontology | Forces accounting coherence and sectoral linkage. | Can be descriptively complete yet weak on expectations or identification. |
| Network ontology | Captures counterparties, contagion and centrality. | Requires granular exposure data and can create false precision. |

## Historical and institutional cases

### Euro-area sovereign-bank loop

Banks held domestic sovereign debt while sovereigns backstopped banks. Entity and contract mapping revealed a feedback loop hidden by aggregate public-debt ratios.

### Commodity producer balance sheets

A fall in commodity prices affects export receipts, fiscal revenue, company cash flow, bank credit and currency funding through different but linked objects.

## Multihorizon interpretation

- **Structural/secular:** identify persistent institutions, stocks or constraints governing **Ontology of Economic, Financial and Physical Reality**; begin with: Map entities and sector boundaries.
- **Cyclical:** determine how the mechanism changes across expansion, slowdown, recession, recovery, inflation and disinflation; interrogate: Record every material stock on both sides of the relevant balance sheets.
- **Tactical/multi-week:** identify which expectation, valuation or policy assumption is vulnerable; test: Reconcile flows with changes in stocks, valuation effects and write-offs.
- **Event/current:** define the new information, contemporaneous expectation and first observable mechanism; verify: Specify contractual seniority, currency, maturity, collateral and optionality.
- Use the full inheritance and conflict standard in [[78 Phase 01 Institutional Fundamental Foundations Canon/06 Multihorizon Synthesis and Institutional Conclusions/Horizon Inheritance, Conflict Resolution and Double-Counting Control]].

## Failure modes and red-team tests

- Treating a flow as if it were a stock.
- Netting positions that differ in currency, maturity or seniority.
- Inferring causality from an accounting identity.
- Ignoring off-balance-sheet contracts and contingent liabilities.
- Using country or sector labels that hide heterogeneous entities.

## Annotated source canon

- **United Nations System of National Accounts.** Authoritative ontology and accounting framework for production, income, capital and balance sheets.
- **IMF Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual.** Definitions for cross-border stocks, flows, valuation changes and functional categories.
- **Federal Reserve Financial Accounts of the United States.** Sectoral balance sheets and flow-of-funds implementation.
- **Wynne Godley and Marc Lavoie, Monetary Economics.** Stock-flow-consistent modeling and sectoral interdependence.
