# What Fundamental Analysis Is — Object, Boundary and Institutional Purpose

> [!abstract] Canonical thesis
> Fundamental analysis is the disciplined study of real economic, financial, institutional and physical systems, the expectations formed about those systems, and the mechanisms through which changes in state or belief alter asset payoffs and relative valuations. It is not a synonym for news commentary, narrative confidence, or price-pattern interpretation.

## Analytical intuition

Imagine the economy and financial system as a network of households, firms, banks, governments, central banks and physical supply chains. Fundamental analysis asks what each part owns, owes, produces, consumes, earns, expects and can sustain. Market prices matter because they reveal expectations and valuation, but price alone does not define the fundamental object.

## Institutional definitions and distinctions

| Term or distinction | Precise meaning |
|---|---|
| Economic state | A probability distribution over latent and observed conditions such as growth, inflation, labor tightness, financial stress and fiscal capacity. |
| Fundamental driver | A causal or accounting variable capable of changing expected cash flows, discount rates, scarcity rents, default risk, currency value or balance-sheet resilience. |
| Market expectation | A belief distribution embedded in surveys, forecasts, curves, relative prices, positioning and institutional behavior. |
| Asset payoff | The distribution of cash flows, carry, principal recovery, scarcity value or monetary service produced by holding an asset. |
| Research conclusion | A bounded statement about state, uncertainty, valuation or scenario asymmetry; not an instruction detached from mandate and risk. |

## Mechanism map

1. Observe and define the system being studied before collecting indicators.
2. Distinguish the underlying state from noisy measurements and delayed publications.
3. Reconstruct what relevant institutions and investors expected before new information arrived.
4. Trace transmission through cash flows, discount rates, credit, currencies, physical balances and institutional constraints.
5. Compare the estimated payoff distribution with the price or relative valuation already offered.
6. Conclude with explicit uncertainty, horizon, invalidation conditions and unresolved questions.

## Formal structure and notation

### General research object

$$
\mathcal{R}_t=\{S_t,\ I_t,\ E_t,\ P_t,\ M,\ H,\ U_t\}
$$

The research object records state S, information I, expectations E, prices or valuations P, mechanisms M, horizon H and uncertainty U. Omitting any element invites a category error.

### Asset-value decomposition

$$
V_t=\mathbb{E}_t\!\left[\sum_{j=1}^{\infty} M_{t,t+j}CF_{t+j}\right]+\Omega_t
$$

Expected cash flows are discounted by a stochastic discount factor M. Omega represents non-cash-flow services such as liquidity, collateral utility, monetary demand or convenience yield.

## Competing interpretations and adversarial synthesis

| View | What it explains | Where it can fail |
|---|---|---|
| Intrinsic-value tradition | Anchors analysis in cash flows, assets and sustainable earning power. | Can underweight reflexivity, funding constraints and changing institutions. |
| Macro-state tradition | Explains common variation through growth, inflation, policy and liquidity. | Can become too aggregate for sector or security selection. |
| Market-implied tradition | Uses prices and derivatives to infer expectations and distributions. | Prices can include risk premia, constraints and technical flows, not pure beliefs. |
| Institutional-political tradition | Explains rules, power, credibility and distributional conflict. | Can become narrative-heavy without measurable implications. |

## Historical and institutional cases

### The 2008 crisis

A narrow reading of reported growth and corporate earnings missed mortgage-credit deterioration, bank leverage, collateral chains and maturity transformation. The case illustrates why the research object must include balance sheets and funding structure.

### The 2021–2022 inflation transition

A framework focused only on demand or only on supply was incomplete. Fiscal transfers, goods bottlenecks, labor reallocation, housing lags, energy and policy credibility interacted across horizons.

## Failure modes and red-team tests

- Calling any information associated with an asset “fundamental” without identifying a payoff channel.
- Using macro vocabulary to disguise an untestable directional opinion.
- Treating the latest published number as the true current state.
- Ignoring the distinction between absolute value, relative value and portfolio exposure.
- Assuming that a complete description automatically produces a useful forecast.

## Adversarial analytical checks and accreditation questions

1. Define the fundamental research object for a sovereign bond, a bank equity, gold and crude oil; show what changes across instruments.
2. Take a popular market narrative and list which claims are fundamental, which are expectation claims, and which are unsupported.
3. Explain why a price can rise while the underlying fundamental state deteriorates.

## Annotated source canon

- **Karl Popper, The Logic of Scientific Discovery.** Falsifiability, severe testing, and the difference between explanation and immunized narrative.
- **Thomas Kuhn, The Structure of Scientific Revolutions.** Paradigms, normal science, and the institutional persistence of models.
- **Daniel Kahneman, Thinking, Fast and Slow.** Bias, overconfidence, substitution, and judgment under uncertainty.
- **Federal Reserve, BIS, IMF and national statistical manuals.** Primary institutional definitions and measurement conventions relevant to the scope of fundamental analysis.
- **Benjamin Graham and David Dodd, Security Analysis.** Classical architecture for separating price from economic value and examining earning power and balance-sheet protection.
- **John Maynard Keynes, The General Theory.** Expectations, uncertainty, liquidity preference and the macro conditions influencing valuation.
