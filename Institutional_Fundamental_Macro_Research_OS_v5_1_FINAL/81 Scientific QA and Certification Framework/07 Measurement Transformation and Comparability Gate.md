# Measurement Transformation and Comparability Gate

## Why measurement precedes interpretation

Economic and financial variables are constructed objects. A change in definition, seasonal adjustment, chain weighting, index rebasing, consolidation scope or accounting policy can create apparent signals that are not economic changes.

## Required measurement record

For every material series or ratio document:

- concept measured;
- population and coverage;
- frequency and release lag;
- unit and price basis;
- nominal or real status;
- seasonal and calendar adjustment;
- stock, flow, rate, share or index classification;
- annualization convention;
- revision policy;
- known breaks;
- transformation used in the report;
- comparability limits across countries or firms.

## Transformation discipline

Do not mix:

- level and growth rate;
- month-on-month and year-on-year without base-effect analysis;
- nominal and real values;
- market value and book value;
- gross and net exposure;
- consolidated and unconsolidated balance sheets;
- spot and forward prices;
- physical units with financial notional;
- seasonally adjusted and unadjusted series.

## Ratio decomposition

Ratios must be decomposed before interpretation. For example, debt-to-GDP can move because debt changed, nominal GDP changed, valuation changed or the exchange rate changed. Margin can rise because price, mix, productivity or accounting classification changed.

## Cross-country comparability

Apply manuals and institutional definitions, including current IMF macroeconomic statistical standards. Where national practices differ, preserve the difference and avoid false ranking.

## Veto conditions

- the transformation is undocumented;
- units are incompatible;
- an index is treated as an absolute quantity;
- a denominator effect is ignored;
- revisions or methodology changes dominate the observed move;
- a proxy is used without a mapping to the target construct.
