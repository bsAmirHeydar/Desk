# Cross-Asset Balance-Sheet and Flow Transmission Gate

## Transmission map

A certified report traces the shock through relevant balance sheets and markets rather than listing correlations. Potential nodes include:

- households and mortgages;
- firms, banks and nonbanks;
- central banks and treasuries;
- dealers and clearing houses;
- asset managers, insurers and pensions;
- commodity producers, consumers and merchants;
- foreign reserve managers and sovereign wealth funds.

## Cross-asset roles

- **leader**: first market expected to price the mechanism;
- **confirmation**: independent evidence consistent with the mechanism;
- **transmission market**: carries the shock to the target asset;
- **hedge or offset**: absorbs or reverses the exposure;
- **contradiction**: behavior inconsistent with the primary model.

## Balance-sheet constraints

Assess leverage, duration, currency mismatch, liquidity needs, collateral, margin, capital requirements and hedging mandates. Institutions may transact for constraint reasons rather than directional beliefs.

## Flow versus stock

A large stock of holdings does not imply a current flow. Distinguish position, desired position, forced flow and marginal price-setting capacity.

## Cross-asset confirmation rule

Confirmation is strongest when the related market is economically independent and correctly timed. For example, a move in the same equity complex may not be independent, while front-end rates, credit or physical spreads may provide separate information.

## Veto conditions

- a cross-asset matrix is presented without a causal path;
- the same common factor is counted as multiple confirmations;
- balance-sheet constraints are ignored during stress;
- gross notional is confused with net risk or marginal flow.
