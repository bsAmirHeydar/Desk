# Valuation, Expectations and Payoff Gate

## Mandatory separation

Every analysis separates:

1. fundamental state;
2. expected future state;
3. market-implied distribution;
4. valuation or risk premium;
5. carry and financing;
6. catalyst and path;
7. payoff asymmetry.

A strong fundamental condition can produce a poor payoff when fully priced. A weak condition can produce a positive payoff when expectations are worse and improve at the margin.

## Asset-specific payoff maps

- **equities**: revenue, margins, reinvestment, discount rate, dilution and risk premium;
- **bonds**: policy path, inflation, term premium, credit, convexity and carry/roll;
- **FX**: relative policy, external balance, valuation, carry, intervention and funding;
- **commodities**: physical balance, inventories, curve, quality/location basis and hedging;
- **credit**: default, recovery, downgrade, liquidity, optionality and carry;
- **options**: physical versus risk-neutral distribution, volatility risk premium and path dependence.

## Pricing-gap standard

State the market baseline before stating a view. Evidence can include surveys, futures, OIS, yield curves, option distributions, analyst estimates, valuation ratios, spreads or physical forward curves. If the baseline cannot be reconstructed, the conclusion must be conditional.

## Expected payoff

Expected payoff is scenario-weighted and net of relevant carry, financing, convexity, liquidity and transaction cost. It is not a price target disguised as certainty.

## Veto conditions

- the report describes fundamentals without expectations;
- it states direction without valuation or payoff translation;
- carry or financing can dominate the stated horizon but are omitted;
- a market-implied measure is treated as a pure forecast without risk-premium caveats.
