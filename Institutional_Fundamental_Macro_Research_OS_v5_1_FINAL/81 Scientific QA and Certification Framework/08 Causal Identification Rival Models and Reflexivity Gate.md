# Causal Identification, Rival Models and Reflexivity Gate

## Causal map

A certified analysis states:

- treatment, shock or initiating condition;
- outcome variable;
- mediators and transmission channels;
- confounders and simultaneous responses;
- institutional reaction functions;
- expected timing and sign by horizon;
- conditions under which the relation reverses;
- observable falsifiers.

## Primary and rival models

At least one serious rival is required whenever the conclusion could change under a plausible alternative mechanism. Examples:

- higher yields from stronger real growth versus higher term premium;
- currency strength from policy divergence versus safe-haven demand;
- equity rally from earnings improvement versus discount-rate compression;
- commodity price rise from physical shortage versus financial positioning;
- credit-spread widening from default risk versus liquidity and dealer balance-sheet constraints.

## Identification strength labels

- **identified**: credible quasi-experimental, institutional or accounting evidence;
- **strongly supported**: multiple independent channels and correct lead-lag behavior;
- **plausible**: coherent but observationally underidentified;
- **speculative**: narrative with weak discriminating evidence;
- **rejected**: contradicted by key observations.

## Reflexivity

Market prices can change the fundamentals they appear to forecast. Equity values affect financing, collateral and confidence; exchange rates affect inflation and balance sheets; sovereign yields affect fiscal sustainability; commodity prices affect investment and future supply. The report must identify feedback loops and possible nonlinear thresholds.

## No post-hoc causality

A price move after an event does not prove the event caused the move. Test the proposed channel against:

- the first reacting market;
- the sign and magnitude across related assets;
- contemporaneous alternative news;
- positioning and liquidity;
- persistence after the initial window.
