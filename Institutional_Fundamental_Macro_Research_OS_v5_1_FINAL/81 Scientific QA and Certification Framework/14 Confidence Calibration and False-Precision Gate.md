# Confidence Calibration and False-Precision Gate

## Confidence dimensions

Confidence is multidimensional:

- state measurement confidence;
- expectations reconstruction confidence;
- causal identification confidence;
- payoff translation confidence;
- timing and horizon confidence;
- data freshness confidence;
- source completeness confidence.

A single overall number may be reported only after the dimensions are shown.

## Probability language

Use numeric probabilities only when they arise from a documented model, reference class or explicit judgmental process. Otherwise use calibrated bands such as:

- low: weak evidence or major unresolved conflict;
- moderate: coherent evidence with material uncertainty;
- high: multiple independent sources and discriminating confirmation;
- very high: rare, reserved for identities, contractual facts or near-direct measurements.

## Confidence caps

The maximum confidence is capped by the weakest material gate. Examples:

- missing consensus distribution;
- stale positioning proxy;
- uncertain physical inventory;
- unresolved policy reaction function;
- historical vintage gap;
- unobserved dealer positioning;
- accounting restatement risk.

## Calibration audit

When repeated analyses are available, compare stated confidence with realized direction, magnitude and thesis validity. Use Brier or log scores for probabilistic events and record overconfidence by domain.

## Veto conditions

- probabilities are stated without basis;
- confidence ignores missing data;
- verbal certainty exceeds the evidence grade;
- the conclusion is precise while the object or cutoff is ambiguous.
