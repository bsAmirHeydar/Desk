## Principle

Sessions differ in participant mix, liquidity, instruments, and catalyst exposure. Do not turn this into a rigid claim that one session must compress and another must expand. Use session structure as a conditional layer.

## Asia

Often most sensitive to:

- China/Japan/Australia/New Zealand data;
- Bank of Japan and regional policy;
- local equity and FX flows;
- commodity/physical gold demand;
- overnight US repricing digestion;
- thinner liquidity in some global instruments.

Questions:

- Did Asia confirm or fade the US close?
- Was the move local or broad?
- Did USD/JPY, CNH, commodities, or regional equities lead?
- Is the European session likely to reinterpret it?

## London

Often important for:

- global FX liquidity;
- European rates and equities;
- UK/euro-area data;
- commodity and precious-metal activity;
- repositioning before US data;
- overlap with Asia close and later New York.

Questions:

- Did European rates change the global policy story?
- Is dollar movement broad?
- Did gold/oil confirm?
- Is the London move likely to survive US data?

## New York

Often central for:

- US macro releases;
- Treasury cash market;
- US index futures and cash open;
- major equity/credit liquidity;
- commodity data;
- policy communication;
- options and closing flows.

Questions:

- Did the US release validate or reverse the overnight thesis?
- Did rates move first?
- Did cash-market breadth confirm futures?
- Did credit and volatility confirm?
- Did the move survive the first hour?

## Session Handoff

At each handoff, record:

1. inherited move;
2. original catalyst;
3. cross-asset confirmation;
4. new session’s potential reinterpretation;
5. local event risk;
6. whether the move is extended.

## Overnight Move Classification

### Information-led

New data/policy/earnings created a clear repricing.

### Flow-led

No major information; likely allocation, hedging, or thin-liquidity effect.

### Local shock

One region or asset moved without broad confirmation.

### Global shock

Rates, FX, commodities, credit, and equities cohere.

The classification determines how much confidence to carry into the next session.
