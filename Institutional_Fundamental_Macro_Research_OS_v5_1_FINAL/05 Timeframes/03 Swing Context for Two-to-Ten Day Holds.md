## Objective

A short swing captures a repricing process that is unlikely to finish in one session.

Typical drivers:

- policy-path convergence;
- multi-release growth/inflation sequence;
- earnings revision wave;
- fiscal supply or term-premium repricing;
- commodity balance change;
- positioning unwind;
- geopolitical risk-premium change.

## Swing Thesis Requirements

1. **Mispricing:** Define the expectation gap.
2. **Catalyst sequence:** More than one path to convergence.
3. **Persistence:** Explain why the driver lasts days.
4. **Expression:** Select the asset with the cleanest transmission.
5. **Carry/roll:** Understand the cost of holding.
6. **Overnight risk:** Define gap tolerance.
7. **Invalidation:** Fundamental and implementation.
8. **Event map:** Know all releases and earnings during the hold.
9. **Exit logic:** Target, time stop, and thesis completion.

## Day-to-Swing Conversion

A day trade may convert only when all were defined before entry:

- swing thesis existed;
- swing size was appropriate;
- overnight stop/invalidation was known;
- event risk was accepted;
- observable market-state confirmation supported the longer horizon.

Never convert because the day trade failed.

## Swing Context Layers

### Macro state

Growth, inflation, policy, liquidity, credit.

### Pricing gap

Rates, earnings, FX, commodity curve, volatility.

### Positioning

Crowding, potential squeeze, liquidation risk.

### Catalyst sequence

Data, meetings, auctions, earnings, policy deadlines.

### implementation expression

Daily/4H structure and the current execution framework.

## Example: Gold Long Swing

- Market prices sticky policy.
- Inflation trend softens.
- real yields begin falling.
- dollar weakens.
- central-bank demand supports dips.
- upcoming data can confirm.
- positioning is not extremely crowded.
- invalidation: real yields and dollar reverse above defined levels plus implementation structural break.

## Example: Nasdaq Short Swing

- Market prices benign cuts.
- inflation persistence returns.
- real yields rise.
- earnings revisions flatten.
- concentration is crowded.
- multiple data/auction catalysts can extend repricing.
- invalidation: policy path re-eases, real yields reverse, credit/breadth strengthen, observable market-state confirmation breaks upward.

## Time Stop

A thesis can be wrong by delay. Define:

- catalyst date;
- expected reaction window;
- maximum number of sessions without follow-through.

If the market does not respond despite confirming information, the lack of response is evidence.

## Weekend Risk

Geopolitics, policy announcements, earnings, and liquidity gaps can create discontinuous moves. Reduce size or avoid holding when the thesis cannot survive a plausible gap.
