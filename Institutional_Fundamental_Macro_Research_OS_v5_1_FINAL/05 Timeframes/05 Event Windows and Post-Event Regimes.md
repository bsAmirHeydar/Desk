## Event Window Phases

### Pre-event

- expectation formation;
- positioning;
- volatility premium;
- liquidity reduction;
- scenario preparation.

### Instant reaction

- headline parsing;
- automated execution;
- spread widening;
- first cross-asset move.

### Decomposition

- components and revisions;
- policy implications;
- competing narratives;
- partial retracement.

### Acceptance or rejection

- event extreme holds or fails;
- cash participants enter;
- rates and credit confirm;
- implementation continuation or reversal appears.

### Post-event regime

The event may create a new multi-session pricing state.

## Event-Day Permission

Possible policies:

- no entry before event;
- reduced risk before event;
- trade only after first decomposition;
- trade continuation after acceptance;
- trade reversal only when causal markets reverse;
- avoid if implied move and slippage make asymmetry poor.

## Acceptance Test

A move is more likely accepted when:

- causal market holds the repricing;
- cross-asset cluster remains coherent;
- first temporary counter-move is contained;
- cash-session participation confirms;
- volume/liquidity normalize without reversal;
- narrative remains stable after details.

## Rejection Test

- headline move reverses after components;
- front-end rates reverse;
- dollar or real yields reject;
- credit contradicts;
- event extreme fails repeatedly;
- implied volatility collapses without directional follow-through.

## Post-Event Regime Questions

- Did the policy path change for one meeting or many?
- Did the dominant constraint change?
- Did the event alter growth, inflation, or only noise?
- Did positioning reset?
- Is there another catalyst needed?
- What is the new invalidation level in causal markets?

## Event Half-Life

- Noisy second-tier data: minutes.
- Clean high-impact surprise: hours to days.
- Central-bank path change: days to weeks.
- Fiscal or geopolitical structural action: weeks or longer.
- One-off headline without implementation: minutes/hours.

Half-life should be inferred from repricing and follow-through, not assigned from event labels alone.
