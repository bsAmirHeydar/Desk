# Narrative Object Ontology

## Definition

A narrative is a market-used causal interpretation, not a slogan. It selects facts, supplies a mechanism, identifies a future state and implies consequences for specific assets and horizons.

## Minimum object

Each narrative must contain a stable identifier, plain-language thesis, selected and ignored facts, causal chain, assets, horizons, attention state, validity, dominance, price/flow control, institutional adoption, fact and narrative persistence, saturation, consumption, remaining power, fragility, rival narratives, transition triggers and provenance.

## Identity discipline

Narrative identity should remain stable while scores update. Create a new narrative identifier only when the interpretive claim or causal chain changes materially. A mere reinforcement is a state transition, not a new narrative. A policy clarification that changes the mechanism may require a successor narrative linked to its predecessor.

## Scope discipline

Narratives can be global, asset-specific, sector-specific or horizon-specific. “Rates matter” is too broad. “A persistent upward revision in the Fed path raises real yields and compresses Nasdaq duration valuation over the session and multi-day horizon” is sufficiently specified to test.
