# Market Attention as a Scarce Selection Process

## Economic object

Market attention is scarce. Investors cannot process every release, policy statement, earnings detail, flow condition and geopolitical development with equal intensity. Attention therefore behaves like a selection regime: the market promotes a subset of variables into active decision inputs and relegates others to background priors.

## Selection forces

Attention is more likely to concentrate when a fact is surprising, materially linked to portfolio risk, close to a known catalyst, easy to communicate, repeatedly reinforced, or capable of explaining several markets at once. Attention may remain low when a fact is slow-moving, difficult to measure, already embedded in mandates, temporarily displaced by a more urgent catalyst, or lacking a discovery trigger.

## State variables

Track awareness, salience, acceleration, concentration, breadth, institutional relevance, price sensitivity and competing-attention load. These are not interchangeable. High awareness with low price sensitivity describes a popular but weak controller. Low awareness with high conditional sensitivity describes a latent narrative.

## Operational rule

Attention is inferred from a portfolio of evidence: contemporaneous language, repeated conditional reactions, policy emphasis, expectation changes, options repricing, sector breadth, cross-asset leadership and verified flow/positioning. No single mention count or return is sufficient.
