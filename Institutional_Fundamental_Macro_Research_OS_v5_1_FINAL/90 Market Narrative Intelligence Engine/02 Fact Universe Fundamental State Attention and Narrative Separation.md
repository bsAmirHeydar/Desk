# Fact Universe Fundamental State Attention and Narrative Separation

## Four different objects

- A **fact** is an observed or credibly inferred state change.
- A **fundamental state** is the V11 assessment of the fact's direction, force, persistence, consumption and residual pressure.
- **Attention** is the market's selective allocation of processing capacity to a fact or question.
- A **narrative** is an interpretation that links selected facts to an expected causal path and asset consequence.

These objects have separate identifiers and ledgers. A fact may support several narratives. A narrative may select only part of the fact universe. Attention can move before validity improves, and validity can improve while attention falls.

## Required causal grammar

```text
observed fact
+ prior expectation
→ surprise or state revision
→ selected interpretation
→ expected transmission path
→ asset/horizon consequence
```

The analyst must list selected facts and ignored facts for each narrative. The omission is analytically important: many narratives remain coherent only because contradictory facts are excluded.

## Forbidden shortcuts

- Do not label a headline as a fact without source and timestamp.
- Do not label a fact as a narrative without an interpretive claim.
- Do not infer attention solely because price moved.
- Do not infer validity because the narrative was profitable.
- Do not infer fact consumption from narrative saturation.

The daily state stores the V11 fact vector under `inherited_v11_state` and the V12 attention/narrative objects separately.
