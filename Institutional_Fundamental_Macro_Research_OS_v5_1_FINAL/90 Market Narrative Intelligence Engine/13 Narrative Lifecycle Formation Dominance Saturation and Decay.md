# Narrative Lifecycle Formation Dominance Saturation and Decay

## Lifecycle

Use the states `UNNOTICED`, `LATENT`, `EMERGING`, `ACCELERATING`, `COMPETING`, `DOMINANT`, `BROADENING`, `MATURE`, `SATURATED`, `FRAGMENTING`, `LOSING_CONTROL`, `DISPLACED`, `DORMANT` and `REACTIVATED`.

A lifecycle transition requires an observable reason: new fact support, broader adoption, repeated reaction, contradiction, flow exhaustion, policy reset, rival takeover or event displacement.

## Non-monotonicity

Narratives do not decay mechanically. A mature narrative can be reopened by independent evidence. A saturated narrative can remain price-dominant if the underlying process is persistent. A displaced structural narrative can become dormant rather than invalidated.

## Recordkeeping

Store first-detected time, first-dominant time, transition time, evidence and prior state. Historical analysis must use only information available at each transition cutoff.
