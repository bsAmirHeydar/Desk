# Reflexivity Feedback and Self-Reinforcement

## Feedback map

Reflexivity links selected facts, beliefs, positioning, prices and new reality. Positive loops amplify; negative loops self-correct or destroy the original thesis.

Examples include price gains validating an AI-capex thesis and enabling cheaper financing; tighter financial conditions weakening growth and changing the policy path; safe-haven flows raising the dollar and creating liquidity stress that later forces Gold liquidation.

## Required fields

Every loop must identify the initial fact, belief transition, behavior change, price or funding effect, real-state consequence, alternative explanation and break condition. Mark whether the loop changes reality or only market price.

## Circularity control

“Price rose because the narrative was strong” is inadmissible when price is also the only evidence of strength. At least one non-price observation or predeclared causal sequence is required to establish the loop.
