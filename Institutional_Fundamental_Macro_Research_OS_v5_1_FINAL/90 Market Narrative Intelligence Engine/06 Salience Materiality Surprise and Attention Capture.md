# Salience Materiality Surprise and Attention Capture

## Distinct concepts

**Materiality** measures economic consequence. **Surprise** measures deviation from the priced or stated expectation. **Salience** measures the probability that market participants notice and use the information. Attention capture is strongest when these overlap, but they often diverge.

A highly material structural fact may be low-salience today. A small but symbolically important surprise may capture attention. A vivid headline may be salient but economically weak.

## Salience channels

Evaluate event proximity, simplicity, repetition, controversy, emotional intensity, portfolio-risk relevance, policy relevance, earnings relevance and explanatory usefulness. A fact that resolves an existing market question often captures more attention than an equally large fact outside the active debate.

## Output

Record `materiality`, `surprise`, `salience`, `attention_capture_state` and a reason code. Never use salience as a substitute for validity. If attention appears to rise without a new fact, identify whether repetition, price reflexivity, policy communication or position pressure is the source.
