## Preserved subject-specific foundation

Labor data affects both sides of the macro equation:

- income and demand support;
- wage/inflation persistence;
- recession risk;
- central-bank reaction function.

## Release Stack

### Employment report
- payroll growth;
- unemployment rate;
- participation;
- average hourly earnings;
- average weekly hours;
- household versus establishment survey;
- revisions;
- diffusion/breadth.

### Jobless claims
Higher-frequency but noisy; use trend and continuing claims.

### JOLTS
Openings, hires, quits, layoffs, and vacancy-unemployment balance.

### Private surveys and payroll estimates
Useful context, not substitutes for official data.

## Interpretation Matrix

| Labor outcome | Inflation context | Credit context | Likely dominant read |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strong | Hot | Stable | More hawkish policy |
| Strong | Cooling | Stable | Soft-landing / earnings support |
| Moderately weak | Cooling | Stable | Benign easing |
| Weak | Sticky | Deteriorating | Stagflation/risk-off |
| Very weak | Any | Widening stress | Growth/credit break |

## Hidden Information

A headline payroll number can mislead when:

- prior months are revised sharply;
- hours worked decline;
- unemployment rises due to participation changes;
- wage growth composition shifts;
- job gains are concentrated;
- household and establishment surveys diverge;
- temporary distortions affect the sample.

## Sahm-Type Signals

Recession indicators derived from unemployment-rate changes are useful state diagnostics, not precise day-trading triggers. They should be combined with claims, income, spending, production, and credit.

## Cross-Asset Read

- Front-end yields reveal policy-path interpretation.
- Curve behavior helps distinguish easing hope from recession fear.
- Credit separates benign weakness from adverse weakness.
- NQ/ES leadership reveals duration versus cyclicality.
- USD response depends on relative policy and global risk demand.
- Gold depends on real yields, USD, and tail-risk demand.

## Event Scenarios

### Strong payrolls, soft wages
Growth support may dominate if policy concern is limited.

### Strong payrolls, hot wages
Policy repricing may dominate, especially for NQ.

### Weak payrolls, unemployment stable, revisions modest
Potential benign cooling.

### Weak payrolls, unemployment up, hours down, revisions negative
Higher probability of a genuine growth break.

## Day-Trade Rule

Do not reduce the report to “beat/miss.” Identify which component changes the marginal central-bank and growth interpretation.
