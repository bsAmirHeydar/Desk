## Definition and economic object

Inflation analysis separates level, momentum, breadth, persistence, relative-price shocks, margins, wages, rents, and policy-relevant underlying pressure.

For **Inflation Releases**, the relevant institutional domain is **trading**: conversion of macro information into horizon-specific permissions, scenario paths, technical handoffs, and auditable trade management. Classify every input as observation, derived measurement, model estimate, market-implied estimate, forecast, causal claim, scenario assumption, judgment, or decision rule.

## Preserved subject-specific foundation

This material survived consolidation because it contains subject-specific instruction for **Inflation Releases**, not the former repeated institutional wrapper.

Inflation releases change markets when they alter the expected persistence of inflation or the policy path.

## Pre-Event Context

Record:

- headline and core consensus;
- monthly versus annual rates;
- recent three- and six-month annualized momentum;
- goods, shelter, housing-services, and non-housing-services trends;
- wage and productivity context;
- energy and commodity impulse;
- supply-chain indicators;
- inflation expectations;
- current central-bank focus;
- rate cuts/hikes already priced;
- market positioning and implied volatility.

## Decomposition

### Headline
Sensitive to food and energy; relevant to consumer purchasing power and expectations.

### Core
Removes food and energy but is not automatically the best persistence measure.

### Goods
Sensitive to supply chains, inventories, FX, import prices, and demand normalization.

### Shelter
Large and lagged; understand rent-measurement dynamics.

### Services excluding shelter
Often watched for labor-cost and persistence signals, but category definitions and noise matter.

### Breadth
Is pressure concentrated or widespread?

### Momentum
Month-over-month and short annualized rates may reveal turning points earlier than year-over-year rates.

### Revisions and seasonal factors
A “beat” can be offset by upward prior revisions or unstable seasonal adjustment.

## Reaction Map

### Hotter inflation + resilient growth
Likely channel:

```text
higher expected policy path
→ front-end yields up
→ real yields up
→ USD firmer
→ pressure on duration assets
```

### Hotter inflation + deteriorating growth
Potential stagflation:

```text
policy constraint + cash-flow pressure
→ risk-premium expansion
→ equities weaker
→ oil/gold response depends on source and real yields
```

### Cooler inflation + stable growth/credit
Benign disinflation:

```text
lower policy path
→ real yields down
→ easier financial conditions
→ duration/risk assets supported
```

### Cooler inflation because demand is breaking
Rates may fall, but credit and earnings channels can dominate negatively.

## Cross-Asset Confirmation

Monitor:

- two-year yield and meeting pricing;
- real-yield proxy;
- yield curve;
- dollar;
- breakevens;
- NQ versus ES and sector leadership;
- credit and volatility;
- gold.

## Intraday Trading Protocol

1. Do not trade the headline alone.
2. Read revisions and composition.
3. Observe the first rates reaction.
4. Determine whether NQ is responding to discount rates or growth fear.
5. Wait for acceptance or rejection.
6. Apply permission only after the causal chain becomes coherent.
7. Require the full technical setup.

## Swing Implications

A single inflation print can begin a swing only when:

- it changes the projected policy path;
- the surprise is supported by composition and revisions;
- cross-asset repricing persists into the close;
- subsequent catalysts are unlikely to immediately negate it.

## Common Errors

- Comparing actual only with consensus, not the market hurdle.
- Treating a year-over-year base effect as new monthly pressure.
- Ignoring revisions.
- Assuming all “core” categories carry equal persistence.
- Calling cooler inflation bullish while credit stress is rising.
- Extending a release reaction after rates fully retrace.
