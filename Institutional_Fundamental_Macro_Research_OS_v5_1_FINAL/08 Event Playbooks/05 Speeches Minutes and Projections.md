## Preserved subject-specific foundation

This material survived consolidation because it contains subject-specific instruction for **Speeches, Minutes, and Projections**, not the former repeated institutional wrapper.

Communication matters when it changes beliefs about the reaction function, not merely when it contains emotional adjectives.

## Speaker Classification

Assess:

- voting status;
- institutional role;
- historical influence;
- consistency with committee consensus;
- expertise/domain;
- whether remarks are prepared or Q&A;
- whether the market expected the message;
- proximity to the next decision.

## Statement Parsing

Separate:

- description of current data;
- forecast;
- policy preference;
- conditionality;
- risk balance;
- operational details;
- uncertainty;
- political or institutional constraint.

“Policy may remain restrictive” is not equivalent to a commitment. Translate language into conditional policy implications.

## Minutes

Minutes are delayed records. Their value comes from:

- distribution of views;
- concerns not visible in the statement;
- discussion of risks and implementation;
- evidence about the reaction function;
- disagreement and thresholds.

Because information may be stale, ask what has changed since the meeting.

## Projections

For each forecast set, inspect:

- median;
- distribution/range;
- revisions;
- internal consistency among growth, unemployment, inflation, and policy;
- difference between projections and market pricing;
- whether the path depends on financial conditions.

## Dot Plot Discipline

Dots are individual conditional assessments, not a binding committee promise. Market impact depends on:

- median shift;
- distribution;
- chair's interpretation;
- data dependency;
- market's preexisting path;
- credibility.

## Live Protocol

1. Verify exact wording and context.
2. Compare with prior communication.
3. Identify what is genuinely new.
4. Observe front-end rates.
5. Distinguish temporary headline algo response from sustained repricing.
6. Record whether the comment changes the thesis or only the catalyst probability.

## Common Errors

- Treating every speaker equally.
- Trading a quote stripped from its conditional clause.
- Ignoring that minutes are backward-looking.
- Reading projections as unconditional forecasts.
- Calling a statement hawkish/dovish without comparing with market pricing.
