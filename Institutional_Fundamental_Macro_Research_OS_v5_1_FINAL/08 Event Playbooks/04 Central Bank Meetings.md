## Preserved subject-specific foundation

This material survived consolidation because it contains subject-specific instruction for **Central Bank Meetings**, not the former repeated institutional wrapper.

A central-bank meeting can contain several shocks:

1. current policy-rate decision;
2. expected future path;
3. balance-sheet/liquidity guidance;
4. economic-information signal;
5. risk-management stance;
6. communication credibility.

## Pre-Meeting Map

Record:

- current policy rate;
- market-implied path by meeting;
- distribution of expectations;
- economist consensus;
- relevant inflation/growth/labor evolution;
- latest financial conditions;
- policymakers' reaction function;
- balance-sheet policy;
- projections/dot expectations if applicable;
- positioning and implied volatility;
- what would count as hawkish/dovish **relative to price**.

## Decomposition

### Decision surprise
Actual rate or balance-sheet action versus expectation.

### Path surprise
Guidance about future policy.

### Information shock
What the central bank reveals about the economy.

### Risk-management shock
Greater weight on one tail risk.

### Credibility shock
Whether markets believe the stated path.

## Policy versus Information Shock

A rate cut can be:

- supportive if it lowers the discount rate without revealing major damage;
- adverse if it signals the central bank sees severe deterioration;
- inflationary if it appears inconsistent with inflation risk;
- irrelevant if fully priced.

Therefore, “cut = bullish” and “hike = bearish” are invalid universal rules.

## Meeting Timeline

1. Pre-release positioning.
2. Statement.
3. Projections or implementation details.
4. Press conference.
5. Question-and-answer revisions to interpretation.
6. Post-close/next-session digestion.

Do not assume the statement move survives the press conference.

## Cross-Asset Read

- front-end rates: path surprise;
- long-end/term premium: inflation, supply, credibility;
- curve: growth versus policy interpretation;
- USD: relative-policy and risk demand;
- credit: benign versus adverse easing;
- NQ/ES leadership: discount rate versus growth;
- gold: real yields, USD, credibility/tail risk;
- volatility: uncertainty and dealer response.

## Intraday Protocol

- Define no-trade windows.
- Prewrite scenarios.
- Observe the first rate-path repricing.
- Delay certainty until press-conference information is incorporated.
- Use observable market-state confirmation formed after the event, not a pre-event narrative.
- Reduce risk because gaps/slippage can invalidate normal assumptions.
