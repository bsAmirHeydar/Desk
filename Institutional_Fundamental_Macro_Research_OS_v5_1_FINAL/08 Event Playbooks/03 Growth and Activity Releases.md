## GDP Decomposition

\[
Y = C + I + G + NX
\]

Headline GDP can be strong because of inventories or net exports while final domestic demand weakens. Conversely, headline can be weak while underlying consumption and investment remain resilient.

## Nominal versus Real

Strong nominal activity may reflect inflation rather than real volume. Markets care about both because:

- real growth informs earnings and demand;
- nominal growth affects revenues, tax receipts, and debt dynamics;
- inflation affects policy and discount rates.

## Survey versus Hard Data

Surveys may lead but can be sentiment-heavy. Hard data may lag and be revised. Use disagreement as information:

- surveys weak, hard data strong: potential future deceleration or survey bias;
- surveys improving, hard data weak: possible turning point;
- both deteriorating: higher confidence slowdown;
- both improving: higher confidence acceleration.

## Reaction Regimes

### Strong growth, stable inflation
Broad equities and cyclicals may benefit; rate response determines NQ.

### Strong growth, hot inflation
Policy/real-yield channel may pressure duration.

### Weak growth, cooling inflation, stable credit
Duration can benefit from benign easing.

### Weak growth, deteriorating credit
Risk-premium and earnings channels dominate.

## Nowcast Discipline

A nowcast is a model estimate using currently available data. It is not an official forecast and changes as inputs arrive. Track:

- current estimate;
- change after each input;
- contribution of volatile components;
- disagreement with other models;
- whether markets already price the direction.

## Intraday Use

For each release, define the component most connected to today's dominant question. Retail sales can matter through growth, rates, sector earnings, or inflation persistence. The same release can produce opposite equity effects in different regimes.
