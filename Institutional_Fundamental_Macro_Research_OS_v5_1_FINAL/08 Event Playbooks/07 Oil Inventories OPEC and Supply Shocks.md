## Preserved subject-specific foundation

This material survived consolidation because it contains subject-specific instruction for **Oil Inventories, OPEC, and Supply Shocks**, not the former repeated institutional wrapper.

Oil is driven by a physical balance, expectations about that balance, financial conditions, and geopolitical risk premium.

## Balance Identity

\[
\Delta \text{Inventories} = \text{Supply} - \text{Demand}
\]

But observed inventories are distributed across regions, products, and logistics. One weekly number is not the whole balance.

## Inventory Release Decomposition

- crude stocks;
- gasoline/distillate/product stocks;
- refinery utilization;
- imports and exports;
- implied product demand;
- production estimate;
- Strategic Petroleum Reserve flows;
- regional hubs;
- seasonal norms;
- revisions and data noise.

## Confirmation

- prompt spreads/backwardation/contango;
- physical differentials;
- refinery margins;
- tanker/logistics evidence;
- producer discipline;
- demand indicators;
- dollar and global growth context.

## OPEC/Producer Announcements

Separate:

- announced target;
- actual production;
- baseline change;
- voluntary versus collective cut;
- compliance;
- spare capacity;
- duration;
- credibility;
- offsetting non-OPEC supply;
- demand elasticity.

The market trades expected barrels, not the headline number alone.

## Geopolitical Supply Shock

Map:

```text
Event
→ actual production/export/logistics risk
→ duration of disruption
→ available inventories/spare capacity
→ policy response
→ expected physical balance
→ curve and flat-price response
```

## Intraday Event Protocol

- Expect initial headline volatility.
- Verify physical relevance.
- Read the curve, not only the CFD spot price.
- Check whether products confirm crude.
- Distinguish supply shock from broad risk-off demand destruction.
- Use reduced risk around weekly inventory data because measurement noise is high.
