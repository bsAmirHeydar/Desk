## Preserved subject-specific foundation

For NQ and ES, large constituents can create index-level fundamental shocks that temporarily dominate macro variables.

## Earnings Decomposition

- revenue;
- earnings and margin;
- guidance;
- bookings/backlog;
- capital expenditure;
- free cash flow;
- pricing power;
- unit demand;
- geographic/FX effects;
- inventory;
- management tone;
- segment-specific drivers;
- valuation implied before release.

## Expectations Stack

Consensus is only one layer:

1. published consensus;
2. recent estimate revisions;
3. buy-side/whisper expectations;
4. options-implied move;
5. valuation and positioning;
6. price action entering the report;
7. expectations for guidance and long-duration investment.

A company can beat consensus and fall because the market hurdle was higher.

## Index Transmission

Assess:

- index weight;
- sector and factor exposure;
- supplier/customer read-through;
- implications for aggregate capex/productivity;
- effect on earnings breadth;
- effect on rates if investment demand/inflation implications are material;
- options/dealer impact after the release.

## NQ-Specific Questions

- Does the report validate the current AI/technology capital-spending cycle?
- Are revenues and margins keeping pace with valuation?
- Is demand broad or concentrated?
- Does capex improve future cash flows or pressure near-term free cash flow?
- Does guidance change the aggregate earnings path?
- Is the move company-specific or a duration-factor move?

## Day-Trade Use

- Treat after-hours moves as provisional until liquid index trading.
- Examine peer and semiconductor confirmation.
- Compare NQ with ES and equal-weight measures.
- Read rates: a strong earnings report can be offset by higher yields.
- Do not use macro permission to ignore a constituent shock.

## Swing Use

A mega-cap release can alter a multi-day index thesis if it changes:

- aggregate earnings expectations;
- market leadership;
- capex cycle;
- valuation support;
- breadth;
- positioning.

## Common Errors

- Trading headline EPS.
- Ignoring guidance.
- Treating options-implied move as directional.
- Assuming an index must follow one company indefinitely.
- Confusing company-specific weakness with macro deterioration.
