## 7. Regime and multihorizon logic

- **Structural:** legal architecture, demographics, technology, resource base, long-run balance sheets and institutional credibility.
- **Cyclical:** growth, inflation, credit, profits, policy and financing over quarters.
- **Tactical:** expectations, valuation, issuance, positioning and policy repricing over weeks.
- **Multi-day:** persistent new information, catalyst path, carry, funding and forced-flow risk.
- **Day/event:** surprise vector, immediate inference, information half-life and cross-market confirmation.

A lower-horizon shock can change timing, distribution and confidence without automatically reversing the structural state. The conclusion must be reported separately by horizon.

## 15. Deep corporate, equity and industry interpretation

Corporate fundamental analysis begins with the economic unit rather than the reported segment label. Revenue is decomposed into price, volume, mix, currency, acquisitions and accounting changes; margins into input cost, labor, utilization, fixed-cost absorption, product mix, pricing and one-time items. Working capital, capital expenditure, leases, pensions, taxes, stock compensation, restructuring and acquisitions determine whether reported earnings convert into distributable cash.

ROIC must be matched to the capital required to generate operating profit and adjusted consistently for goodwill, acquired intangibles, R&D, leases and excess cash. High historical returns are not automatically durable: competitive entry, regulation, technological substitution, customer concentration and capital-cycle responses determine fade. Reverse valuation asks which revenue, margin, reinvestment and discount-rate assumptions are embedded in price before forming a view.

For indices, bottom-up earnings interact with weighting, concentration, free float, reconstitution, buybacks, issuance, passive flows and derivative hedging. Index performance can diverge from the median constituent when a few large firms dominate market capitalization. Sector analysis requires industry-specific operating units—subscriber economics, drug probabilities, load factors, same-store sales, semiconductor wafer capacity or reserve grade—rather than generic growth and margin ratios.

A current report separates business state, estimate revisions, valuation distribution and market-implied expectations. A historical report uses the filing, guidance and consensus set available at the cutoff and preserves subsequent accounting restatements only in an ex-post section.
