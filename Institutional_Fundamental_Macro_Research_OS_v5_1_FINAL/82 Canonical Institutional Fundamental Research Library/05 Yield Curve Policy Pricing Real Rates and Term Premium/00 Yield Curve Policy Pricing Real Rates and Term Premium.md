## 15. Deep fixed-income and sovereign-market interpretation

Fixed income is a set of contingent discount factors, funding contracts and delivery options rather than a single yield. The analyst decomposes each curve point into expected short rates, inflation compensation, term premium, liquidity and instrument-specific basis. Curve moves must be classified as level, slope, curvature or cross-market relative value, and the decomposition must remain explicit about model dependence. A fitted term premium is not observed data; an OIS forward is not a pure policy expectation; a breakeven is not a survey forecast of inflation.

Treasury and sovereign analysis requires the integration of fiscal financing, auction calendars, dealer balance sheets, repo, collateral scarcity, foreign and domestic holder demand, derivatives hedging and central-bank operations. Auction tails or stop-throughs are informative only relative to pre-auction concession, issue liquidity, allotments and post-auction performance. Repo specialness and fails can change futures basis and swap spreads without a macro view changing.

For futures, the cheapest-to-deliver bond, conversion factor, accrued interest, carry, delivery timing and embedded options determine the economics. For mortgages, prepayment behavior makes duration endogenous: falling yields can shorten expected life and create receiving or buying demand, while rising yields extend duration and can force paying or selling. These convexity flows can dominate short-horizon curve behavior even when the macro state is unchanged.

Every rates conclusion therefore reports three layers: macro policy distribution, sovereign supply/risk-bearing conditions, and instrument plumbing. Independent evidence requires separation of these layers. A yield rise caused by stronger growth is not equivalent to one caused by fiscal-risk premium, collateral dysfunction or forced duration extension.
