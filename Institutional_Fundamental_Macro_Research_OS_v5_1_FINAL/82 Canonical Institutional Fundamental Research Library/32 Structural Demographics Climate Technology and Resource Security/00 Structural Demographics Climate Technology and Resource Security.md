## 7. Regime and multihorizon logic

- **Structural:** legal architecture, demographics, technology, resource base, long-run balance sheets and institutional credibility.
- **Cyclical:** growth, inflation, credit, profits, policy and financing over quarters.
- **Tactical:** expectations, valuation, issuance, positioning and policy repricing over weeks.
- **Multi-day:** persistent new information, catalyst path, carry, funding and forced-flow risk.
- **Day/event:** surprise vector, immediate inference, information half-life and cross-market confirmation.

A lower-horizon shock can change timing, distribution and confidence without automatically reversing the structural state. The conclusion must be reported separately by horizon.

## 11. Falsification, uncertainty and failure modes

- Reject conclusions that depend on an unidentified residual being treated as a structural factor.
- Cap confidence when measurement, sampling, revision or liquidity uncertainty is material.
- Separate absence of evidence from evidence of absence.
- Record the strongest contrary official or market evidence.
- State the expected sign and timing of leading indicators before observing the target outcome.
- Distinguish a wrong state estimate, wrong priced baseline, wrong causal model, wrong horizon and exogenous residual.

## 15. Deep geopolitical and structural-system interpretation

Geopolitical analysis is an institutional probability problem, not a collection of headlines. The analyst maps actors, objectives, domestic constraints, legal authorities, alliance commitments, military or economic capabilities, bargaining space and escalation ladders. Policy announcements are separated from implemented measures, exemptions, enforcement capacity and private-sector adaptation.

Sanctions and export controls transmit through payment systems, shipping, insurance, trade finance, technology access, inventories, substitution and enforcement networks. The gross affected trade value is not the economic loss: incidence depends on alternative buyers and suppliers, price elasticities, routing cost, inventory, currency settlement and secondary sanctions. Election analysis requires rules, coalition arithmetic, legislative thresholds, institutional vetoes and implementation calendars.

Structural forces—demographics, climate, technology, resource endowment and political institutions—operate through capital formation, labor supply, fiscal burdens, productivity, migration and security. They create priors and constraints rather than precise short-horizon timing signals. Historical analogues are used to test mechanisms, not to assert that surface resemblance guarantees the same outcome.

Every scenario identifies observable signposts, decision points and path dependence. Unknown intent is not assigned a pseudo-precise probability; judgmental weights are labeled and updated when actions change the feasible set.
