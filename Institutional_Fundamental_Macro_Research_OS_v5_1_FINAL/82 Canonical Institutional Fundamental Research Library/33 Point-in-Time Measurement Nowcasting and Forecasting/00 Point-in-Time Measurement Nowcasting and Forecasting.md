## 5. Causal architecture and transmission

The primary mechanism must be written as a directed sequence from initiating state or shock through balance-sheet, policy, income, funding, valuation or physical-market channels to the target payoff. The analyst must identify: initiating variable; constrained institution; mediator; expected leading market or statistic; time delay; nonlinear threshold; feedback loop; and falsifier. A target price move alone is never confirmation of its proposed cause.

## 7. Regime and multihorizon logic

- **Structural:** legal architecture, demographics, technology, resource base, long-run balance sheets and institutional credibility.
- **Cyclical:** growth, inflation, credit, profits, policy and financing over quarters.
- **Tactical:** expectations, valuation, issuance, positioning and policy repricing over weeks.
- **Multi-day:** persistent new information, catalyst path, carry, funding and forced-flow risk.
- **Day/event:** surprise vector, immediate inference, information half-life and cross-market confirmation.

A lower-horizon shock can change timing, distribution and confidence without automatically reversing the structural state. The conclusion must be reported separately by horizon.

## 11. Falsification, uncertainty and failure modes

- Reject conclusions that depend on an unidentified residual being treated as a structural factor.
- Cap confidence when measurement, sampling, revision or liquidity uncertainty is material.
- Separate absence of evidence from evidence of absence.
- Record the strongest contrary official or market evidence.
- State the expected sign and timing of leading indicators before observing the target outcome.
- Distinguish a wrong state estimate, wrong priced baseline, wrong causal model, wrong horizon and exogenous residual.

## 15. Deep measurement, inference and synthesis interpretation

Institutional analysis must preserve the information set. Every observation has a reference period, valid time, publication time, vintage and retrieval timestamp. Mixed-frequency and ragged-edge datasets require explicit treatment of missing releases; revised macro data cannot silently replace first releases in a historical reconstruction. Consensus is a distribution of forecasts with contributor and timestamp rules, not merely the median printed on a calendar.

Nowcasts, state-space models, dynamic factors, MIDAS, BVARs, local projections and machine-learning models solve different tasks. Model selection is benchmark-relative and pseudo-real-time. Forecast accuracy, density calibration, stability, interpretability and decision loss are reported separately. Causal claims require an identification argument; predictive importance is not causal effect.

Cross-asset synthesis prevents duplicate evidence. A policy repricing visible in OIS and the two-year yield is one information family, not two independent confirmations. Balance-sheet identities, derivative links and common-factor exposures are encoded before counting evidence. Scenario probabilities are classified as empirically calibrated, model-implied or judgmental and never blended without disclosure.

The final report maintains a contradiction ledger, unknown register, rival-model table and update triggers. Confidence is a statement about the evidence process under a specified model and horizon, not a rhetorical measure of conviction.
