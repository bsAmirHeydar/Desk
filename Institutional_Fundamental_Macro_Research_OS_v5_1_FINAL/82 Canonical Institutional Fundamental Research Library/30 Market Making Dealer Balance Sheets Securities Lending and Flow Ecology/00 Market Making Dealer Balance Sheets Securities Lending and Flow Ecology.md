## 15. Deep derivatives, market-making and flow interpretation

Derivatives translate distributions, funding and balance-sheet constraints into nonlinear payoffs. Implied volatility is the price of a replicating portfolio under risk-neutral probabilities and contains variance risk premium, supply-demand imbalance, jumps and liquidity. Skew and term structure must be interpreted by expiry, strike, forward, rates, dividends, borrow and event calendar.

Dealer exposures are inferred, not observed in aggregate. Gamma, vanna and charm estimates depend on assumptions about customer side, open-interest ownership, netting and hedging frequency. They are useful scenario variables only when labeled as model-implied and cross-checked against intraday liquidity and option-surface changes.

Market-making economics connect spread, adverse selection, inventory risk, funding, capital and hedging cost. Securities lending and borrow constraints affect short positioning, settlement and corporate actions. Systematic strategies—including trend, volatility control, risk parity and target-date rebalancing—respond to realized states through specified rules, but estimates require assets under management, execution windows and portfolio composition.

The analytical task is to separate fundamental information from path-shaping flow. Flow can change the speed, overshoot and liquidation risk of a move; it cannot by itself establish the long-horizon economic value of the asset.
