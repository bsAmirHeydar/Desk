A lower-horizon shock can change timing, distribution and confidence without automatically reversing the structural state. The conclusion must be reported separately by horizon.

## 15. Deep country-system interpretation

Country analysis begins with institutional specificity. The analyst maps the constitution of fiscal authority, central-bank mandate, banking and capital-market structure, exchange-rate regime, external accounts, energy and food dependence, labor institutions, demographics, housing finance and political decision process. Aggregate ratios are interpreted through holder, currency, maturity and legal structure.

A country can combine strong sovereign solvency with private-sector fragility, or a weak fiscal position with a deep domestic investor base that delays adjustment. External vulnerability depends on gross financing needs, liquid reserves, hedged and unhedged foreign-currency exposure, export composition and market access. Local debt must be separated from foreign-law debt; consolidated public-sector obligations may differ from headline central-government debt.

Country data require a methodological map. Informal activity, seasonal patterns, administrative revisions, controlled prices, multiple exchange rates and off-budget entities can make cross-country comparison misleading. Official data are triangulated with trade partners, international institutions, company filings and market instruments while respecting differences in definition.

The country conclusion is reported by horizon and channel: domestic demand, inflation, policy, fiscal financing, external balance, financial stability and political implementation. A single country label such as 'risk-on' or 'reform' is never a sufficient analytical state.
