## The Horizon Error

The horizon error occurs when a valid idea is used at the wrong time scale.

Examples:

- “Debt is unsustainable” is not a reason to short Nasdaq at the open.
- “The Fed will eventually cut” is not enough to buy bonds when the current data are repricing cuts later.
- “Gold benefits from geopolitical uncertainty” does not guarantee that gold rises during a liquidation event.
- “Oil supply is tight” may coexist with a one-day selloff caused by risk reduction or inventory surprise.

## Horizon Stack

A strong trade has alignment or at least non-conflict across the stack.

### Example: Nasdaq Long

- Structural: AI/productivity investment supports earnings capacity.
- Cyclical: growth is slowing but not collapsing.
- Tactical: policy easing is being priced without credit stress.
- Daily: softer inflation lowers real yields.
- Intraday: yields and dollar remain contained during New York.
- implementation: fundamental-state persistence temporary counter-move with valid trigger.

### Example: Gold Short

- Structural: neutral.
- Cyclical: inflation expectations stable.
- Tactical: real yields rising, dollar firm, central-bank demand not an immediate catalyst.
- Daily: hawkish repricing.
- Intraday: real yields extend higher and gold fails to recover after the release.
- implementation: downside continuation setup.

## Context Decay

Every context note should state a decay rule.

- Structural thesis: decay after policy or institutional change.
- Cyclical thesis: decay after several contradictory data waves.
- Tactical thesis: decay when market pricing reaches the target or catalyst passes.
- Daily thesis: decay at session close unless explicitly converted into swing.
- Event thesis: decay when the market has fully digested components and follow-through fails.

## Rule

> Never extend a day trade into a swing merely because the day trade is losing. A swing requires a pre-existing swing thesis, overnight-risk plan, and separate invalidation.
