## Fundamental, Macro, Flow, and Microstructure

These are distinct but connected.

### Fundamental

The economic or financial drivers of an asset’s value: earnings, rates, supply, demand, balance-sheet quality, policy, and risk.

### Macro

Economy-wide and cross-border conditions: growth, inflation, employment, policy, fiscal stance, credit, liquidity, and capital flows.

### Flow

Actual or expected buying and selling caused by allocation, hedging, rebalancing, issuance, redemptions, systematic strategies, or forced deleveraging.

### Microstructure

How orders meet liquidity: spreads, depth, queueing, dealer inventory, market impact, auctions, and session structure.

For day trading, the hierarchy is often:

> Macro defines the pressure field → catalyst activates repricing → flow carries it → microstructure shapes the path → technical execution defines the trade.

## Why Non-Event Context Matters

An event is only one source of change. Markets also move because:

- the interpretation of old data changes;
- yields drift enough to alter valuations;
- financial conditions tighten or ease;
- fiscal issuance changes term premium;
- commodity balances evolve;
- a policy speech changes the reaction function;
- positioning reaches a threshold;
- cross-border funding stress emerges;
- earnings expectations are revised;
- a geopolitical risk premium grows or decays.

The context system must therefore be alive between releases.
