# Core Doctrine

> [!abstract] Research mandate
> Construct a point-in-time, source-controlled, model-aware and falsifiable understanding of **Core Doctrine**. Canonical doctrine is linked; this note contains the topic-specific research object.

## Definition and economic object

**Core Doctrine** is a research object inside the institutional research process, its roles, information boundaries, controls, and decision handoffs. The analyst must isolate the measurable state, the expectation already embedded in prices, the mechanism connecting them, and the horizon on which that inference can survive.

For **Core Doctrine**, the relevant institutional domain is **orientation**: the institutional research process, its roles, information boundaries, controls, and decision handoffs. Classify every input as observation, derived measurement, model estimate, market-implied estimate, forecast, causal claim, scenario assumption, judgment, or decision rule.

## Research questions

1. What exact state or mechanism does **Core Doctrine** represent, in what unit, population, instrument, and convention?
2. Which **Core Doctrine** observations existed at the decision cutoff, which are estimates, and which are revised?
3. What distribution about **Core Doctrine** is embedded in consensus, curves, options, valuation, positioning, or physical basis?
4. Which market or variable must lead if the proposed **Core Doctrine** mechanism is active?
5. What rival model can create the same target move while **Core Doctrine** is unchanged?
6. How do regime, horizon, positioning, liquidity, carry, and implementation alter the payoff?
7. Which predeclared evidence rejects, caps, or expires the **Core Doctrine** decision?

## Day-trading decision translation

- Identify the new **Core Doctrine** information since the prior close and its source timestamp.
- Reconstruct the priced **Core Doctrine** baseline before reading the target move.
- Name the liquid leader closest to the **Core Doctrine** mechanism and one independent confirmation.
- Compare observed transmission with the **Core Doctrine** event/quiet-day historical distribution.
- Assign a permission and a confidence cap; record the **Core Doctrine** cancellation condition.
- Pass only the permission, leader, invalidation, expiry, and size ceiling to technical execution.

For **Core Doctrine**, fundamentals restrict the allowed trade set; they do not supply the candle trigger or authorize widening a structural stop.

## Two-to-ten-day swing translation

- Define the still-open **Core Doctrine** pricing gap rather than the general narrative.
- Estimate the **Core Doctrine** impulse half-life and its uncertainty by regime.
- Map catalysts capable of confirming, reversing, or exhausting the **Core Doctrine** campaign.
- Compare outright and relative expressions for carry, convexity, gap, liquidity, and factor purity.

A valid **Core Doctrine** thesis with no residual pricing gap, adverse carry beyond expected payoff, or an imminent dominating catalyst is not a deployable swing.

## Preserved subject-specific foundation

This material survived consolidation because it contains subject-specific instruction for **Core Doctrine**, not the former repeated institutional wrapper.

## Doctrine 1 — Markets Trade Differences, Not Levels

Asset prices respond primarily to changes in expected future states relative to what was already priced.

A high inflation number can be bullish for a currency if it raises the expected policy path more than it damages growth credibility. It can be bearish if inflation is supply-driven, growth is weak, and the central bank is believed unable or unwilling to respond. The level is not enough.

## Doctrine 2 — Fundamental Context Is a Prior, Not an Entry Signal

Context answers:

- Which direction has a stronger causal case?
- Which direction is more vulnerable to a squeeze?
- What catalyst can activate the thesis?
- What should not happen if the thesis is correct?

Execution answers:

- Is there a valid setup now?
- Where is invalidation?
- Is the trade asymmetric?
- Is liquidity sufficient?

Do not use a macro thesis to enter without a setup.

## Doctrine 3 — Price Is the Clearing Output

Price reflects the interaction of:

- expected cash flows,
- discount rates,
- risk premia,
- policy expectations,
- positioning,
- hedging,
- liquidity,
- forced flows,
- and information quality.

A correct economic forecast can lose money when it is already priced, poorly timed, expressed through the wrong asset, or overwhelmed by a different transmission channel.

## Doctrine 4 — The Reaction Function Matters More Than the Data Point

A release matters because it changes the expected behavior of:

- a central bank,
- a government,
- corporations,
- households,
- or large asset allocators.

Ask, “What action path does this information change?”

## Doctrine 5 — Cross-Asset Coherence Raises Confidence

A single price move is ambiguous. A coherent cluster is more informative.

Example of a hawkish repricing cluster:

- front-end yields higher,
- real yields higher,
- policy-cut probabilities lower,
- dollar stronger,
- gold weaker,
- long-duration equities underperforming.

Example of a growth scare cluster:

- yields lower,
- curve dynamics consistent with easing expectations,
- credit spreads wider,
- cyclicals weaker,
- defensives outperforming,
- oil weaker,
- volatility higher.

No cluster is universal. The purpose is to identify which causal explanation fits the widest set of observations.

## Doctrine 6 — Regime Determines Sign and Magnitude

The market’s dominant concern can be:

- inflation,
- growth,
- liquidity,
- solvency,
- fiscal credibility,
- geopolitics,
- earnings,
- or positioning.

The same data surprise has different meaning under different dominant concerns.

## Doctrine 7 — Horizon Matching Is Mandatory

A six-month fiscal thesis may support a swing bias but be irrelevant to a one-hour trade. A 30-minute dealer-hedging flow may dominate an intraday move while leaving the macro thesis intact.

Every thesis needs:

- expected start time,
- expected persistence,
- activation condition,
- decay condition,
- invalidation.

## Doctrine 8 — Uncertainty Must Be Represented

Use probability ranges, not certainty language.

A practical scale:

- 50–55%: weak lean
- 55–65%: usable directional preference
- 65–75%: strong context, still conditional
- above 75%: rare; demand unusually coherent evidence

This scale is not a forecast of the next candle. It is confidence in the context model.

## Doctrine 9 — “No Trade” Is a Positive Decision

No trade is appropriate when:

- growth and inflation impulses conflict,
- cross-asset signals are incoherent,
- a major catalyst is imminent,
- the market has already completed the expected move,
- positioning creates extreme squeeze risk,
- the technical setup opposes structural invalidation,
- or the time horizon is mismatched.

## Doctrine 10 — Attribution Is Part of the Edge

After the trade, separate:

- thesis quality,
- timing quality,
- instrument choice,
- execution quality,
- stop placement,
- target logic,
- and random outcome.

A losing trade can validate the process. A winning trade can reveal a bad process.
