## Why Plumbing Matters

The financial system needs cash, reserves, collateral, dealer balance sheet, clearing capacity, and settlement infrastructure. Stress in these systems can dominate ordinary macro relationships.

## Core Components

### Money Markets

- policy corridor/floor;
- overnight rates;
- secured and unsecured funding;
- money-market funds;
- central-bank facilities.

### Repo and Collateral

Repo exchanges cash for collateral. Rates and specialness can reveal:

- collateral scarcity;
- funding demand;
- balance-sheet constraints;
- settlement pressure.

### Reserves

Bank reserves support settlement and liquidity. The relevant question is not only total reserves but their distribution and demand.

### Treasury Cash and Settlement

Government receipts, spending, issuance, and settlements move cash between Treasury accounts and the private system.

### Dealer Balance Sheet

Dealers intermediate bonds, derivatives, and financing under capital and risk constraints. Limited balance sheet can reduce market depth and raise volatility.

### Dollar Funding

Global institutions borrow and hedge dollars through cash and derivatives markets. Cross-currency basis and swap markets can reveal stress.

```mermaid

    A[Funding stress] --> B[Higher margin/collateral demand]
```

## Day-Trading Signs

- abnormal basis or funding moves;
- Treasury market depth deterioration;
- simultaneous selling of risky and safe assets;
- sharp dollar strength;
- wider credit and volatility;
- failed ordinary correlations;
- central-bank facility headlines.

## Normal vs Stress Liquidity

### Normal

Macro information transmits through rates, FX, and risk premia with relatively stable market depth.

### Stress

Cash demand and balance-sheet preservation dominate. Gold, Treasuries, or high-quality assets may be sold temporarily. The dollar can strengthen even when US-specific data are weak.

When funding stress is plausible:

- avoid converting liquidity noise into long-term macro belief.
