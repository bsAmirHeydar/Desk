## Core Decomposition

A nominal government yield can be approximated as:

\[
\text{Nominal yield}
\approx
\text{Real yield}
+
\text{Inflation compensation}
\]

Inflation compensation is not a pure forecast. It includes expected inflation, inflation risk premium, and market/liquidity effects.

## Why the Decomposition Matters

A 10-year nominal yield rising 10 basis points can mean:

- real growth or policy expectations rose;
- inflation compensation rose;
- term premium rose;
- liquidity changed;
- or several channels moved together.

The asset implications differ.

## Real Yields

Real yields represent the return above inflation compensation on inflation-protected debt.

Higher real yields generally:

- raise the discount rate on future cash flows;
- challenge long-duration equity valuations;
- increase the opportunity cost of holding non-yielding gold;
- support the currency through relative return;
- tighten financial conditions.

These are conditional tendencies, not laws.

## Breakevens

Breakeven inflation is the difference between nominal and inflation-protected yields of similar maturity.

Breakevens can rise because of:

- higher expected inflation;
- higher inflation uncertainty/risk premium;
- commodity shocks;
- stronger nominal demand;
- liquidity differences.

## Event Decomposition

After inflation data, observe:

1. nominal two-year and ten-year yields;
2. real yields;
3. breakevens;
4. policy futures;
5. dollar;
6. gold;
7. equity duration;
8. curve shape.

### Hot inflation with real yields up

More directly hawkish and adverse to gold/Nasdaq.

### Hot inflation with breakevens up but real yields stable

More inflation-risk than policy-tightening. Equity response depends on margins, policy credibility, and sector composition.

### Soft inflation with real yields down and credit stable

Constructive rate relief.

### Soft inflation with real yields down and credit wider

Potential growth scare.

## Liquidity Caveat

TIPS and nominal Treasuries have different liquidity. During stress, breakeven moves can reflect liquidity distortion rather than clean inflation expectations.

## Horizon

- 1–2 year inflation pricing: more sensitive to near-term inflation and policy.
- 5–10 year pricing: credibility and long-run inflation risk.
- 5y5y forward: long-horizon expectation/risk signal, but model and liquidity caveats apply.

## Gold Rule

Do not say “inflation is high, therefore buy gold.” Ask:

- Did real yields rise or fall?
- Did the dollar strengthen or weaken?
- Did policy credibility improve or deteriorate?
- Is demand monetary, geopolitical, physical, or reserve-related?
- Is the horizon intraday or structural?

## Nasdaq Rule

Do not say “yields rose, therefore short.” Ask:

- Which yield?
- Real or breakeven?
- Growth-led or policy-led?
- Did earnings expectations improve?
- Did credit remain benign?
- Is technology leadership broad or concentrated?
