## Executed internal reference answer

The reference answer begins with the October 6, 1979 Federal Reserve operating-procedure change and the pre-existing loss of inflation credibility. Monetary aggregates and reserve targeting did not make money growth mechanically controllable; the practical transmission ran through scarce reserves, volatile federal-funds rates, bank credit, housing and recession. The correct ex-ante state was persistent broad inflation with an official willingness to accept real-economy cost. Rival explanations include oil shocks and fiscal demand, but neither removes the role of policy credibility. A full answer separates the 1979 announcement from later rate peaks and the 1981-82 recession.

### Horizon and inference controls

- **Event/day:** identify the immediate information shock, constrained market or balance sheet and the first admissible confirming evidence.
- **Multi-day/tactical:** explain persistence, policy response, funding, positioning and the information expected next.
- **Cyclical/structural:** state whether the event changes the underlying regime or only reveals an existing fragility.
- **Rival model:** preserve at least one mechanism that can explain the same observed move.
- **Ex-post boundary:** later outcomes are used only to evaluate the prior process, not to rewrite the ex-ante information set.
