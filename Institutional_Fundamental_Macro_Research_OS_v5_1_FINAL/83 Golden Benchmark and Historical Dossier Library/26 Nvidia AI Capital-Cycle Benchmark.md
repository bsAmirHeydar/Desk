1. Resolve the exact institution, security, contract, geography and legal regime.

5. Identify the variables or markets expected to lead if the mechanism is correct.
6. Report conclusions separately for event, day, multi-day, tactical, cyclical and structural horizons.
7. Run the claim-evidence, contradiction, unknowns and false-precision gates.

## Executed internal reference answer

The Nvidia benchmark analyzes AI-accelerator demand, hyperscaler capex, product cycle, supply and packaging constraints, customer concentration, gross margin, competitive alternatives and expectations embedded in valuation. A correct answer does not equate strong current orders with an unlimited terminal market; it tests capacity, digestion, inference demand, software ecosystem and return on customer investment.

### Horizon and inference controls

- **Event/day:** identify the immediate information shock, constrained market or balance sheet and the first admissible confirming evidence.
- **Multi-day/tactical:** explain persistence, policy response, funding, positioning and the information expected next.
- **Cyclical/structural:** state whether the event changes the underlying regime or only reveals an existing fragility.
- **Rival model:** preserve at least one mechanism that can explain the same observed move.
- **Ex-post boundary:** later outcomes are used only to evaluate the prior process, not to rewrite the ex-ante information set.

### Consolidated legacy note: 02 Evidence Source Lineage and Claim Types

Evidence must be ranked by its relationship to the phenomenon, not by how persuasive the prose appears.

1. **Primary legal or statistical record:** law, regulation, filing, official statistical release, exchange specification, auction result, central-bank decision.
2. **Primary operational record:** transaction record, inventory record, settlement data, company transcript, physical-flow measurement, official balance sheet.
3. **Derived official estimate:** seasonally adjusted series, national accounts estimate, central-bank model estimate.
4. **Auditable market-derived estimate:** curve-implied policy path, breakeven, option-implied distribution, basis or spread calculated from documented inputs.
5. **Peer-reviewed or institutionally documented model:** published methodology with reproducible assumptions.
6. **Reputable secondary analysis:** useful for synthesis, not a substitute for primary evidence.
7. **Expert judgment:** admissible only when labeled, bounded and reviewable.
8. **Unverified commentary:** never sufficient for a material claim.

| Claim type | Example | Minimum support |
|---|---|---|
| Observation | CPI was published at a stated value | Primary release and timestamp |
| Accounting identity | Sector balances sum consistently | Definitions, units and reconciliation |
| Measurement claim | Shelter inflation momentum changed | Transformation and sensitivity analysis |
| Forecast claim | Growth distribution shifted lower | Model version, benchmark and interval |
| Causal claim | Funding stress caused basis widening | Identification strategy and rival models |
| Market-pricing claim | More easing is embedded in the curve | Instrument specification and curve construction |
| Scenario claim | A tariff shock would raise near-term goods inflation | Conditional assumptions and transmission map |
| Decision claim | Deployment is unfavorable after costs | Scenario payoff, risk, cost and portfolio context |

### Consolidated legacy note: Epistemology and Uncertainty

This material survived consolidation because it contains subject-specific instruction for **Epistemology and Uncertainty**, not the former repeated institutional wrapper.

- the two-year yield rose 12 basis points;
- the release printed 0.4% month over month;
- the prior was revised down;
- credit spreads widened.

A fact produced by a methodology that may contain sampling error, revisions, seasonal adjustment, or model assumptions.

1. direct market prices and official releases;
2. official methodology and primary documents;
3. robust, replicated empirical research;
4. transparent market-implied estimates;
5. professional surveys;
6. reasoned inference;
7. media narrative;
8. anonymous social-media claims.

Higher rank does not guarantee relevance. A reliable quarterly statistic can still be too slow for an intraday decision.

```text
Context confidence =
    state clarity
  × expectation clarity
  × transmission clarity
  × timing relevance
  × cross-asset coherence
  × robustness to alternatives
```

This is a conceptual multiplication. If any component is near zero, total confidence must fall.

- the state estimate is wrong;
- the expectation estimate is wrong;
- the transmission channel changes;
- a hidden flow dominates;
- the market focuses on a different component;
- timing is early;
- the asset is a poor expression;
- the move is already priced;
- or the qualified implementation condition does not appear.

- “The evidence currently favors…”
- “The market appears to be repricing…”
- “This would be consistent with…”
- “The thesis requires…”
- “Confidence falls if…”

- “The market must…”
- “This always means…”
- “Institutions are doing…”
- “They manipulated…”
- “The news was good, so price should rise.”

1. Start with a prior.
2. Assess the reliability of new evidence.
3. Ask how likely the evidence was under each scenario.
4. Update the scenario probabilities.
5. Do not overreact to one noisy observation.
6. Do not underreact to a coherent cluster.

The purpose is not mathematical theater. It is to prevent narrative anchoring.
