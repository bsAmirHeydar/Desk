# Scope doctrine

D1 solves a narrow but foundational problem: Alpha Lab had strong point-in-time, provenance, dependency and materiality concepts distributed across V11–V16.1, but no single constitution answered **what counts as a fact across every present and future market science**.

D1 therefore governs admission of evidence before the scientific decision stack. It is upstream of V11 Fundamental, V12 Narrative, V13 instrument routing, V15.1 Timing and V16.1 production control.

## In scope

- explicit fact and inference classes;
- source identity and source-quality tiers;
- source snapshot identity and content hashes;
- event/reference/effective/publication/first-seen/retrieval/ingestion/validity/supersession clocks;
- first-release and revision vintages;
- immutable fact identities and version chains;
- parent lineage for derived facts;
- claim-to-fact and claim-to-root-cause lineage;
- same-root dependency detection;
- decision materiality and marginal flip potential;
- missing-data semantics;
- historical cutoff replay;
- six-market fact-family coverage declarations;
- Decision Evidence Packs and Fact Coverage Receipts;
- executable validation and preflight.

## Explicitly not in scope

D1 does not create new forecasting power simply by naming more data. It does not estimate hidden dealer books, infer unknown hedge-fund flow, reverse engineer proprietary feeds, or assign live authority to D2 domains. It does not optimize a strategy, choose position size, change Donchian logic, or assert profitability.

## Acceptance principle

A record can be *valid evidence* while still being *insufficient for a decision*. Conversely, incomplete context is not automatically fatal. D1 distinguishes record validity from decision sufficiency. The latter depends on materiality and whether the uncertainty can plausibly alter the current decision.
