# Market-Making, Dealer Economics, Inventory and Capital Constraints

## Economic function

Market makers provide immediacy by absorbing temporary order imbalance, financing inventory and connecting venues. Their willingness to intermediate depends on expected spread revenue, volatility, adverse selection, capital, funding, balance sheet and hedging cost.

## State vector

Bid-ask spread, depth, inventory, turnover, volatility, funding, haircuts, capital charges, netting, concentration, settlement risk, hedge liquidity and customer flow. Public data rarely reveal exact dealer inventory, so distinguish observation from inference.

## Inventory model

A dealer with unwanted inventory adjusts quote, spread, size and hedge. In competitive normal markets, inventory mean-reverts through customer flow and interdealer trading. In stress, dealers may reduce capacity together, causing discontinuous price impact.

## Adverse selection

Spread compensates for informed flow, inventory and operating cost. News or opacity raises adverse-selection risk and reduces displayed liquidity. A wide spread may reflect information risk rather than pure funding stress.

## Capital and balance sheet

Leverage exposure, risk-weighted assets, supplementary leverage, liquidity rules and internal limits affect intermediation. Quarter-end and stress can alter capacity independent of fundamental value.

## Cross-market hedging

Dealers may hedge cash inventory with futures, swaps, options, ETFs or correlated securities. Basis movements reveal hedge and financing conditions but can also reflect delivery or collateral optionality.

## Analysis contract

Use observable spread, depth, basis, fails, funding, volume and venue data. Treat dealer-position estimates as models with uncertainty. Explain how intermediation constraints affect the target asset's transmission and price discovery.
