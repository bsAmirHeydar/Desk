# Securities Lending, Short Interest and Borrow Markets

## Market function

Securities lending transfers temporary ownership against collateral to enable short selling, settlement, market making and financing. Borrow conditions contain information about scarcity and positioning but are not a direct valuation signal.

## Key variables

Loan balance, utilization, lendable supply, borrow fee, rebate, collateral, term, recall, concentration, short interest, days to cover and settlement fails. Definitions differ across data providers.

## Supply

Pensions, insurers, index funds, mutual funds and custodians supply securities subject to policy, recall and collateral constraints. Passive ownership can increase lendable supply but concentration and corporate actions can reduce availability.

## Demand

Demand arises from directional shorts, relative-value trades, options hedging, convertible arbitrage, ETF creation/redemption and settlement. High borrow fee can reflect small float rather than broad bearish conviction.

## Squeeze mechanics

Positive news, recall, rising fee, option hedging or financing withdrawal can force covering. Squeeze probability depends on position concentration, liquidity and catalyst, not short interest alone.

## Analysis contract

Separate observed short interest from current borrow demand; identify data lag; examine fee, utilization, float, ownership, liquidity, options and catalysts. Never infer exact hedge-fund positions without evidence.
