# Volatility Supply Ecosystem, Insurance and Structured Products

## Structural participants

Volatility supply and demand arise from option market makers, hedge funds, pensions, insurers, banks, retail products, autocallables, variable annuities, target-volatility funds and corporate hedgers. The same option surface can reflect different underlying mandates.

## Insurance-linked demand

Variable annuity and guaranteed-product hedging can create long-dated equity, rate and volatility demand. Insurers dynamically hedge guarantees subject to accounting, capital, lapse and policyholder behavior.

## Structured-product supply

Autocallables, yield-enhancement notes and barrier products embed path-dependent option exposures. Issuer hedging may create local gamma, vega and correlation flows that change with spot and time.

## Volatility risk premium

Implied volatility includes expected variance, risk aversion, crash insurance demand, dealer balance-sheet cost and supply. Compare implied with a physical distribution using consistent horizon and jump assumptions.

## Regime transitions

Short-volatility positions can appear stable until leverage, margin or barrier effects create convex deleveraging. Analyze concentration, collateral and liquidity rather than extrapolating calm realized volatility.

## Analysis contract

Identify product ecosystem, maturity, strike/barrier distribution where observable, dealer sign uncertainty, vega/gamma/charm exposure, funding and scenario feedback. Avoid asserting dealer positioning from open interest alone.
