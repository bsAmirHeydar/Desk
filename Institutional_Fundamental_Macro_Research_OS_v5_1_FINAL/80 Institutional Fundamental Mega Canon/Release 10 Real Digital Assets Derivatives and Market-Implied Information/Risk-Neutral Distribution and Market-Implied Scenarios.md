# Risk-Neutral Distribution and Market-Implied Scenarios

> [!abstract] Analytical purpose
> Option prices can infer a risk-neutral distribution, which differs from a physical forecast because it embeds risk aversion and hedging demand.

## Competing models

- A demand-led interpretation in which state-price density is the dominant state variable.
- A supply, balance-sheet or institutional interpretation in which risk-neutral moments constrains adjustment.
- A market-pricing interpretation in which the observed move primarily reflects prior expectations, positioning, liquidity or risk premium rather than a change in underlying state.
- A structural-break interpretation in which historical elasticities no longer transport because policy, technology, regulation or market structure has changed.

A final synthesis may assign different weights to the models, but it must not silently merge mutually inconsistent assumptions.

## Regime dependence

- **Risk-Neutral Distribution and Market-Implied Scenarios in expansion with benign funding and anchored expectations:** emphasize persistence, capacity and valuation rather than crisis channels.
- **Risk-Neutral Distribution and Market-Implied Scenarios in a late-cycle or inflation-constrained regime:** emphasize policy reaction, financing cost, margin pressure and nonlinear sensitivity.
- **Risk-Neutral Distribution and Market-Implied Scenarios in contraction or deleveraging:** emphasize liquidity, balance sheets, default, forced adjustment and policy backstops.
- **Risk-Neutral Distribution and Market-Implied Scenarios under a supply or geopolitical shock:** separate physical loss and substitution from nominal repricing and policy response.
- **Risk-Neutral Distribution and Market-Implied Scenarios during financial stress:** prioritize funding, collateral, market depth and institutional capacity over average historical relationships.
