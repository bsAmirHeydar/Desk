# Nuclear Fuel Cycle, Uranium, Conversion, Enrichment and Utility Procurement

## Fuel-chain structure

The nuclear fuel cycle includes mining and milling, conversion, enrichment, fuel fabrication, reactor loading, spent-fuel management and secondary supply. Bottlenecks in one stage cannot always be offset by abundance in another.

## Uranium supply

Assess mine production, jurisdiction, grade, cost, development lead time, permitting, inventories, by-product output and restart risk. Spot supply is a small part of a market dominated by long-term contracts and utility security-of-supply preferences.

## Conversion and enrichment

Conversion capacity and separative work units can become binding constraints. Enrichment economics depend on feed-versus-SWU substitution and tails assay. Trade restrictions or geopolitical concentration can change procurement strategy and inventory demand.

## Demand

Model reactor fleet, capacity factors, new builds, retirements, restarts, uprates and initial core loads. Utility contracting schedules, uncovered requirements and inventory policies affect timing more than annual consumption alone.

## Secondary supplies

Government inventories, re-enrichment, underfeeding/overfeeding, recycled materials and producer inventories can alter primary demand. Classify supply by availability, policy and economic incentive.

## Price formation

Separate spot uranium, term contracts, conversion and enrichment. Financial vehicles can change visible inventory and spot liquidity but do not replace utility procurement analysis.

## Current analysis

Map each stage, contracting coverage, sanctions/trade constraints, producer disruptions, utility tenders and inventory. State where the actual bottleneck lies.
