# Electricity Market Design, LMP, Capacity and Ancillary Services

## Physical constraint

Electricity is produced and consumed nearly simultaneously. Storage is limited relative to system scale, flows follow network physics and transmission constraints create location-specific scarcity. A national fuel narrative is insufficient without grid topology and market rules.

## Market layers

- day-ahead and real-time energy;
- locational marginal prices decomposed into energy, congestion and losses;
- capacity or resource-adequacy mechanisms;
- ancillary services such as regulation, reserves and frequency response;
- bilateral contracts, hedges and retail tariffs;
- uplift and out-of-market reliability actions.

## State vector

Load, weather, generation availability, fuel prices, hydro conditions, renewable output, storage, outages, transmission constraints, interconnection, reserve margin and demand response. Net load and ramping often matter more than gross demand.

## LMP interpretation

LMP measures the marginal cost of serving an additional unit at a node subject to system constraints. Large price differences may reflect congestion rather than system-wide energy scarcity. Negative prices can arise from inflexible generation, subsidies or transmission limitations.

## Capacity economics

Evaluate accreditation, performance obligations, scarcity pricing, entry costs, retirement, queue delays and reliability rules. Capacity prices can diverge from energy-market profitability.

## Fuel and grid coupling

Natural-gas pipeline constraints, coal or nuclear outages, hydro availability and renewable intermittency connect power to other physical markets. Data-center and industrial loads alter regional demand and transmission needs.

## Current analysis

Define ISO/RTO or balancing area, node/hub, product and settlement. Use weather, load forecast, outages, fuel basis, transmission constraints, day-ahead/real-time spreads and reserve conditions. Do not generalize one region to another.
