# Shipping, Freight, Charter Rates and Chokepoint Economics

## Segmentation

Dry bulk, crude tankers, product tankers, LNG/LPG carriers, containers and specialized vessels have different fleets, contracts and demand. A single “shipping rate” is not a coherent market object.

## Demand in ton-miles

Freight demand depends on cargo volume multiplied by distance. Trade diversion can raise ton-mile demand even when global volume falls. Ballast legs, port congestion and speed alter effective capacity.

## Supply

Fleet size, orderbook, scrapping, shipyard capacity, delivery schedule, regulations, speed, downtime and congestion determine available vessel-days. New supply has long lags.

## Contract forms

Distinguish spot voyage, time charter, contracts of affreightment and freight derivatives. Earnings depend on bunker fuel, port cost, canal fees, waiting time and utilization.

## Chokepoints and insurance

Canal restrictions, war-risk premiums, sanctions, flag and classification rules can reroute vessels or reduce availability. Insurance cost and legal compliance can be binding even when ships physically exist.

## Analysis contract

Specify vessel class, route, cargo, rate unit and contract period. Use fleet/orderbook data, port calls, AIS with coverage caveats, congestion, bunker prices, canal status and cargo balances.
