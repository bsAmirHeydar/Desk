# Agricultural Balance Sheets, Basis, Storage and Weather Risk

## Balance-sheet identity

Beginning stocks + production + imports = domestic use + exports + ending stocks. Analyze each component by crop year, geography and quality. Stocks-to-use is informative but not sufficient without location, ownership and deliverability.

## Production

Area planted, harvested area and yield respond to price, weather, seed technology, input costs, crop rotation and policy. Separate planting risk, growing-season risk and harvest risk.

## Demand

Food, feed, biofuel and exports have different elasticities. Livestock cycles, energy policy, currency, income and substitution change demand. Export commitments and actual shipments must be distinguished.

## Basis and storage

Local cash basis reflects transport, elevator capacity, quality, regional surplus/deficit and delivery economics. Futures spreads encode storage incentive but are constrained by financing and physical capacity.

## Weather

Use crop-stage-specific exposure rather than generic drought headlines. Temperature, precipitation, soil moisture and storm timing have nonlinear effects. Forecast uncertainty should be explicit.

## Policy and geopolitics

Export restrictions, tariffs, subsidies, sanctions, biofuel mandates and strategic reserves can dominate normal elasticity. Shipping routes and port capacity link agricultural balances to freight.

## Current analysis

Define commodity, grade, crop year, location and contract. Use USDA WASDE and crop reports, national agencies, export sales, weather and cash/futures basis. Label unofficial production estimates.
