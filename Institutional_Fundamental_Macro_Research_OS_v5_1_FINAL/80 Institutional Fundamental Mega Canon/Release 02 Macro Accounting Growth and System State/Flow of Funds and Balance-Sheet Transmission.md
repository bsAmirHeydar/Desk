# Flow of Funds and Balance-Sheet Transmission

> [!abstract] Analytical purpose
> Flow-of-funds analysis connects transactions to stocks and exposes financing channels hidden by GDP. It is essential for distinguishing spending financed by current income from spending financed by asset sales, borrowing or balance-sheet expansion.

## Competing models

- A demand-led interpretation in which transaction accounts is the dominant state variable.
- A supply, balance-sheet or institutional interpretation in which revaluation effects constrains adjustment.
- A market-pricing interpretation in which the observed move primarily reflects prior expectations, positioning, liquidity or risk premium rather than a change in underlying state.
- A structural-break interpretation in which historical elasticities no longer transport because policy, technology, regulation or market structure has changed.

A final synthesis may assign different weights to the models, but it must not silently merge mutually inconsistent assumptions.

## Regime dependence

- **Flow of Funds and Balance-Sheet Transmission in expansion with benign funding and anchored expectations:** emphasize persistence, capacity and valuation rather than crisis channels.
- **Flow of Funds and Balance-Sheet Transmission in a late-cycle or inflation-constrained regime:** emphasize policy reaction, financing cost, margin pressure and nonlinear sensitivity.
- **Flow of Funds and Balance-Sheet Transmission in contraction or deleveraging:** emphasize liquidity, balance sheets, default, forced adjustment and policy backstops.
- **Flow of Funds and Balance-Sheet Transmission under a supply or geopolitical shock:** separate physical loss and substitution from nominal repricing and policy response.
- **Flow of Funds and Balance-Sheet Transmission during financial stress:** prioritize funding, collateral, market depth and institutional capacity over average historical relationships.
