# Finance Companies, Specialty Lenders and Nonbank Credit Origination

## Scope

Nonbank lenders include consumer-finance companies, mortgage originators, equipment and auto lenders, marketplace lenders, business-development companies and specialty commercial lenders. They may avoid deposit regulation but depend more heavily on wholesale funding and capital markets.

## Fundamental state vector

- origination volume and channel quality;
- borrower mix and underwriting standards;
- gain-on-sale versus retained-credit economics;
- warehouse lines, securitization and committed facilities;
- asset duration and funding maturity;
- delinquency, roll rates, default and recovery;
- servicing advances and operational capacity;
- regulatory and legal exposure;
- capital, liquidity and covenant headroom.

## Originate-to-distribute risk

Revenue can rise while future credit quality deteriorates. Analyze representations and warranties, repurchase obligations, first-loss retention, residual interests and dependence on securitization markets.

## Funding fragility

Warehouse lenders can reprice, reduce advance rates or demand collateral. If securitization markets close, originations fall and retained assets rise. Liquidity stress can precede recognized credit loss.

## Analysis contract

Link borrower performance to funding and capital-market access. Distinguish credit cycle, funding cycle, accounting gain recognition and regulatory intervention.
