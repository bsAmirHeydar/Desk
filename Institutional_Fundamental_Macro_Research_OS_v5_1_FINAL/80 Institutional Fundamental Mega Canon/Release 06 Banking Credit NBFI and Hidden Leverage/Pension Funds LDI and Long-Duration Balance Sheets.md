# Pension Funds, LDI and Long-Duration Balance Sheets

## Economic object

Defined-benefit pensions are leveraged economic balance sheets even when accounting presentation obscures the leverage. Assets fund long-duration, rate-sensitive and inflation-sensitive liabilities under regulatory and sponsor constraints.

## Liability measurement

Funded status depends on discount-rate convention, inflation, wage growth, longevity, benefit indexation and member demographics. Rising discount rates can improve accounting funded status while causing large losses or collateral needs on hedges.

## Asset allocation

Map public equity, credit, government bonds, private assets, real estate, derivatives, cash and currency hedges. Illiquid valuations may lag public markets and create denominator effects.

## LDI mechanics

Liability-driven investment uses duration exposure, often through derivatives or repo, to hedge liability-rate sensitivity. Leverage creates collateral calls when yields move sharply. Forced asset sales can transmit pension stress into government bonds, credit and currencies.

## Sponsor and regulatory feedback

Funding contributions affect corporate or public budgets. Contribution holidays, benefit changes, guarantee systems and political constraints alter the economic risk. Public pensions may use optimistic return assumptions and face tax-base constraints.

## Current analysis

Assess funded status under consistent economic assumptions, hedge ratio, leverage, collateral waterfall, liquid asset buffer, private-asset marks, contribution capacity and concentration. Separate solvency from short-term liquidity.
