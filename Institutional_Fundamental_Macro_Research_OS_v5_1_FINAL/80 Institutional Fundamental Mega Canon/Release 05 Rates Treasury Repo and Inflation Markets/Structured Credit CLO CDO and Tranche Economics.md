# Structured Credit, CLO, CDO and Tranche Economics

## Core principle

Structured credit reallocates the timing and distribution of losses through subordination, coverage tests and payment waterfalls. Analysis must model the structure and collateral jointly; tranche labels are not sufficient.

## CLO collateral

For leveraged-loan CLOs assess obligor and industry concentration, ratings, spread, maturity wall, covenant quality, recoveries, loan prices, defaults, CCC buckets and manager trading. Manager behavior and reinvestment flexibility can materially alter outcomes.

## Waterfalls and tests

Document interest and principal priority, overcollateralization tests, interest-diversion tests, collateral-quality tests, reinvestment period, non-call period, maturity, manager fees and optional redemption. Test how collateral migration redirects cash from equity and junior debt to senior tranches.

## Tranche risk

Senior tranches are exposed to correlation and tail clustering even when expected losses are small. Junior tranches depend on excess spread, financing, collateral marks, default timing and reinvestment opportunities. Equity returns are path-dependent and leveraged to manager decisions.

## CDO distinctions

Differentiate cash versus synthetic structures, corporate versus asset-backed collateral, static versus managed pools and resecuritizations. Synthetic structures introduce counterparty, basis and mark-to-market dynamics beyond cash credit losses.

## Scenario design

Vary default timing, recovery, correlation, downgrade migration, spread, prepayment, collateral trading and reinvestment. Historical average default assumptions are insufficient during underwriting-regime changes.

## Systemic transmission

Structured-credit stress can propagate through warehouse financing, bank commitments, fund redemptions, insurer holdings, margin and dealer inventory. Price declines may reflect liquidity and forced sales before realized collateral losses.

## Evidence

Use offering documents, trustee reports, collateral tapes where available, rating methodologies as secondary inputs, regulatory filings and loan-market data. Record model assumptions and structural ambiguities.
