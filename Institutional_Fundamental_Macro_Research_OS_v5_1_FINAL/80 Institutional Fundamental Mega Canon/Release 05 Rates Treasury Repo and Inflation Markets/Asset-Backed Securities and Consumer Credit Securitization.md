# Asset-Backed Securities and Consumer Credit Securitization

## Scope

ABS convert receivables such as auto loans, credit cards, student loans, equipment leases and unsecured consumer credit into tranched securities. Fundamental analysis begins with collateral performance, underwriting vintage, servicer behavior, structural protections and funding conditions.

## Collateral analysis

Measure borrower credit distribution, loan-to-value, term, coupon, seasoning, geographic and dealer concentration, collateral depreciation, payment rates, delinquencies, defaults, recoveries and prepayments. Vintage analysis is essential because underwriting standards and borrower mix change over time.

## Waterfall and enhancement

Map payment priority, subordination, overcollateralization, reserve accounts, excess spread, triggers, step-down tests, revolving periods and clean-up calls. The same collateral can create materially different risk across tranches.

## Consumer macro transmission

Employment, real income, inflation, interest burden, used-vehicle prices, housing wealth and access to refinancing affect collateral. Credit performance is nonlinear: early delinquencies, roll rates and recovery values often reveal stress before charge-offs.

## Servicer and counterparty risk

Assess servicing quality, advancing obligations, commingling, backup servicer, derivative counterparties and bank-account providers. Operational or liquidity failure can impair cash flow even when collateral losses remain manageable.

## Valuation

Model expected loss, timing, WAL, prepayment, spread, trigger migration and liquidity. Scenario tests should include unemployment, recovery-value, payment-rate and funding shocks. Ratings are inputs, not substitutes for waterfall analysis.

## Source routes

Use SEC filings and prospectuses, trustee reports, servicer data, Federal Reserve consumer-credit series and official delinquency statistics. Distinguish issuer-reported collateral data from modeled projections.
