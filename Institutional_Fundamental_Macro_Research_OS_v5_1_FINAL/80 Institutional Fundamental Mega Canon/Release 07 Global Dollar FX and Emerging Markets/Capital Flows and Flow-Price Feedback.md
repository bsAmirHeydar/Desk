# Capital Flows and Flow-Price Feedback

> [!abstract] Analytical purpose
> Capital flows respond to return differentials, risk, benchmarks, hedging and policy. Flows also move prices and can become self-reinforcing.

## Competing models

- A demand-led interpretation in which portfolio flows is the dominant state variable.
- A supply, balance-sheet or institutional interpretation in which direct investment constrains adjustment.
- A market-pricing interpretation in which the observed move primarily reflects prior expectations, positioning, liquidity or risk premium rather than a change in underlying state.
- A structural-break interpretation in which historical elasticities no longer transport because policy, technology, regulation or market structure has changed.

A final synthesis may assign different weights to the models, but it must not silently merge mutually inconsistent assumptions.

## Regime dependence

- **Capital Flows and Flow-Price Feedback in expansion with benign funding and anchored expectations:** emphasize persistence, capacity and valuation rather than crisis channels.
- **Capital Flows and Flow-Price Feedback in a late-cycle or inflation-constrained regime:** emphasize policy reaction, financing cost, margin pressure and nonlinear sensitivity.
- **Capital Flows and Flow-Price Feedback in contraction or deleveraging:** emphasize liquidity, balance sheets, default, forced adjustment and policy backstops.
- **Capital Flows and Flow-Price Feedback under a supply or geopolitical shock:** separate physical loss and substitution from nominal repricing and policy response.
- **Capital Flows and Flow-Price Feedback during financial stress:** prioritize funding, collateral, market depth and institutional capacity over average historical relationships.

## Failure modes and red-team checks

- For **Capital Flows and Flow-Price Feedback**, confusing a level with its rate of change or an annualized short-run rate with a year-over-year rate.
- Treating an estimate, proxy or model output as a directly observed fact.

- Double-counting the same underlying shock through correlated indicators.
