# Sovereign Wealth Funds, Reserve Managers and Official-Sector Flows

## Institutional taxonomy

- stabilization funds smooth commodity or fiscal revenue;
- savings funds transfer wealth across generations;
- reserve-investment corporations seek higher returns on excess reserves;
- development funds pursue domestic strategic objectives;
- central-bank reserve managers prioritize liquidity, safety and intervention capacity.

## Mandates and constraints

Analyze legal ownership, funding source, withdrawal rules, benchmark, currency, liquidity, governance, transparency, domestic-investment restrictions and political objectives. Official flows may not maximize short-run return.

## Balance-sheet transmission

Commodity revenue, fiscal transfers, intervention, reserve accumulation and currency hedging connect official institutions to sovereign bonds, FX, equities and real assets. Sterilization and government deposits affect domestic liquidity.

## Asset allocation

Distinguish strategic allocation from tactical flow. Changes can arise from valuation, rebalancing, fiscal withdrawals or mandate change. Public disclosures are often lagged and incomplete.

## Reserve adequacy

Reserve managers consider import coverage, short-term external debt, banking liabilities, exchange-rate regime and stress needs. Higher reserves do not automatically imply greater risk-taking capacity.

## Analysis contract

Identify the institution, mandate, funding flow, disclosure lag and feasible instruments. Treat reported holdings, custody data and market flows as partial evidence. Avoid attributing market moves to official buyers without confirmation.
