# Inflation Decomposition and Price-Setting Channels

> [!abstract] Analytical purpose
> Price changes should be decomposed into commodities, margins, wages, rents, taxes, exchange rates, supply constraints and demand. The same headline rate can imply very different persistence.

## Competing models

- A demand-led interpretation in which goods, services, food and energy is the dominant state variable.
- A supply, balance-sheet or institutional interpretation in which input cost and margin pass-through constrains adjustment.
- A market-pricing interpretation in which the observed move primarily reflects prior expectations, positioning, liquidity or risk premium rather than a change in underlying state.
- A structural-break interpretation in which historical elasticities no longer transport because policy, technology, regulation or market structure has changed.

A final synthesis may assign different weights to the models, but it must not silently merge mutually inconsistent assumptions.

## Regime dependence

- **Inflation Decomposition and Price-Setting Channels in expansion with benign funding and anchored expectations:** emphasize persistence, capacity and valuation rather than crisis channels.
- **Inflation Decomposition and Price-Setting Channels in a late-cycle or inflation-constrained regime:** emphasize policy reaction, financing cost, margin pressure and nonlinear sensitivity.
- **Inflation Decomposition and Price-Setting Channels in contraction or deleveraging:** emphasize liquidity, balance sheets, default, forced adjustment and policy backstops.
- **Inflation Decomposition and Price-Setting Channels under a supply or geopolitical shock:** separate physical loss and substitution from nominal repricing and policy response.
- **Inflation Decomposition and Price-Setting Channels during financial stress:** prioritize funding, collateral, market depth and institutional capacity over average historical relationships.

- For **Inflation Decomposition and Price-Setting Channels**, confusing a level with its rate of change or an annualized short-run rate with a year-over-year rate.
