## Reflexivity

Prices do not only reflect fundamentals; they can alter fundamentals.

Examples:

- rising equities lower the cost of capital and support spending;
- falling bond yields reduce mortgage and corporate borrowing costs;
- a stronger currency lowers imported inflation but hurts exporters;
- a weaker currency raises local inflation and can force policy tightening;
- wider credit spreads restrict lending and weaken future growth;
- higher oil prices transfer income and change inflation expectations.

## Positive Feedback

A move amplifies itself.

### Volatility–deleveraging loop

> Volatility rises → risk models cut exposure → selling increases → volatility rises further.

### Collateral loop

> Asset prices fall → collateral value declines → margin calls → forced selling → further decline.

### Trend and systematic flow

> Price trend strengthens → systematic strategies increase exposure → trend strengthens.

### Currency balance-sheet loop

> Dollar strengthens → dollar debt burden rises → risk assets weaken → demand for dollars rises.

## Negative Feedback

A move creates forces that oppose it.

- higher yields slow demand and eventually reduce inflation;
- higher oil prices encourage supply and reduce demand;
- currency appreciation reduces imported inflation and future policy pressure;
- extreme positioning increases squeeze risk;
- valuation compression increases expected return.

## Nonlinearity

Market response is often small until a threshold is crossed.

Thresholds include:

- margin and collateral levels;
- option strikes and dealer gamma;
- policy credibility limits;
- bank capital constraints;
- volatility-control triggers;
- reserve scarcity;
- commodity storage capacity;
- implementation levels where execution flow concentrates.

## State-Dependent Policy Transmission

The same policy surprise can have different effects when:

- leverage is high vs low;
- banks are capital-constrained vs healthy;
- inflation is anchored vs unanchored;
- growth is strong vs weak;
- markets are liquid vs stressed;
- positioning is neutral vs crowded.

## Day-Trading Relevance

Reflexive moves often show:

- accelerating cross-asset coherence;
- poor temporary counter-move depth;
- repeated failure of countertrend attempts;
- expanding volatility;
- widening breadth;
- rising volume around continuation;
- and stronger response to small additional information.

Negative-feedback environments show:

- overshoot and reversal;
- cross-asset disagreement;
- failure to hold event extremes;
- and compression after the first impulse.

## Practical Question

> Is the current move generating balance-sheet and flow consequences that make continuation more likely, or is it creating valuation and positioning forces that make reversal more likely?

This question links fundamentals to intraday path without pretending price patterns alone explain the move.
