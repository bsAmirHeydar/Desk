## Why Transmission Matters

A fundamental fact matters only through a channel. The channel identifies:

- the first market that should move;
- the lagged markets;
- the expected sign;
- the horizon;
- the conditions for amplification;
- and the evidence that would reject the thesis.

## Core Monetary Transmission

```mermaid
flowchart LR
    A[Policy expectation] --> B[Front-end rates]
    B --> C[Yield curve]
    C --> D[Funding and discount rates]
    D --> E[Credit, housing, capex]
    E --> F[Demand and labor]
    F --> G[Inflation]
```

Market prices can move at every node before the real economy responds.

## Equity Transmission

### Growth impulse

> Better activity → higher revenue/earnings expectations → tighter credit spreads → stronger equities.

### Discount-rate impulse

> Higher inflation/policy path → higher real yields → lower valuation multiples → long-duration equities weaker.

### Liquidity impulse

> Easier funding/lower volatility → greater risk capacity → lower risk premium → equities stronger.

### Fiscal impulse

> Government spending/tax support → stronger nominal demand and earnings → potentially higher yields and mixed equity effects.

## FX Transmission

> Relative growth/inflation → relative policy path → relative expected return → capital flow and currency repricing.

But FX can also be driven by:

- global risk appetite;
- funding demand;
- terms of trade;
- intervention;
- external debt;
- reserve flows;
- hedging costs.

## Gold Transmission

Common chains include:

- lower real yields → lower opportunity cost → gold stronger;
- weaker dollar → higher dollar gold price;
- fiscal or geopolitical uncertainty → risk/credibility demand;
- liquidation stress → gold may fall initially as cash is raised;
- central-bank or physical demand → medium-term support independent of daily rates.

## Oil Transmission

> Demand growth + supply discipline − inventory accumulation → physical tightness → backwardation/convenience yield → higher spot and front contracts.

Financial channels can interrupt:

- dollar strength;
- recession fears;
- deleveraging;
- volatility shocks;
- geopolitical risk premium.

## Credit Transmission

> Tighter policy or weaker growth → lower debt-service capacity → wider spreads → tighter financial conditions → lower investment and risk appetite.

Credit is often the bridge between a benign rate rally and a dangerous growth scare.

## Transmission Lags

| Channel | Market response | Economic response |
|---|---:|---:|
| Policy surprise → front-end rates | seconds | — |
| Rates → FX/equity valuation | seconds–days | — |
| Financial conditions → demand | months | months |
| Labor weakening → wage pressure | months | months |
| Supply shock → inflation | days–quarters | days–quarters |
| Fiscal spending → activity | weeks–quarters | weeks–quarters |

## Chain Validation

For every thesis, specify:

1. shock;
2. expectation change;
3. first market;
4. second market;
5. real-economy consequence;
6. expected persistence;
7. contradictory observation.

Example:

> Softer core inflation should lower the expected policy path. The two-year yield should fall first. Real yields and the dollar should soften. Nasdaq should benefit if credit remains stable. If yields reverse higher or credit widens sharply, the equity-long interpretation loses confidence.
