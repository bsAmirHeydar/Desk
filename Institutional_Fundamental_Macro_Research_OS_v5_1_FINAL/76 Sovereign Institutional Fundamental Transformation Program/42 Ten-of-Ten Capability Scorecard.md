# 42 Ten-of-Ten Capability Scorecard

## Scoring doctrine

A score of 10 is not awarded for breadth, prose, naming or intent. It requires production evidence.

| Capability | 10/10 evidence |
|---|---|
| Knowledge architecture | Complete ontology, low duplication, reviewed monographs and retrieval tests |
| Evidence | Claim-level lineage, archived artifacts, precise locators and source-quality controls |
| Point-in-time data | Bitemporal warehouse, release calendar and leakage test suite |
| Models | Reproducible code, benchmarks, uncertainty, pseudo-real-time results and monitoring |
| Multihorizon reasoning | Separate calibrated state objects and tested conflict rules |
| Asset engines | Point-in-time inputs, historical evaluation, live monitoring and documented failure modes |
| Decision engine | Incremental value over baselines after costs, calibrated confidence and no-deployment evaluation |
| Portfolio | Hidden-beta aggregation, scenario loss, liquidity stress and independent risk approval |
| Operations | Data SLAs, versioned deployments, incident response, rollback and retirement |
| Governance | Clear decision rights, independent validation, dissent records and accreditation |

## Score bands

- **0–2:** concept or outline only;
- **3–4:** documented method with incomplete evidence;
- **5–6:** implemented prototype with limited point-in-time testing;
- **7–8:** production-capable with broad validation and governance;
- **9:** mature, replicated and resilient across regimes;
- **10:** externally challenged, continuously monitored, independently reproducible and demonstrably valuable.

## Rule

No capability may inherit a score from another capability. A strong knowledge base does not imply strong data, and a strong model does not imply a safe portfolio process.
