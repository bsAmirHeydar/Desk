# Narrative Saturation Consensus and Crowding

## Decision purpose

Distinguish a widely known narrative from a crowded position and from a completed economic thesis. Narrative saturation measures attention and consensus; crowding measures exposure and exit vulnerability.

## Governing distinctions

- Awareness, consensus and positioning are separate.
- A saturated narrative can remain fundamentally correct.
- Crowding can create asymmetric reversal even when the state is persistent.
- A niche but fully priced thesis is possible.

## Operating method

1. Map narrative prevalence across contemporaneous high-quality sources.
2. Measure forecast dispersion and scenario concentration where possible.
3. Use positioning evidence separately.
4. Assess whether valuation/payoff embeds the dominant story.
5. Identify what evidence would still surprise the consensus.

## Required outputs

- `narrative_prevalence`
- `consensus_concentration`
- `positioning_crowding`
- `valuation_embedding`
- `contrarian_surprise_map`

## Failure modes and controls

- **Failure:** Counting article volume as position size  
  **Control:** Keep attention and exposure separate.
- **Failure:** Consensus narrative automatically faded  
  **Control:** Test state persistence and unresolved pricing gap.
- **Failure:** Current content used in historical replay  
  **Control:** Use archived contemporaneous evidence only.
