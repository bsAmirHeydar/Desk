# Gold Force Consumption and Persistence Book

## Decision purpose

Translate V11 into gold’s multiple regimes: real-rate opportunity cost, dollar, reserve diversification, official-sector purchases, ETF/futures positioning, physical-market demand, fiscal/monetary credibility, geopolitical insurance and liquidity liquidation. The book prevents the label “safe haven” from obscuring opposite mechanisms.

## Governing distinctions

- Gold can rise on lower real yields, reserve demand, credibility deterioration or geopolitical insurance; these mechanisms have different half-lives.
- A crisis can initially support gold but later force liquidation through dollar/funding stress.
- Official-sector and physical demand are slow processes; headline fear and futures covering are faster and more consumable.
- DXY is not an independent confirmation of EURUSD and must not be double-counted.
- Futures positioning is not identical to physical balance or central-bank demand.

## Operating method

1. Freeze real yields, dollar, policy path, ETF/futures proxies, physical indicators, official-sector evidence and funding conditions.
2. Classify the dominant gold regime and list viable rival regimes.
3. Map the catalyst through opportunity cost, currency, insurance demand, reserve policy, physical balance and liquidity channels.
4. Test whether price response is confirmed by the causal leader rather than by mechanically related instruments.
5. Estimate consumption by channel; a headline impulse may be exhausted while official-sector pressure remains.
6. Estimate separate persistence for macro rates, central-bank demand, physical balance, geopolitical risk and speculative flows.
7. Publish the liquidation hazard and the conditions that reopen or invalidate the thesis.

## Failure modes and controls

- **Failure:** Calling every rally safe-haven demand  
  **Control:** Require mechanism-specific evidence.
- **Failure:** Ignoring dollar funding liquidation  
  **Control:** Track funding and broad-dollar stress.
- **Failure:** Using monthly official data as intraday proof  
  **Control:** Respect frequency and publication lag.
- **Failure:** Treating futures positioning as physical demand  
  **Control:** Keep ledgers separate.
- **Failure:** Mechanically applying inverse real-yield beta  
  **Control:** Condition on regime and rival channels.

## Gold channel half-life priors

- Headline fear: fast recognition, often high reversal hazard.
- Policy-path/real-yield repricing: event-to-multi-day, regime dependent.
- ETF allocation: multi-session and observable with lag.
- Official-sector accumulation: slow, persistent, low-frequency evidence.
- Physical tightness/premiums: regional and seasonal, requiring careful source treatment.
- Fiscal/monetary credibility: structural but vulnerable to countervailing real-rate changes.
- Funding liquidation: fast and path-destructive even when the slow thesis remains bullish.

Consumption must therefore be channel-specific. A single gold-consumption score is only a communication summary over a disclosed vector.
