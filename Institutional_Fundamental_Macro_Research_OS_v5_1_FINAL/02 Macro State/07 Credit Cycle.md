## What It Is

The credit cycle is the expansion and contraction of borrowing capacity, underwriting standards, leverage, debt service, refinancing access, and default risk. It is often the mechanism that turns a slowdown into a recession or separates a soft landing from a hard landing.

## Why Markets Care

The market does not trade the variable in isolation. It trades how the variable changes expected policy, cash flows, default risk, risk premia, and relative returns.

## Driver Map

- Policy rates and benchmark yields.
- Borrower income and cash flow.
- Collateral values.
- Bank capital, deposits, and risk appetite.
- Corporate leverage and maturity walls.
- Default and recovery expectations.
- Private-credit conditions.
- Regulation and lending standards.
- Market access and investor demand.

## Indicator Hierarchy

### Leading and High-Frequency

- Credit-spread momentum.
- Bank lending standards and loan demand.
- Distressed ratios and downgrade breadth.
- Refinancing calendar and maturity walls.
- Equity performance of banks and lower-quality borrowers.

### Coincident

- Loan growth and lending rates.
- High-yield and investment-grade spreads.
- Commercial paper and securitization conditions.
- Delinquencies and early-stage arrears.

### Lagging or Confirming

- Defaults, bankruptcies, charge-offs, and unemployment.
- Realized recoveries.

### Market-Implied or Financial

- CDS indices.
- HYG/LQD relative behavior or equivalent spread proxies.
- Bank equities and preferreds.
- Yield-curve and funding spreads.

## Interpretation Framework

For every update, separate:

1. **Level** — where the variable stands.
2. **Momentum** — whether it is improving or deteriorating.
3. **Breadth** — how widely the change is distributed.
4. **Quality** — whether the composition is durable.
5. **Revision risk** — how much history may change.
6. **Expectation gap** — what was already priced.
7. **Policy relevance** — whether the reaction function changes.
8. **Horizon** — when the impact should appear.

## Transmission to Markets

- Benign credit allows lower yields to support equities.
- Widening spreads increase discount rates and signal cash-flow risk.
- Credit deterioration tightens conditions independently of policy.
- Bank stress can weaken the currency or strengthen it through safe-haven effects depending on jurisdiction.
- Commodity producers and small caps are more sensitive to refinancing conditions.

## Day-Trading Translation

Credit is the key discriminator when yields fall. If yields fall and credit remains stable, policy relief can support equities. If yields fall because growth or solvency risk is rising and spreads widen, an equity rally is less trustworthy. Intraday, use liquid spread proxies carefully; they are not perfect but provide causal context.

The daily objective is not to forecast the next data print from scratch. It is to know which outcome would force the largest repricing and which cross-asset market should confirm first.

## Short-Swing Translation

For swings, monitor spread trend, downgrade/default cycle, lending standards, refinancing pressure, and bank behavior. A macro slowdown with stable credit is different from one with a self-reinforcing tightening loop.

A swing thesis should survive normal intraday noise. It needs a multi-session pricing gap, a catalyst sequence, and a clearly separate overnight invalidation.

## Common Traps

- Waiting for defaults, which are lagging.
- Using one ETF price without duration adjustment.
- Ignoring private credit and bank channels.
- Assuming all spread widening predicts immediate recession.
- Confusing Treasury-yield decline with total financing-cost decline.
- Ignoring sector concentration.
- Treating bank equity weakness as purely idiosyncratic.

## Diagnostic Questions

- Are spreads widening because of rates, growth, liquidity, or specific sectors?
- Is lending availability deteriorating?
- Are lower-quality borrowers underperforming?
- Is refinancing becoming uneconomic?
- Do bank equities confirm stress?
- Does policy relief improve spreads or fail to transmit?
- Is credit contradicting the equity narrative?
