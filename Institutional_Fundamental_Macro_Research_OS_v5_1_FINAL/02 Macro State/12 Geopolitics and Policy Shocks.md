## What It Is

Geopolitical analysis for trading is the translation of political events into probabilities of changes in physical supply, trade, sanctions, fiscal spending, monetary behavior, risk premia, and capital mobility. It is not prediction by dramatic headline.

## Why Markets Care

The market does not trade the variable in isolation. It trades how the variable changes expected policy, cash flows, default risk, risk premia, and relative returns.

## Driver Map

- Probability of escalation and de-escalation.
- Operational capacity and credible intent.
- Supply routes, chokepoints, and substitution.
- Sanctions design, enforcement, and exemptions.
- Alliance response and policy coordination.
- Fiscal and defense spending.
- Refugee, labor, and trade effects.
- Cyber, infrastructure, and payment-system disruption.
- Market positioning and existing risk premium.

## Indicator Hierarchy

### Leading and High-Frequency

- Official actions and verified operational changes.
- Shipping, insurance, freight, and physical premiums.
- Sanctions text and implementation dates.
- Military logistics and diplomatic schedules.
- Options skew and commodity curves.

### Coincident

- Actual supply disruptions.
- Trade rerouting.
- Capital controls and intervention.
- Fiscal announcements.

### Lagging or Confirming

- Growth, inflation, and investment effects.
- Supply-chain relocation.
- Political regime and institutional change.

### Market-Implied or Financial

- Oil/gas curves and physical differentials.
- Gold, dollar, safe-haven FX, and sovereign bonds.
- Defense and exposed-sector equities.
- Credit and volatility.

## Interpretation Framework

For every update, separate:

1. **Level** — where the variable stands.
2. **Momentum** — whether it is improving or deteriorating.
3. **Breadth** — how widely the change is distributed.
4. **Quality** — whether the composition is durable.
5. **Revision risk** — how much history may change.
6. **Expectation gap** — what was already priced.
7. **Policy relevance** — whether the reaction function changes.
8. **Horizon** — when the impact should appear.

## Transmission to Markets

- Supply disruption can raise commodities and inflation while lowering growth.
- Safe-haven demand can strengthen the dollar, gold, or sovereign bonds depending on funding conditions.
- Sanctions can alter trade routes and terms of trade.
- Defense spending can support specific sectors and fiscal demand.
- De-escalation removes risk premium even if structural fundamentals remain.

## Day-Trading Translation

For day trading, classify the headline: rhetoric, decision, implementation, physical effect, or escalation. Require confirmation from the market closest to the physical channel. A headline about oil supply should appear in crude spreads, shipping, or regional differentials—not only social media.

The daily objective is not to forecast the next data print from scratch. It is to know which outcome would force the largest repricing and which cross-asset market should confirm first.

## Short-Swing Translation

For swings, build a probability tree with timelines, supply impact, policy response, and decay of risk premium. Position size must reflect gap and weekend risk. Geopolitical trades have discontinuous outcomes and should not be managed as ordinary mean-reverting noise.

A swing thesis should survive normal intraday noise. It needs a multi-session pricing gap, a catalyst sequence, and a clearly separate overnight invalidation.

## Common Traps

- Trading unverified headlines.
- Confusing rhetoric with capability.
- Assuming every conflict is risk-off for all assets.
- Ignoring what is already priced.
- Using moral judgment as probability analysis.
- Failing to define de-escalation.
- Ignoring second-order policy and fiscal effects.
- Holding oversized positions through binary gaps.

## Diagnostic Questions

- What changed operationally?
- What is the probability and magnitude of supply or policy impact?
- Which asset is closest to the causal channel?
- How much risk premium was already present?
- What would de-escalation look like?
- What is the timeline?
- Can the position survive a discontinuous gap?
