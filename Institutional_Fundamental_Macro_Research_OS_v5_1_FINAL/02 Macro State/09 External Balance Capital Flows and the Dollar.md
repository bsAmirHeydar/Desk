## What It Is

External balance describes trade, income, transfers, cross-border assets and liabilities, and the financing of current-account positions. Currency prices reflect marginal demand for settlement, investment, hedging, reserves, and funding—not trade alone.

## Why Markets Care

The market does not trade the variable in isolation. It trades how the variable changes expected policy, cash flows, default risk, risk premia, and relative returns.

## Transmission to Markets

- Higher relative expected rates can support a currency.
- Stronger relative growth can attract capital, but may also widen imports.
- A stronger dollar tightens global financial conditions and external balance sheets.
- Commodity-price gains can support exporter currencies.
- Safe-haven and funding demand can overwhelm rate differentials in stress.
- Hedging costs can change the attractiveness of cross-border bonds.

## Day-Trading Translation

For daily FX or dollar-sensitive assets, focus on **relative** surprises. A strong US print matters differently if Europe is also repricing higher. Watch front-end differential changes, broad dollar behavior, risk assets, and funding stress. For gold and commodities, distinguish a dollar move caused by relative policy from one caused by global deleveraging.

The daily objective is not to forecast the next data print from scratch. It is to know which outcome would force the largest repricing and which cross-asset market should confirm first.

## Short-Swing Translation

For swings, map relative policy paths, growth surprise indices, terms of trade, current-account quality, external debt, and positioning. Avoid a one-variable carry model; FX often switches between rate, growth, risk, and funding regimes.

A swing thesis should survive normal intraday noise. It needs a multi-session pricing gap, a catalyst sequence, and a clearly separate overnight invalidation.

## Common Traps

- Assuming trade balance alone determines FX.
- Using current policy-rate differentials instead of expected paths.
- Ignoring hedging costs.
- Treating the dollar only as a US variable.
- Assuming risk-off always strengthens every safe-haven currency.
- Ignoring intervention risk.
- Using slow balance-of-payments data for precise timing.

- Is the move broad dollar or pair-specific?
- Are capital flows confirming?
- Is funding stress present?
- How are terms of trade changing?
- Is positioning crowded?
- Does the currency move tighten or ease domestic conditions?
