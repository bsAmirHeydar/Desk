# Knowledge Completion Waves, Dependencies and Release Plan

## Release logic

The stages are not software sprints. They are editorial and scholarly completion waves. A wave is released when its knowledge artifacts pass the Ten-of-Ten exit audit.

### Release K1 — Foundations
Stages 01–07 establish scope, epistemology, competing schools, mathematics and statistics.

### Release K2 — Macro core
Stages 08–17 complete accounting, growth, inflation, labor, fiscal and monetary systems.

### Release K3 — Financial architecture
Stages 18–26 complete rates, inflation-linked markets, sovereign plumbing, banks, credit, global dollar, FX and emerging markets.

### Release K4 — Asset and corporate canon
Stages 27–37 complete accounting, valuation, equities, fixed income, precious metals, energy, commodities, real estate, digital assets, derivatives and flows.

### Release K5 — System integration
Stages 38–43 complete cross-asset transmission, geopolitics, climate, structural forces, country books and historical regimes.

### Release K6 — Research and horizon synthesis
Stages 44–51 complete forecasting, econometrics, current and historical analysis, day and multi-day context, scenarios and portfolio drivers.

### Release K7 — Educational and scholarly completion
Stages 52–57 complete pedagogy, glossary, source canon, editorial integration, adversarial audit and maintenance.

## Dependency rule

A downstream subject may be drafted early, but it cannot be declared complete until its prerequisite concepts are complete and linked. For example, Gold cannot receive 10/10 until real rates, inflation compensation, dollar funding, central-bank reserves, physical supply and historical monetary regimes are complete.

## Review boards

Each release receives four knowledge-only reviews:

1. **Beginner review** — can a new learner follow the sequence?
2. **Specialist review** — are definitions, models and controversies technically correct?
3. **Cross-domain review** — are transmissions to other systems coherent?
4. **Adversarial review** — what is missing, overstated, duplicated or internally inconsistent?
