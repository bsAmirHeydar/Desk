# Institutional Fundamental Analysis Objective

> [!important] Governing objective
> The Vault exists to produce complete, defensible, multihorizon fundamental analysis at the standard expected from a large institutional macro and cross-asset research organization. Education, pedagogy, exercises and examinations are secondary quality-control tools; they are not the product objective.

## The product of the Vault

The product is not a course, textbook, curriculum or collection of definitions. The product is an **institutional analysis intelligence base** capable of supporting questions such as:

- What is the complete fundamental state of a market now?
- What was knowable at a historical cutoff, and what conclusion was justified then?
- Which structural, cyclical, tactical and event forces are active simultaneously?
- What is priced, what is not priced, and which assumption is vulnerable?
- Which transmission channel links the economic state to the asset payoff?
- Which rival model best explains the evidence, and what evidence would distinguish them?
- Which cross-asset markets confirm or contradict the conclusion?
- Which scenarios dominate, what are their probabilities and asymmetries, and what changes them?
- Where are the unknowns, data-quality limits, revisions and model risks?
- What is the conclusion for a market, company, country, commodity, event or portfolio at each horizon?

## Fundamental-only boundary

The Vault may study prices, yields, curves, spreads, implied volatility, basis, market depth, flows and positioning when they reveal expectations, valuation, constraints, risk premia, liquidity or transmission. It does not contain chart-pattern systems, indicator rules, price-bar methods or entry-trigger frameworks.

## Definition of institutional completeness

A domain is complete only when the Vault can retrieve and combine its knowledge into an analysis that contains:

1. object and scope definition;
2. state decomposition;
3. measurement and source architecture;
4. historical and institutional context;
5. competing models;
6. causal mechanisms;
7. expectations and market pricing;
8. valuation and payoff mapping;
9. cross-asset and cross-country transmission;
10. horizon-specific conclusions;
11. scenarios, tails and nonlinearities;
12. contradiction and unknown ledgers;
13. confidence, invalidation and expiry;
14. point-in-time integrity for historical work;
15. claim-level source lineage;
16. explicit residual gaps.

## Non-goals

- Maximizing note count or word count.
- Writing the same research template under different titles.
- Optimizing for beginner readability as the primary acceptance criterion.
- Treating a glossary, exercise set or examination as proof of analytical completeness.
- Claiming 10/10 because a topic is named or summarized.

## V17 evidence-truth requirement

Institutional completeness now also requires explicit epistemic class, source/vintage lineage and point-in-time visibility for every load-bearing claim. A conclusion is not considered defensible if it depends on future revisions, unlabeled proxies, inference presented as observation, or fabricated independence among same-root evidence.

## V18 D2 evidence-science objective

Every production run must now distinguish institutional position, actual transaction flow, funding/plumbing conditions, institutional mechanics and market capacity under D1 point-in-time evidence rules. In V18 these states are captured for research and forward learning only; they may not originate Fundamental Direction or change final permission until D3 integration is validated.

## V19 D3 causal-integration objective

Institutional completeness now requires that Positioning, Actual Flow, Funding/Plumbing, Mechanics and Capacity be translated through explicit causal channels, materiality and independent-root lineage before they can affect edge usability. These sciences may constrain or suppress transmission but may not originate or flip Fundamental Direction.
