# 01 Monograph Definition of Done

A monograph is a self-contained research reference, not a long checklist. It must teach the object from first principles, specify how the institution measures it, show how competing models differ, and provide enough detail for reproduction.

## Required architecture

1. **Concept and institutional relevance:** exact definition, scope and boundary.
2. **Accounting and mathematics:** identities, assumptions, derivations and variable table.
3. **Data:** source fields, units, frequency, vintages, breaks and licensing.
4. **Measurement:** transformations, seasonal adjustment, aggregation and uncertainty.
5. **Models:** simple benchmark, advanced model, estimation and diagnostics.
6. **Institutional mechanics:** legal, operational, market and balance-sheet constraints.
7. **Historical evidence:** point-in-time cases including failures.
8. **Cross-asset transmission:** causal leader, confirmations and rival mechanisms.
9. **Decision use:** horizons, scenarios, liquidity, cost, portfolio and expiry.
10. **Implementation:** code package, tests, monitoring and model card.
11. **Sources:** claim-level evidence and precise locators.
12. **Exercises:** replication, sensitivity and adversarial questions.

## Minimum depth

Length is not a quality metric, but a monograph that cannot define variables, show data, derive its main relations, explain estimation and reproduce a historical example is incomplete. Complex topics may require a hierarchy of chapters rather than one oversized note.

## Review

A subject expert, model reviewer, data reviewer and independent risk reviewer sign separate sections. Editorial approval cannot substitute for scientific or operational approval.
